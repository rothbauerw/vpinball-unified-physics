KICKER DESIGN  (all 7 sections approved by the user 2026-11-12; requirements: Requirements.txt section F)
==========================================================================================================
Closest existing models (ModelStandards 1b): static surface = a wall's hard-surface host (parts/surface.cpp ~235-270: RubberSurface faces -> HitRubber host, BarsTriangles probe);
the hole = the turntable's CarvedHole (rubbercontact.h ~803, playfield faces inside it do not press); the sensing = trigger None zones (physics/nonezones.h, parts/trigger.cpp
455-620: centre test on the chord, events queued and fired after the step); a held ball = the magnets' pair form with the other ball held (pairform.h kind 0, b < 0).
Lead log: runs/2026-11-12-lead/log.md. Tests so far: runs/2026-11-12-kicker-cup.


1. Physical laws and bodies  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12)
-----------------------------------------
Physical kicker (Requirements F 1-8):
 a. Bodies: the ball, and one static body, the kicker's collision mesh: the cup (hand-made, faces only where a ball touches) plus the hole's wall below the rim (c gives the rim).
    Nothing in the kicker moves; the switch and pins are left out (F 3).
 b. Law: the existing static-mesh contact (ContactCore: Hertz + damping + friction + twist, BarsTriangles for several pressed faces). No new law. Material: hard surface
    (cup plastic or steel), from an ini section [Kicker:<name>] with [Surfaces] defaults, as walls do.
 c. The hole: the playfield's faces do not press a ball inside the hole's circle, as for the turntable's CarvedHole. This replaces today's exclusion (g_playfieldHoles /
    InPlayfieldHole), which takes the playfield away from the ball at any height and hands the ball to stock. Playfield only, as the turntable (F 2). The hole's radius is
    the real cutout's (18.93 mm at the playfield top for the Sys7 cutout, measured in Kickers.blend; 1.34 mm bevel to 17.6, wall 16.4), part of the cup data, not the VPX
    kicker radius (25 U = 13.5 mm, the ball's own radius).
 d. Gravity, the cup and friction bring the ball to rest; nothing else holds it (F 1).
 e. The kick: one instantaneous velocity change of the ball where it is (the script's speed, angle, inclination, scatter and offset), the drive; the cup and rim then act
    on the ball through the same contact law (F 4).
 f. Held: the cup switch closes when the ball's centre is inside the hole and its bottom is within 0.5 mm of the resting depth, and opens only 3 mm above that close point
    (Requirements F 6, changed by the user 2026-11-12 after K2's Hit chatter at a single 8 mm depth), judged on each step's chord as trigger None zones do. It moves nothing.
Virtual kicker (F 9-10):
 g. Bodies: the ball and a sensing zone. A parked ball is not stepped (its own flag, not stock's m_lockedInKicker).
 h. Holding (user 2026-11-13, after K4): a parked or carried ball has infinite mass. It is not stepped (no gravity, no surface contacts, spin zero); its place is fixed
    (parked) or the arm's path at each moment (carried). In the shared ball-ball pair law it is a body that forces do not move, with a given velocity (0, or the path's);
    another ball meets it with the same law and bounces off it as off a ball that will not move. A parked ball does no work; a carried ball's work on another ball is the
    arm's drive work, booked. This puts in the unified law what stock does for its locked ball (hitball.cpp: infinite mass). History: the magnets' held form is an energy
    term, not a contact (K3c); a reset hold (ordinary ball in the cycle, put back at its end) let a resting neighbour sink 0.1 mm in 150 ms (K4) and keeping the put-back's
    depth would store energy made by a teleport.
 i. Capture zeroes the parked ball's speed and spin (energy only leaves); Kick restores motion as the drive in e.
Stock is not deleted (user 2026-11-12): on the unified path the new kicker replaces it; with unified = 0 the stock kicker runs unchanged. Once accepted, the unified path's
own kicker workarounds go: the g_playfieldHoles exclusion and its hand-over to stock, the m_lockedInKicker skips inside the contact system (PairEligible, contactsystem.cpp
skips), the stock counter's kicker rows. KickerHitCircle, collide.cpp, quadtree.cpp and hitball.cpp's frozen ball stay for stock.


2. Stiff things and the step  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12; ModelStandards 3)
------------------------------------------------------------
 a. The kicker has no internal contacts (no stops, pins or blades: F 3), so its stiffest contact is the ball on the cup, a hard surface like a ball on a wall. Its step comes
    from the shared step rule (ModelStandards 2c); the kicker states no step facts of its own.
 b. Several pressed faces at once (two ledges, a ring of faces): one Hertz bar per pressed face (multi_contact, on for every static host except the playfield,
    hitrubber.cpp:1671). A ball resting across a fold between two faces is the trap 'no root at a fold' (ModelStandards 6): the cup mesh is built so no resting place
    sits on a fold, and the rest-grid check (mk_grid.py) runs on each cup.
 c. The rim: the playfield's carved hole (1c) gives it: a face point inside the circle moves out to the circle (rubbercontact.h:1060-1072), so the rim is a sharp circular edge
    of the playfield, with the same law and edge rounding (edge_radius_mm) as every mesh edge. The cutout's real bevel is left out until the playfield cut-hole work.
 d. The kick changes the ball's velocity between cycles, as any script action does; the next step meets the ball already moving (its approach speed is a step fact). The
    resting squash given back at the kick is micro-joules (depth about 1 um, measured offline).
 e. Virtual kicker: a ball meeting a parked ball is a steel-on-steel pair, the stiffest contact in the game, the same as two moving balls today: the shared rule.
 f. A held ball at rest is touching every cycle; whether it sleeps (to-do 1.10, sleep off by default) belongs to the cost section.


3. Energy  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12; ModelStandards 2h)
-----------------------------------------
 a. Drives: gravity (the ball falls into the cup) and the kick. Nothing else adds energy: no lift to playfield level (stock's hold lifts the ball), no funnel, no speed restore.
 b. Losses: the contact's damping and friction in the cup and on the rim; the virtual kicker's capture (speed and spin set to zero) is a loss the kicker reports.
 c. The kick acts between cycles. Its work (kinetic energy after minus before, the scatter and a downward inclination included) is reported to the energy book as drive work,
    so the between-cycle comparison does not read it as a rise.
 d. A kick or a CreateBall that starts a ball inside something (the script's offset): the existing first-sight rule applies (contactsystem.cpp:810: a contact found inside
    that was not inside at the last cycle's end has stored nothing; the kicker calls NotePlaced). No stored energy reaches the ball.
 e. The resting squash in the cup was stored by gravity and is given back by the same contact law; nothing to book.
 f. Checks: the energy book closes every cycle on the bench (catch, rest, kick); a ball falling into the cup and resting never gains energy (step sweep, ModelStandards 5a).


4. The kick  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12)
-------------------------
 a. Physical kicker: the kick changes only the held ball's velocity, where it is; it never moves the ball. KickXYZ's x, y, z offsets and KickZ's height are teleports and are
    ignored (one on-screen notice per kicker, rule 8). Angle and inclination in degrees only (user 2026-11-12: the radians rule only confuses). Horizontal speed = speed cos(incl),
    vertical = speed sin(incl), up or down (stock's downward quirk dropped). Scatter as today. The cup and rim then act through the contact law.
    REPLACED 2026-11-13 (user): a velocity set in the cup sends a fast ball into the hole wall before it rises (K3: 0.86 -> 0.30 m/s), and a fixed upward speed does too.
 c. The arm stroke (physical kicker, APPROVED by the user 2026-11-13, picture runs/2026-11-12-kicker-cup/arm_final.png): the ball is carried along a quarter circle of radius 17 mm (an idealised kicker arm, its pivot below the end
    point, in the plane of the kick direction after scatter): from its resting place, starting straight up, to the top, where it moves horizontally in the kick direction with
    its bottom 0.25 in (6.35 mm) above the playfield (rise 17.0 mm for Sys7, 17 mm forward, about 3 mm clear of the hole). Speed (user 2026-11-13, replaces steady
    acceleration): the ball reaches the kick's speed within the first 1 ms cycle and keeps it along the arc until release (full arc 26.7 mm: 62 ms at 0.43 m/s, 27 ms at
    1 m/s), then flies free. Inclination (user 2026-11-13): the stroke ends where the arc's direction equals the inclination: none or 0 deg = the full arc, 45 deg = half the
    arc (released 12.0 mm higher, 5.0 mm forward, moving 45 deg up), 90 deg = no arc (straight up from rest). Values chosen for feel, not measured.
    Carried, not riding: the path is prescribed (a real arm would lose the ball near the top above 0.41 m/s). During the carry: spin zero; the cup is not touched (the arc is
    clear of it); another ball meets the carried ball as in 1h (infinite mass moving on the path); the switch opens as in 1f.
    Energy: the arm's work (kinetic energy at exit plus the lift's m g h) is the kick's drive work, booked as in 3c.
 b. Virtual kicker: today's behaviour, for compatibility: offsets applied, velocity set, stock's speed split; but angle and inclination in degrees only, as a (user 2026-11-12).
    Stated limit: an old script that passes inclination in radians (e.g. KickZ 0, 30, 1.56 for a kick straight up) kicks nearly flat and must be edited.


5. Cost estimate  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12; DevelopmentRules 5, ModelStandards 4)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Offline measurements (runs/2026-11-12-kicker-cup, results_handmade.txt; one contact check = BarsTriangles with the faces within the ball's reach, true distance):
 Sys7 hand-made cup, 341 faces: 0.11 us at rest, 1.07 / 0.99 us 2 / 5 mm off rest (full display mesh 0.45 / 5.3 / 3.0 us). MEASURED.
 a. 1 ball (held or rolling through): checks per object-cycle median 4 (to-do 1.17) -> about 0.5 us a cycle at rest, about 4 us rolling. INFERRED (offline cost x engine count).
 b. 2 balls (one held, one hitting it or both in a large cup): the cup's cost twice plus one ball pair; about 8 us. INFERRED.
 c. 6 balls: a kicker holds one ball; 6 only as a pile over the hole (rare); stated, not designed for. Cost = the pile's, already judged by the pile requirements (Requirements B).
 d. Worst seen 598 checks per object-cycle (to-do 1.17) would be 60-640 us for any cup: over the 30 us limit, as for every mesh; the fix is the check count (1.17), not the cup.
 e. The hole: each playfield face check tests the carved holes (a few ns each); with about 10 kickers on a table, only holes near the ball should be tested (the finder's
    list), else every playfield check pays for every hole. To measure.
 f. Sensing zone: one chord test per ball near the kicker per cycle (trigger None zones: ns). Virtual kicker: one ball pair per ball near a parked ball.
 g. Sleep: a held ball at rest costs about 0.5 us a cycle, so sleep (to-do 1.10) is not needed for the kicker.
 h. Measured in the engine before the kicker is called done: worst cycle and worst 7-cycle window with 1, 2 and 6 balls, focused driver run (rule 3).


6. What breaks  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12; DevelopmentRules 7; all INFERRED until tested)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
 a. A physical kicker not on the playfield (wall top, ramp): no carved hole there (1c). It is refused at load with a notice (rule 8) asking for the virtual type. Stated limit
    until the cut-hole work.
 b. VPX kicker radius vs the cup: the drawn kicker and the real cup can differ in size; physics uses the cup, the drawing is untouched; a notice when they differ by more
    than a set amount.
 c. CreateBall into an occupied kicker is not allowed (user 2026-11-12), both types: when a ball is held, or any ball overlaps the new ball's place, no ball is created
    (CreateBall returns nothing) and a notice is raised once per kicker (rule 8).
 d. Kick with no held ball: nothing happens (as today). Kick speed 0: the ball stays. Huge speed: an ordinary fast ball (the fast-ball watchdog sees it).
 e. Kick or DestroyBall inside the Hit handler: events fire after the step (2f), so the ball is in a settled state of the step; same as triggers today.
 f. Scripts that read BallCntOver, LastCapturedBall or the held ball: answered from the held state (F 6); a ball that rattles out is no longer held.
 g. A held ball now feels magnets, nudge and tilt (stock froze it): correct physics; a nudge can rock it out of a shallow cup.
 h. Table restart and teardown: carved holes cleared at engine start (as the turntable's), the cup host deleted with the kicker, a parked ball destroyed with the table.
 i. A kicker hole over another hole or a turntable: both carve; stated, not designed for.
 j. A table without kickers, and a second table in the same process: nothing kept in statics but the hole list, which is cleared at engine start.
 k. A virtual KickXYZ / KickZ offset or a CreateBall that places the ball inside an object leaves it embedded there for good: the first-sight rule (3d) stores no energy from
    the placement, so nothing pushes it out (K5a: 11 mm inside Wall10; 8 mm into the playfield, rolling 1.5 s across the table that deep). No notice (user 2026-11-13): the
    table author must choose offsets that start the ball in free space.
 Checked how: each case above gets a line in the kicker's stress set (rule 10) or the bench; which are run is decided when the build is planned.


7. Settings, events and the interface  (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-12; ModelStandards 2a, 2f, 2g; DevelopmentRules 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 a. Switch: [Features] kickers = 1 (on by default while tested); 0 = today's stock kicker unchanged. Deleted with the stock path once the user accepts the new one.
 b. Per kicker, [Kicker:<name>] in the table's physics ini: type = physical | virtual (default virtual, user 2026-11-12 after K3); cup = sys7 (the only
    cup at first); switch_depth_mm (default per cup, tuned); material keys as a wall's hard surface (e_mpa, mu, restitution, edge_radius_mm ...) with [Surfaces] defaults.
    Unknown or duplicate keys raise the notice (rule 8). Each key described beside it in the code; all values written to the log at load.
 c. The cup mesh is compiled in (a header like the stock meshes, mm, kicker frame), one per cup type; its hole radius is part of the cup data.
 d. Interface: the physical kicker is a static surface host (a HitRubber, as a wall builds one) plus the carved hole; no moving part, no GroupStep::Part. The virtual kicker
    is a sensing zone plus the parked-ball state; the parked ball enters the pair law as a held ball (pairform.h b < 0). No kicker special case inside contactsystem.cpp.
 e. Events: Hit and Unhit from the held test (F 6), queued with their time and fired after the step, as trigger None zones do (trigger.cpp CycleEnd); no script runs inside
    the step. A destroyed ball is dropped from the queue (BallGone).
 f. Stock counter: the kicker leaves AllowedStock when converted; any stock use by a converted kicker fails the driver run.
