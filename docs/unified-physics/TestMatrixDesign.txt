1. Fixed limits every run must pass on its own  (approved by the user 2026-10-27)
-----------------------------------------------
A run fails if any one of these happens. No older run is needed to judge it.

1. Energy rises. Per cycle the total energy (balls: motion, spin, height; parts: motion and stored spring energy) may rise only by the work the drives put in
   (coils, motors, spring preload, gravity, nudge), plus an allowance: 1 % of the energy present or 1 mJ, whichever is larger (1 mJ = an 80 g ball from rest to
   0.16 m/s). The allowance is INFERRED, a starting value; MEASURED only offline: bumper energy error 0.8 % at a 1 us step (ProjectAssessment 3.0).
   Not built yet: the energy check (to-do 1.6) and the drive-work reports (ModelStandards 2h). Until they exist, limit 3 stands in for this one.
2. Overlap too deep. No contact deeper than 1.25 x the depth the contact law gives for that impact's closing speed. Scale: steel on steel at 3 m/s about 76 um
   (Hertz, E 210 GPa, ball R 13.5 mm, 80 g; calculated, not measured). The margin 1.25 is INFERRED, a starting value.
3. Launch. No ball faster than 8 m/s unless the test names a drive that can do it (e.g. the plunger). 8 m/s = the fast-ball watchdog default (VPX_SPEED_WATCH,
   PhysicsEngine.cpp); INFERRED as a limit, a starting value. Section 4 adds tighter limits per part.
4. Ball through a wall. After every cycle no ball is inside a solid object, below the playfield or outside the table. Zero allowed. Below the playfield = ball
   centre more than 200 U under it (user, 2026-10-28: pockets and subways are lower than the playfield).
5. Safety net tripped. Any trip fails the run: rewind cap reached, a ball with no past seated inside a part, an overlap replay, a hand-back to stock. Each is already
   a WARN and a notification (rule 8); a net may exist, a run that needs it fails.
6. Cost (DevelopmentRules rule 5). On the stress table: physics over the worst 7 consecutive cycles <= 3 ms, worst single cycle <= 1.5 ms. Per active object
   <= 30 us per 1 ms cycle with one ball. Measured on focused runs only.

The starting values (allowance in 1, margin in 2, speed in 3) are reviewed after the first stress-table run and only lowered with data.


2. Inputs that repeat exactly  (approved by the user 2026-10-27)
-----------------------------
Why runs differed (read from the code, not run; INFERRED unless marked):
- Script timers fire on physics time (stdafx.h ACCURATETIMERS; PhysicsEngine.cpp UpdatePhysics calls FireTimers(0) once per 1 ms cycle; hittimer.cpp Update). But the GL
  build stops firing them for the rest of a frame once the script has used 5 ms of real time in that frame (MAX_TIMERS_MSEC_OVERALL, unless frame pacing). The
  unfocused harness runs about 190 cycles per frame, so a test script that writes a file every 1 ms can pass 5 ms: inputs then shift with machine load.
  Most likely cause of the old matrix's run-to-run differences.
- Some work runs once per frame, not per cycle (frame-sync and game-sync timers, input polling, plugin messages); the cycles per frame depend on real time.
- One random generator with a fixed seed (def.cpp mwc64x_state) serves stock scatter, the collision order swaps (PhysicsEngine.cpp), the kd-tree order, and
  also sounds and light sequences: draws made per frame can shift the physics draws.
- MEASURED before: flipper and parity runs gave identical files (cmp); the sling script (gsling2) repeated only its first ~4 shots.

Design: an engine test driver, harness only; test inputs no longer go through the table script.
1. A plain input file lists actions by physics millisecond: create a ball (position, velocity, spin, mass), teleport, flipper on/off, coil fire, nudge.
2. At the start of each cycle, before the physics step, the driver applies that cycle's actions. No script and no frame timing involved.
3. The driver resets the random generator at table start.
4. The driver records the ball states and checks the section 1 limits inside the engine (no loss to slow script sampling).
5. run.ps1 gets an input-file option; the table script stays empty.
6. Once per build, one case runs twice and the two output files must be identical: the proof of repeatability.
Needs: one new file, one call in PhysicsEngine.cpp (already ours), the reseed, the run.ps1 option. Environment variable for the input file: harness only, listed in
NewPhysicsReference.txt.

4. Pass cases per model  (approved by the user 2026-10-27)
-----------------------
Two rules for every model, on top of section 1:
a. Tighter launch limit: a ball's exit energy <= its entry energy + the most the part's drive could add in the contact; a part with no drive (wall, rubber, gate,
   spinner) <= entry energy. Needs each part's drive-work report (ModelStandards 2h, not built); until then section 1 limit 3 (8 m/s) applies.
b. Events fire exactly once per hit: no doubles, none missed.

Common cases, every model, each at 1 m/s and 3 m/s (ModelStandards 5c):
- one hit at three angles
- two balls on the part
- a ball between this part and another
- a contact found late (ball placed already touching the part)
- the part switched off
- table restart with a ball on the part
- offline: step sweep against the 1 us reference. Fail: energy rises at any step size, or exit speed more than 5 % or direction more than 2 deg from the reference.
  5 % and 2 deg are INFERRED starting values.

Extra cases per model:
1. Contact core (playfield, walls, rubbers, posts, ball on ball): drop that ends at rest with no creep; 13-ball pile at rest; 10 m/s ball into a thin post (no
   tunnelling); a ball pinched between two balls.
2. Flippers: flip with a ball resting on the bat; ball hits the bat mid-swing (part hit while driven); release at the end of the stroke (launch of 2026-10-13);
   ball pinched between bat and sling at full swing; two balls on a raised bat (to-do 2.5).
3. Bands and slings: sling fires with a ball on the band; ball pinched between band and post; hits at three points along the band.
4. Jet bumpers: fires with a ball on the skirt; ball between two bumpers; three-bumper triangle; pinch under the ring; Collidable switched with a ball inside
   (to-do 4.2, 4.3).
5. Gates and spinners: both directions; back-hit on a one-way gate; hit while still swinging or spinning; ball placed inside it.
6. Magnets (off-axis only, ModelStandards 8): pass by with the coil on; coil switched on with a ball near the pole; hold and release; 2 and 6 balls; a non-magnetic
   ball.
7. Turntable: ball rolls onto the spinning disc; ball at the disc edge / gap; motor starts with a ball on it; 2 and 3 balls on the spinning disc (user,
   2026-10-28: common, not rare; UnifiedContactDesign 12.1 f).
Open: bands have no SaveState, so their rows run without the snapshot self-test (VPX_SNAPSHOT_TEST) until to-do 1.3 is done. Flipper bats have it since 2026-10-28
   (run_bat_flip1, input runs/2026-10-09-slow-contact/drive_flip1.txt, row 2 on the stress table; its 1 m/s mid-swing timing needs retiming). The disc has it since
   2026-10-28 (run_disc3: 12,001 steps, 0 different; input runs/2026-10-09-slow-contact/drive_disc3.txt covers row 7 on the stress table).
   Design for to-do 1.3: UnifiedContactDesign.txt section 12 (approved 2026-10-28, not built); its proof is 12.4.
