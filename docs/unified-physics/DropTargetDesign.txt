1. BODIES AND COORDINATES (DRAFT)
---------------------------------
Paper numbers first (lead arithmetic, INFERRED):
- Fall: the flat target's top drops 30.9 mm to flush. Gravity alone takes 79 ms; the 15-35 ms of D5 needs a mean net downward acceleration of 51-276 m/s^2,
  i.e. a mean net force of 0.4-2.2 N on an 8 g target (D2 6.8-9.1 g). The drop spring is the drive; its size comes from the fall time (TUNED, D5).
- Resting ball (Requirement I.12): an 80 g ball on a 6.5 deg slope pushes 0.09 N. The latch's friction, mu times the spring's pull on the lip (mu 0.3 nylon on
  steel ASSUMED), is 0.12-0.66 N: a resting ball usually leaves the target up, near the edge with a weak spring. The requirement holds without a rule.
- Brick (I.5): a ball pressing the face with N pinches the target on the slot's back wall; friction mu N holds the fall when mu N > the spring's pull, N > 1.3-7 N
  at mu 0.3. A hard hit's contact force is far above that, so a pinch holds the target up while the ball presses. The brick itself (the target still up after
  the ball leaves) needs the target to come forward onto its lip again before it has fallen past the catch: a race between the back and the down motion.
Proposal: one rigid body, the target, on two coordinates:
   y  back travel, 0 resting forward on the latch lip; the slot's back wall a stop behind it; unlatched once y passes the lip width (D3: 1/16 in default).
   z  drop, 0 latched up; the latch a stop under it only while y is short of the lip width; the bottom stop at flush (D4).
   Drives: the drop spring (down, with a forward share that pulls the target onto the lip: D1, D7) and gravity; the reset bar as a driven stop under z (as the
   plunger's coil stop). Friction: Coulomb at the lip and at the slot's walls, loaded by the spring and by the balls' push.
   Tilt and flex left out (the VPW script's 8 deg bend is drawn only); the brick by flex (I.5) is then not covered, only the pinch.
DECIDED 11-19 (user): A, mass on both y and z (the paper below).
The choice was: mass on which coordinates.
   A. Mass on both (8 g): the fall and the race of the brick come out of the motion. Risk: the light-part trap (to-do 11.1; the stand-up face): an 8 g body with a
      ball's Hertz contact on it sets steps of tens of us while the ball presses.
   B. Mass on z only, y massless (as the stand-up face): y follows the ball's push, yielding at the lip's friction, stopped by the wall. Cheaper; but with no mass
      the target comes forward the instant the ball lets go, so the brick's race is decided by z alone (a modelling choice, to check on paper).
   Lead recommendation: decide on paper, 1-D then 2-D, the 4 standard cases (soft hit that does not drop, normal hit that drops and the ball carries on past,
   hard head-on hit, resting ball) with A and B, against an all-mass 1 us reference; judge fall time, ball exit speed, brick or not, energy.
   Paper (runs/2026-11-19-drop-paper, 2-D head-on, flat target, all contacts springs, 1 us, converged at 0.5 us): drop or not and flush time agree (0.1 m/s holds,
   0.2 m/s and up drop, flush 27-41 ms after the touch); B throws the ball back about 0.4 m/s harder at every speed from 0.3 m/s (1 m/s rolling: -0.26 A, -0.69 B)
   and keeps the massless target pressed on a ball moving away. Resting ball held, no creep, in both. Energy worst rise 0.004 mJ.
   A's fastest time scale: the target pinched between the ball and the back wall rings at 122-178 us (stop stiffness 1e7 N/m ASSUMED): a cost risk for the
   engine that points to impact-law stops (ModelStandards 3a), not to dropping the mass. Lead recommendation: A.
   Paper side result, ACCEPTED by the user 11-19 (PartData D6a): a head-on hit at normal speed rebounds (target on the back wall within about 2 ms, long before
   it has dropped); 0.39 of the incoming speed at 1 m/s on paper, about 0.38 from the user's video-tuned script. A glancing hit carries on past.

3. ENGINE FORM (DRAFT)
----------------------
Facts (lead, code read 11-19): a moving surface belongs to ONE GroupStep::Part (ContactMovingPart::GroupPart; a Hit's Columns name that part's coordinates).
BodyPart (bodypart.h, 658 lines) is one coordinate through and through: one pin stop, one rate, StopExcess / PinOnStop / FreedByStep / Accept / Impact on that
coordinate. Its users: hinge (gates, spinners), disc, button cap, plunger rod, auto plunger. Parts with more coordinates (bumper 4, bat 2) carry their own stop code.
The target needs one part with two coordinates, each with its own stops, pins and friction. Options:
   A. Widen BodyPart itself to N coordinates. One class, but every user's code paths change at once.
   B. A target part of its own with two coordinates, reusing BodyStop / BodyStopLaw / the impact law, its pin and stop logic written for two (as the bumper and
      bat did). Least risk to others; a third copy of the stop logic (to-do 27 already asks for one shared stop piece).
   C. Split BodyPart into a per-coordinate AXIS (state, stops, pin, impact, rest rule, friction pin) and the Part around it: today's BodyPart = a part with one
      axis (unchanged behaviour), the target = a part with two. This is to-do 27's shared stop piece, started for the 1-coordinate bodies.
   Lead recommendation: C. One stop path for every body (rule: one shared path), the target gets it without a copy. Cost: a refactor of a shared file with five
   users; proof = the same bytes on each user's bench run at /fp:precise before and after (ModelStandards traps 115, 117), done as its own piece before the target.
   Couplings between the two axes, all between steps (constant inside a step): the latch stop on z is present only while y < lip, the catch stop on y only
   while z > 0 (a stop switched on while the body is past it waits for its free side: BodyStop.armed); the walls' friction on z is loaded by the ball's push
   while y rests on the back wall. A crossing of y = lip inside a step acts at the step's end: at most one step late (INFERRED small: steps are 0.1-1 ms with
   the flexes, the fall 25 ms; to check offline).
DECIDED 11-19 (user): C, to-do 27's shared stop piece. Sizing (runs/2026-11-19-drop-axis/sizing.md; lead checked hingegroup.h:523, groupstep.h:2140-2148,
partrecords.h:162): about 400 of bodypart.h's 658 lines move into an Axis, 100-150 new, 20-40 changed, all in bodypart.h; the five users need no source change
if BodyState stays the first state record (hingegroup.h:523 reads at sizeof(BodyState)) and the one-coordinate callbacks keep their names (axis 0). The group
step already loops over coordinates; a 2-axis part needs: every axis on a crossing stop pinned (else the 1e-29 s search trap, groupstep.h:2146), the balls'
load per part read by each axis, pending drive / loss summed, stop events with an axis index, the Turn step fact per axis kind (an angle or a length).
Byte risk: Eval's row text, Accept's summing order, record order: proof at /fp:precise.

4. FRICTION AS A RIGID HOLD (DRAFT, drop lead 2, 2026-11-19; for the cost fault, STATUS item 1; the user: right for this model, not the general answer)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured (runs/2026-11-19-drop-lead2/prof, bench, 3 m/s head-on, P3 build): 162 of the hit's 173 steps are set by z's pin stick spring (bodypart.h PinStep, Mindlin's
patch K ~ 3e6 N/m at a 200-243 N push, h = 0.39 / omega ~ 14-73 us); worst cycle 48 steps, about 12 us a step. The hold needs 0.06-1.96 N (P2 trace) against a limit of
24-83 N: the spring stretches under 1 um and stores under 1 uJ. Its motion carries nothing the ball or the drop depends on: ModelStandards 3a, a rigid law.
a. Law: Coulomb's static friction as a constraint. An axis of the target at rest whose net force (drive, spring, gravity, the balls' load) is within the friction limit
   (mu N, N from the lip or the walls, as today's AxisFrictionLimit) is HELD: no unknown (dof 0), as a body on a stop, but two-sided, at its own place.
b. Leaving: after a step solved with the axis held, the net force needed past the limit frees it and the step is solved again with it free (BodyAxis's existing
   AxisFreedByStep path for a body on a stop, extended to the hold). It leaves at rest: the spring's ring speed (today's release speed) is gone, physically < 1 uJ.
c. Entering: (1) at a step's start, rate 0 and net within the limit (the pinch: the latch goes from under z, which is at rest; Decide's existing place).
   (2) inside a step, a sliding axis whose friction can stop it: the friction in the axis's row is the force that brings its rate to zero, clipped to +-lim
   (implicit Coulomb at the velocity level); unclipped, the step ends at rest and the axis is held from the next step; clipped, it slides at dynRatio x lim.
d. Energy: a held axis does no work (no move); sliding loses dynRatio lim |dq|; nothing stores. The pin spring's book terms go.
e. Deleted for the target: its pin spring (AxisPinStiffness override, patchReffM), BodyAxis::pinStepFact (only the target sets it). The hinge, disc, button and
   plungers keep PinStep (pivot_model 1) unchanged: the hold is per axis, opt-in, so their bytes stay (proof at /fp:precise as P0).
f. What it does not handle (rule 7): a load swinging back and forth across the limit (stick-slip) makes one re-solve per release (count it); a hold
   decided at a step's start acts at most one step late when the latch goes mid-step (the lip mark ends the step there: INFERRED none); friction across axes
   inside one step (the walls' N from y's push) stays the between-steps coupling of P1. Not the general friction answer: one coordinate's friction, not a contact patch.
g. Checks: offline P2 cases (S03, N1, H3, H6, reset, resting ball) at 1 us and step scales 1, 0.5, 0.25: drop time, rebound, brick or not and energy against today's
   (should match within the stick's < 1 uJ), steps per hit; bench driveB: the pinch's steps per cycle and worst cycle (today 48, 946 us traced); S, P, A bytes.
   Cost after it is not predicted: the pinch's steps then come from the ball's contact (76 us unsteady or 30-700 us steady in this hit).

5. THE TILTING TARGET (DRAFT v2, drop lead 3, 2026-11-20; replaces sections 1-4 once reviewed with the user section by section)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: lead 3 log (runs/2026-11-20-drop-lead3/log.md) and the paper chain runs/2026-11-20-drop-pin, -tilt, -beamref, -modal, -modalstep, -wall, -impulse, -twoscale,
-twoscale2, -lean. Yardstick: the converged 20-element beam model (beamref part B with the agreed defaults, impulse.py): flat target, 1 m/s rolling (N1) rebound 0.655,
3 m/s head-on (H3) 0.648. It is a model, not data (user 11-20: no measured rebound exists). Goals (user 11-20): rebound within 10 % of it (better wanted), the drop fully

5.1 Bodies and coordinates (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-20)
   One rigid body, the target, on two coordinates: theta, its tilt about the bottom clip (the clip lets it turn, not translate: D9), and z, its drop (the clip slides
   down with it). The clip sits P below the playfield top, a per-target setting, default 4 in (101.6 mm). Its mass, its inertia about the clip and its bend come from
   the selected type's shape (DECIDED 11-20): the drawn mesh above, its lowest section carried down to the clip, nylon 1.14 g/cm3 and 2.5 GPa (ESTIMATED; ABS an option).
   Flat target: 5.87 g, 3.82e-5 kg m2 about the clip. The bend is not a coordinate: it is a static spring with a stored energy (5.2b), never stepped as a vibration.
   Why not more: the target's slowest bends (196 / 655 Hz flat) are too slow for an impact law and too fast for one step per cycle as coordinates (paper -impulse,
   -modalstep); what they do to the ball is carried by 5.2b's static bend and 5.2c's loss. Left out: the vibrations themselves, the target rocking between the wall and
   the lip after the ball leaves (the beam model shows it slowing the drop to 39-40 ms, outside D5: user 11-20, not modelled), sideways tilt.

5.2 Contacts, stops and losses (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-20)
   a. Ball on the face while the target is free of the wall: an impact. Restitution from the shared Hertz + Kuwabara-Kono law (rubbercontact.h ImpactRestitutionKK) at
      the reduced mass of the ball and the target's tilt at the contact height. Repeat touches inside a cycle are taken in time order (5.4).
   b. Ball on the face while the target's back is on the slot's wall edge: a press. The ball rides the target's static bend: the stiffness at the contact height with
      three supports (clip, wall edge, latch point on the lip side), k(a) (flat 5.16e4 N/m at the ball centre's height), Hertz in series left out (60-240 x stiffer).
      Stored energy V(p, a) = 1/2 k(a) (p - theta (a - a0))^2. The face lean (the ball's upward push: the exit hop) is the slope of V along the face, the one contact
      force acting on the bent face's turned normal, not an added force (paper -lean). The nylon loss as a damper at the ball-on-bend frequency (zeta = tan_delta / 2).
      The press is a damped oscillator solved in closed form over the cycle (exact).
   c. Energy the press leaves in the target's faster bends: the bends' response to the press pulse (closed form per mode), lost in the nylon, taken from the ball's
      normal speed when the press ends. Booked once per press from the whole pulse (fault 5.5 b), never more than the kinetic energy there.
   d. Squeeze: a wall hit while the ball is on the face, or a face touch while the target is on the wall, pins the target (inelastic).
   Rear hit (user 2026-11-21, replaces 11-20's slot front edge): the front stop is the latch lip (the stem rests forward against it, 5.5d), so a ball from behind
      presses the target's bend with the clip and the lip as supports, mirroring 5.2b (no rear face in the model yet: paper -cases-a).
   e. Rigid stops by the impact law: the wall edge (paper -wall: its stiffness changes the beam model's rebound < 1 %), the front stop, the latch top (holds z while
      the latch point is short of the lip), the lip side (the catch), the bottom (inelastic in the cycle it is crossed). The reset bar: a driven stop (the coil).
   f. Coulomb friction (mu 0.3, ASSUMED): ball on the face, the target on the wall edge and lip side (loads the drop), ball on the playfield.
   g. Drives: gravity, the drop spring (pull and forward share), the reset coil. Nothing else adds energy.

5.3 Every call energy safe (user 11-20; APPROVED by the user 2026-11-20). The step's form makes a rise impossible, not only detected:
   Form: v1 = v0 + (dt F_c + sum J) / m and q1 = q0 + dt (v0 + v1) / 2, so the energy change is F_c . dq + sum J . (v0 + v1) / 2. Hence two rules, one per kind of call:
   R1 Conservative forces (gravity, drop spring, the forward spring torque, the bend V(p, a) with its lean): a discrete gradient over the step's own move,
      F_c = -(V(q1) - V(q0)) / (q1 - q0), solved with that move, so F_c . dq = -dV exactly (paper -lean: the lean's own book +1.8e-12 mJ).
   R2 Every impulse (contact, stop, latch, friction, squeeze): its work at the step's MEAN velocity is never positive, J . (v0 + v1) / 2 <= 0. Normal: restitution
      e <= 1 (v_n1 = -e v_n0). Friction: against the mean slip, clipped at stick, never reversing it, solved with the other impulses so v1 is the final one.
      Stops: inelastic or e <= 1, found as events in time order (5.4), never a position clamp (a clamp that keeps speed raised energy +2.4 mJ, paper -twoscale2).
   R3 Losses (nylon damper, the bends' loss 5.2c) only remove energy, never more than is there.
   R4 Sources (CLAUDE.md 3): gravity and the drop spring's preload are stored energies (V = m g h; V = F_s (f_fwd y - z) as a tilt torque and a drop pull), in the
      book like the bend's; R1 lets each give the moving parts exactly what its stored energy drops, never more: the spring at most F_s x the drop (flat about
      22 mJ over 30.9 mm, INFERRED). Only the reset coil (its driven stop raises the target and restores the spring) and a nudge add energy, each booked as a drive.
   R5 A per-cycle energy book stays on as a net (rule 8 notice), never as the fix.
   Today's paper breaks R2 once (fault 5.5 a) and the old lean broke R1 (fixed): the build starts by bringing both to rule.

5.4 The step: one per 1 ms cycle, no sub-steps (APPROVED by the user 2026-11-20, as revised)
   Inside a cycle the free motions are straight lines (the target's tilt and the ball between touches) or the closed-form press, so events are found in time order and
   taken in closed form. An event IS a sub-step in cost (user 11-20): each needs a probe of the target's mesh (a few us each, INFERRED, unmeasured), so events count
   against the 30 us per object. The paper saw up to 9 per cycle, nearly all the free target ping-ponging between the ball and the wall edge.
   No cap (the paper's 40 is deleted). REVISED 11-20 (user OK, paper -v2faults): the knocks are taken one by one in time order (up to 9-10 per cycle in the first
   1-3 ms of a hit). A simultaneous impulse that pins the target cut events to 2-3 but raised the rebound 16-26 %: it drops the energy each knock loses at the wall
   (H3 165 against 60 mJ), so it is not the knock series' limit. If the cost test fails, sum the knock series in closed form keeping its loss (unchecked).
   First build test: the worst cycle's us with real events and mesh probes against the 30 us limit (DevelopmentRules 5). Paper results at 1 ms (MEASURED, paper -lean): rebound N1 0.658 (+0.4 %),
