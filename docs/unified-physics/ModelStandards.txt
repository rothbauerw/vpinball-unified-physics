MODEL STANDARDS - how every physics model is built  (approved by the user section by section, 2026-10-27)
=============================================================================================
Read with docs/unified-physics/DevelopmentRules.txt (the rules there come first). This file says HOW a model is built so that each new one reuses the last instead of relearning.
A model is not done until every box in section 7 is ticked.


1. Before writing anything
--------------------------
a. Name the closest existing model and read its files first:
      moving part with its own bodies  -> jet bumper (contactbumper.h, contactbumperpart.h) and gate/spinner (contacthingebody.h, contacthingepart.h)
      static surface                   -> contactsurface.h, hitrubber.cpp (hard surfaces, ramps)
      ball contact law                 -> rubbercontact.h ContactCore (Hertz + damping + friction + twist)
b. Read section 6 (known traps) of this file.
c. Write the design in docs/<Model>Design.txt: the physical laws, the bodies and their stiffest internal contact, the cost estimate, what it breaks (rule 7),
   and every way energy enters the model (coil, spring preload, motor), showing that only those add energy (ProjectAssessment-2026-10-27.txt 3.0).
d. Ask the user before engine code.


2. The contract every model meets
---------------------------------
a. One interface. A part that moves implements ContactMovingPart (contactmovingpart.h), all of it: Probe, BallStep, ContactItem, StepInIsland, AdvanceAlone,
   SaveState / RestoreState, SurfaceSpeedBound. No private stepping path; no special case for the model inside contactsystem.cpp.
   This is the rule until ProjectAssessment 3.0 (energy by construction) is built. The target: a part only describes itself (bodies, masses, springs, contact laws,
   stops, drives) and the engine steps part and balls together; the interface then shrinks to that description.
   The target's interface exists since 2026-10-31 (GroupStepDesign 18), though no real part uses it yet: a part on the group step implements GroupStep::Part (coordinates, stored energy and its forces, step facts, stop events, Loads) and ContactMovingPart's GroupPart, GroupSurfaces, GroupProbe, GroupLaw, TouchBegan, with Probe, SurfaceSpeedBound, Origin, Reach and SaveState / RestoreState as before. ContactItem, BallStep and StepInIsland are not called for it. A new part goes this way, not the old loop's. A part must keep no shapes of its own, and its AdvanceAlone calls GroupStep::AdvanceAlone.
b. One loop. Balls touching the model are stepped by the shared loop (SubstepsArrival). It gives the shared guards for free:
      arrival steps        NextStep
      closing speed        relative to the surface, plus SurfaceSpeedBound
      depth check          RwLate, every substep, touching contacts included
      rewind               RwTake / RwRestore (needs the model's SaveState)
      overlap at first sight   UnifiedContactDesign.txt section 11
      energy check         built 2026-10-31, contactsystem.cpp EnergyCheckStart / End: ball energy with every contact's stored energy; no island with a drive in it yet
      stock counter        built 2026-10-31, stockcount.cpp: every stock Collide and Contact by object in every driver run; a converted object that uses stock fails the run. The model leaves AllowedStock the day it is converted
      recovery guard       a step that left the model's range: stop and restart from zero inside the contact system (no stock hand-back, no cooldown); WARN + on-screen report
      on-screen report     rule 8
   An exception (today: bands; the turntable disc and the flipper bats joined 2026-10-28) goes in the to-do with the missing piece; the model is not called done.
   Once 3.0 is proven the guard list gets shorter; the rule stays: use what the shared loop provides.
c. One step rule. The substep a contact needs comes from the shared step function, never from constants inside the model. (Today the rule exists in four copies:
   see ProjectAssessment-2026-10-27.txt 3.1. Until the shared function exists, a new model calls NextStep's rule, not a copy of it.)
   On the group step (GroupStepDesign 18.4) a part states facts through StepFactList: a stiffness, an approach, a turn, a time left, a time constant, a field. The factors live in GroupStep::Settings; a private omega-h or share in a part is a fault.
d. One contact law. A ball touching anything uses ContactCore with the material's parameters. A new law only if the physics differs (name the law), and then in
   rubbercontact.h beside the others, shared.
   We do not rewrite physics we already have (user, 2026-10-30). A ball pressing a band onto a post, end post or stop post, IS a rubber on a post and uses the same solve as a ring. Between a post and the span a band acts as a span for a short distance, then as a rubber on a post. Band behaviour is judged against a rubber on a post in that region; the rubber-on-post rebound targets are not targets for free spans.
e. Pure SI maths with no VPX types in the model's header, so the same code runs in the offline test. VPX units only in the adapter.
f. Events go into the part's event list (or DeferEvent) and are fired after the step. No script runs inside the step.
g. Settings come from the table's physics ini through the settings reader. Environment variables are for diagnostics only, read once, and listed in
   NewPhysicsReference.txt. Duplicate or unknown keys raise a notification (rule 8).
h. Energy enters only through named drives (coil, motor, spring preload, gravity, nudge). The model reports their work so the energy books close every cycle.


3. Stiff things
---------------
a. The stiffest contact sets the step for the whole island. For every internal contact (stops, pins, switch blades) ask first: does the motion during the contact matter?
   If not, use the impact law: restitution from the same Hertz + damping law, then a resting constraint, no stiff spring. (Proven on the bumper ring stops 2026-10-27.)
   The bounce can never return more energy than came in.
b. A part's own bodies step at their own time scale, not once per ball substep. Fine steps only near a stop. The engine chooses those step sizes, not the part.
c. A part with nothing driving it and nothing touching it sleeps, and SurfaceSpeedBound still covers what can wake it.
d. Steep fields count as stiff too: a force that rises sharply over a short distance (e.g. a magnet's pull near the pole) sets the step like a stiff contact.


4. Cost
-------
a. Frame budget at 144 Hz: physics over any 7 consecutive cycles <= 3 ms, including the worst case (confirmed by the user 2026-11-03 for ball piles); single cycle <= 1.5 ms (proposed). Burst exception (user 2026-11-03): within 0.2 s after a hit event (a landing, a flip, a fast ball into a pile) up to 10 ms per 7 cycles; everything else, every pile at rest included, stays at 3 ms.
b. Per active object (proposed): <= 30 us per 1 ms cycle with one ball on it; state the cost with 2 and 6 balls.
   (User, 2026-11-06: 30 us in EACH 1 ms cycle for the object with the ball(s) on it (user, 2026-11-06), worst cycle counts. Not a 7-cycle window, not other objects.)
c. Measure with VPX_CONTACT_TIMING (per-cycle CSV) and VPX_CONTACT_PROFILE (scopes, CPROF). Report: worst 7-cycle window, p99, maximum, and what the slow cycles
   spent their time on. Profile before optimising: a guess at the cost is not a finding.
d. New step code: no allocation, no string building, no getenv, no dynamic_cast in loops. Existing allocations are tidied when that code is next touched (user,
   2026-10-27). Material constants computed once at setup.
e. Every model runs on the stress table before it is called done.
f. Energy-safe stepping (ProjectAssessment 3.0): report the passes per contact it needs and how much longer each step can be in return.


5. Tests
--------
a. Offline: the model alone in runs/<date>-<model>/ against a 1 us fixed-step reference: the outputs that matter, plus the energy balance (work in - losses - stored).
   Sweep the step size and report where it breaks. At every step size the energy may only fall unless a drive adds it: a rise at any step size is a fail.
b. In the engine: its rows in the test matrix with numeric pass/fail, and the snapshot self-test (VPX_SNAPSHOT_TEST=1) with 0 differences.
   The test matrix is being rebuilt (user, 2026-10-27; the old batch_matrix.ps1 / batch_statu.ps1 matrix only showed behaviour staying the same and took 45-60 min).
   Basis of the new one (user-approved):
   1. Each run passes or fails on its own against fixed limits (energy never rises, overlap within limit, no launch, no ball through a wall, cost within budget).
      No old run is needed.
   2. Inputs repeat exactly: the table script's inputs follow physics time, not a timer, so the same build gives the same result.
   3. Offline first: most checks run on the model alone in seconds; the engine runs only what needs the whole table.
   4. Results are saved with the build they came from; nothing is rerun just to compare.
   The design (limits, test driver, stress table, cases per model, offline split, run time) is in docs/unified-physics/TestMatrixDesign.txt (user-approved 2026-10-27). Not built yet.
c. Play-speed cases: 1 m/s typical, 3 m/s extreme; two balls on the part; a ball between two parts; table restart; the part switched off.
   Plus the cases that have launched balls before: a contact found late, a ball pinched between two moving parts, a part hit while its coil drives it.
d. Use run.ps1 and the test matrix in docs/unified-physics/TestMatrixDesign.txt (shared offline bench, engine test driver, stress table), not the old batch_*.ps1 matrix. A new script is
   added to the shared set with a one-line description, not left as a one-off.
e. Every matrix run writes its pass/fail report to disk the day it runs.


6. Known traps (each has cost at least one session)
---------------------------------------------------
- Stiff stop springs set the step: bumper 2026-10-27, 85 % of the step time.
- The step was planned once per cycle from the arrival time, so the contact after arrival ran at the arrival step: bumper launch, 2026-10-27.
- The acceleration bound left out the part's own drive: overlap at first sight, 2026-10-26.
- Free balls in an island missed their gravity in the substeps: energy source, 2026-10-21.
- A destroyed part kept balls pointing at it: crash at play end, 2026-10-20 (ReleaseBat).
- A host object left in the registry across table restarts: turntable slowdown, 2026-10-13.
- A duplicate ini key silently won: magnet ball_interaction, 2026-10-20.
- Harness: unfocused runs throttle the 1 ms timer; the anti-hung rule skips game time; always check which ini was loaded; VPX_CONTACT_TIMING=1 writes a file named "1".
- Scripted runs are not deterministic (script timer): compare against run-to-run noise from the same exe before calling a difference real.
- Long steps create energy: bumper replay at a 100 us step launches at 10.6 m/s vs about 0.5 m/s at 1 us, 2026-10-27. Catches it: 5a step sweep (energy may not rise).
- A fix went into one copy of a copied rule (parts use a fixed 9.81 m/s2, the core uses gravity plus nudge), 2026-10-27. Catches it: none yet (2c removes the copies).
- Comment and code disagree (hitrubber.h:57 says rest factor 0.2, hitrubber.cpp sets 0.8), 2026-10-27. Catches it: none yet.
- A matrix run left no report and was missed, 2026-10-27. Catches it: 5e.
- A new model kept a route to stock: a failed solve on the cord band set a stock cooldown, ball 1.04 -> 6.15 m/s in play while the offline proof stayed exact, 2026-10-30. Catches it: none yet.
- A solver's first guess kept between steps: a step taken again from a snapshot differed in the last digits (117 of 2001 steps), 2026-10-30. Catches it: the snapshot self-test (5b).
- A stretch re-seated at the friction limit with the damper's share in it, which charges the spring with work nobody did: the old loop's ring gains at least 21.9 mJ in a 3 m/s hit (bench, 1 us), a still bat face leaves with 32 % more energy at 6 m/s. First blamed on the spring stiffening with the squash (0.6 mJ at most at 1 m/s, also fixed): measured 2026-10-30 by switching each off, runs/2026-10-30-rubber-review. Catches it: an energy book that counts what the springs hold, 5a.
- A contact law for a thick solid body used on a thin tube: full Mindlin stiffness gives too much grip, whatever E or mu is (tangential to normal frequency 1.53 for nu 0.5). Catches it: rolling rebound targets on the model, 5a.
- A seat along a tilted normal pressed the ball 1.95 mm into the playfield: 109 J stored, launch at 34 m/s, 2026-10-30. Catches it: the energy check (between cycles).
- A probe returns a surface's nearest face only: a ramp's wall was hidden by its floor, the rewinds ran out, launch at 69 m/s, 2026-10-30. Catches it: the energy check (across a cycle), runs/2026-10-30-ring-launch/case_ramp.txt.
- A section matched by name made a plain ring a band; the band model refused it for having no span, and it fell to a stock impulse with no notice on screen: seven test tables, a ball's rise 23 mm against 3.4 mm, found 2026-10-31 only by asking what a rise was. A refusal to model something is a route to stock. Catches it: the 'stock collision' log line; none yet that fails a run on it (to-do 1.7: 13 routes remain).
- A sliding contact's spring set the step though its stiffness is not in the motion: a ring hit took 97 steps where 24 do, and the skid after it 1686 where 300 do, 2026-10-31. Then the same rule on an impact took a steel wall hit from +1.0 % to +7.4 % of its reference, because the shear's short step was also resolving the normal hit: a step rule can be doing a second job. Catches it: what-set-each-step trace plus gs_test's exits at the rule (runs/2026-10-31-ring-cost: trace1.py, suite.py C).
- Stock's ball-ball collision moved a ball by its own last collision record, not the pair's overlap: here the Drain kicker's -8 U, capped at 2.5 U, 1.2 mm into a wall between cycles, 31 J, out at 12 m/s; which ball moved depended on heap addresses, so runs did not repeat, 2026-10-31. Catches it: the energy check (between cycles), two runs of a drive byte-identical (bench).
- A step rule that read a hit's rebound half as resting: closing = max(0, closing speed) is 0 once the balls recede, so a cycle starting there took a step 4 times too long and the pair rebounded up to 1.14, 2026-10-31. Catches it: the energy check (across a cycle), runs/2026-10-31-pile-energy/bench/pr_rule.cpp (cycle boundary swept across a hit).
- A closed energy book proves nothing about a state update whose change is booked as a loss: a cord letting go of a ball changed its length 0.35 mm and lost 10 mJ with no force, book at 0.000 uJ, 2026-10-31. Catches it: V after each state update against V before at the same position (runs/2026-10-31-cord-rethink/today/p_log.py).
- A contact could pull: fN = fe + fd with no clamp, damping past the spring at the end of a rebound; a wall set to 0.40 rebounded 0.26 at 3 m/s, 2026-10-31. Energy stayed exact, so no energy test saw it. Catches it: rebound against the law's own integral (runs/2026-10-31-static-group/gs_e.cpp).
- A rate test reads a hit's turnaround as steady, and an energy test reads a static load as a hit, 2026-10-31. Catches it: real hits replayed against 1 us (run_replay.py).
- A ball rests exactly where the push direction is ambiguous: a fold of one surface has no root at all for an implicit step, 2026-10-31. Stress tables don't rest balls there. Catches it: a grid of balls left to come to rest (mk_grid.py).
- The energy step's closure kept the energy and moved the ball 4.3 m, 2026-10-31. Catches it: a position limit in the step; the "no ball outside" check.
- A depth rise blamed on a missed contact was a stock part moving the ball: a trigger's Collide (collideex.cpp:748) adds STATICTIME * vel at hit time 0, 11 times in one cycle, 0.457 U, 35 um into the playfield, 2026-10-31. Inferred first, and wrong. Catches it: the zero rule's report by cause, then a trace.
- An exact-state check that compares speed never holds: stock adds gravity between cycles, so the ball moved by its float rounding every cycle (up to 0.13 um at y = 2000 U) and read as a fault, 2026-10-31. Catches it: the zero rule's report (nm lines on a resting ball).
- State kept between cycles outside the ball is outside its snapshot: the self-test failed on p3 with 6 engaged balls once the exact state moved to a map, 2026-10-31. Catches it: the snapshot self-test (5b).
- A byte difference is not a logic difference: removing fixed arrays changed the last digits under /fp:fast, and carried exact state amplifies them to another game, 2026-10-31. Catches it: compile both trees at /fp:precise; identical bytes prove no logic changed (bench_build.py, BENCH_FP).
- A private build linked to older objects: a new virtual crashes at the first gate; a stale object gives old results with no error (the first stock-counter build returned an earlier stage's bytes), 2026-10-31. Catches it: closure.py for the file list; compare a row's bytes to the reference before reading a result.
- One shot's result depends on the shots before it in the same run: the same exe gave S006 605 U apart after 1.8 s with the earlier shots removed, and a ball moved 24.5 U in its first ms where the full run moved it 0 - 1 U, 2026-10-31. A difference between two builds on one shot can be that, not the law. Catches it: the same shot at a fixed 1 us in the same drive, and the shot alone (runs/2026-10-31-static-group: four_read.py, close.py).
- A shared file changed and merged with no run on one of its users: fixed arrays became grown ones, one cord path wrote past them, any table with a cord crashed at 3 s; the lead's "no band tests while the band is in flux" had dropped even a run to see that it starts, 2026-10-31. Catches it: one smoke run per user of a changed shared file before a merge, even a model not being judged; direct writes no longer compile.
- A fixed surface inherited a moving one's columns: hits are kept and reused between cycles, a fixed surface writes no columns, so the count stayed and ChooseStep indexed part -1: a crash, 2026-10-31. Byte comparisons can't see it: no moving part runs on those rows. Catches it: a test part through the engine path (runs/2026-10-31-interface-step/mk_wired.py), not only offline.
- A part's depth compared with an end depth nobody sets for parts: the zero rule read SurfaceContact::endDepth, 0 for a moving part, so the part's whole squash went into the zero every cycle ("deeper with no step"), 2026-10-31. The per-cycle book closes anyway. Catches it: the same test part; the zero rule's DepthFault lines. Any rule that reads kept state: ask who sets it for every kind of surface.
- A query that keeps state, called in the wrong order: StopExcess(zero) after StopExcess(x) wipes what a part needs for its pin, 2026-10-31. Found on paper. Catches it: mapping each private copy onto an interface before calling it done.
- A claim of "just arithmetic every part writes by hand" was wrong for one part: the cord's plate has its own tolerance and stays out of the balls' move, 2026-10-31. Catches it: same bytes after moving code into a shared rule.
- A constraint decided at the end of a step, after a switch: a coil let go with the link taut stayed on its stop for one 170 us step, took 67 uJ of 211, and a 10 ms tap gave no shot, 2026-10-31. Catches it: a cradle tap at the rule against 1 us.
- A group's limit on surfaces dropped the ones past it: six balls, ball through the playfield, 2026-10-31 (the limit is gone, GroupStepDesign 17.6). Catches it: driver limit 4, the note on screen.
- Energy that leaves between cycles booked in a step: a book error with no gain, 2026-10-31. Catches it: E0 against the last E1 in the book file.
- A slope taken over a move near rounding: a chord (V1 - V0) / move at 1e-9 rad and a gradient at tolerance 1e-17 m carried noise, the solve never settled, a shorter step fell to the clean branch and steps went under 2 us, 2026-11-01. Catches it: same bytes at a 2 us floor and a 10 ns floor.
- A kept probe reused a field its next shape did not write: a wall took a gate's bend, 0.4 mJ then 1188 mJ, launch at 165 m/s, 2026-10-31. Only the real bench showed it: the offline bench never changes shapes between cycles. Catches it: real bench limit 3, the group's book.
- Viscoelastic damping pulls at the end of every contact (sqrt(d) against d^1.5). On a light part that is a throw: a gate went to 56 deg after a blocked hit, 2026-10-31. Catches it: peak swing against 1 us in the step sweep.
- A noise stop kept the move after the point it had judged, a false "solved": 0.45 uJ, 2026-10-31. Catches it: the book at the rule.
- A zero-length stop step: starting from rest with friction acting, the stop test took the start for the speed's zero, so the search ended there every step: 2 million steps at one time while a gate settled on its stop, 2026-11-02. Five shots passed; a slower one found it. Catches it: the offline run past settling, step count.
Each trap names the test that now catches it ("none yet" until one exists); the older entries above get theirs as they are reviewed.

------------------------
a. The to-do list is a short list of OPEN items, one line each, with a link to detail. History goes to an archive file, not the list.
b. A new trap goes into section 6 the day it is found.
c. A memory that names a file, branch or flag is checked when it is read; a stale one is fixed at once.
d. Every session reads this file first; CLAUDE.md points to it.

9. Rules for one model only
---------------------------
Magnets (user, 2026-10-18; moved here from CLAUDE.md 2026-10-27):
- Do not generalize the magnet physics. It is too complex: state results for the case measured only.
- Off-axis data for magnet decisions. On-axis is the least likely pinball case; label on-axis results as such and never base a decision on them alone.
