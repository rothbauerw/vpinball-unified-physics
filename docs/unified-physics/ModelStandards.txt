MODEL STANDARDS - how every physics model is built  (approved by the user section by section, 2026-10-27)
=============================================================================================
Read with docs/unified-physics/DevelopmentRules.txt (the rules there come first). This file says HOW a model is built so that each new one reuses the last instead of relearning.
A model is done when it meets sections 1 - 5 and DevelopmentRules 3, 5 and 10. The report lists each point with how it was checked: measured, or only reasoned (INFERRED).


1. Before writing anything
--------------------------
a. First write what the model must do (requirements), apart from how it's modelled, and review them with the user one at a time
   They live in docs/unified-physics/Requirements.txt, one section per model, agreed items only; a draft stays in its run folder until agreed.
b. Then name the closest existing model and read its files:
      moving part with its own bodies  -> jet bumper (bumpergroup.h, contactbumperpart.h) and gate/spinner (hingegroup.h, contacthingepart.h): the group step path (2a)
      static surface                   -> contactsurface.h, hitrubber.cpp (hard surfaces, ramps)
      ball contact law                 -> rubbercontact.h ContactCore (Hertz + damping + friction + twist)
c. Read section 6 (known traps) of this file.
d. Then write the design in docs/unified-physics/<Model>Design.txt: the physical laws, the bodies and their stiffest internal contact, the cost estimate, what it breaks (rule 7),
   and every way energy enters the model (coil, spring preload, motor), showing that only those add energy (2h). Review it with the user section by section, and ask before engine code.
e. Keep a simple model simple: time every piece you add, and keep it only if it measurably improves the result.
f. Don't take a stock VPX property into a model because it exists; use it only if the physics needs it.


2. The contract every model meets
---------------------------------
a. One interface. A moving part describes itself and the engine steps it with the balls: it implements GroupStep::Part (groupstep.h: its coordinates, stored energy
   and forces, step facts, stops, loads, Accept, SaveState / RestoreState) and ContactMovingPart's GroupPart, GroupSurfaces, GroupProbe, GroupLaw, TouchBegan, Probe,
   SurfaceSpeedBound, Origin, Reach and SaveState / RestoreState. Patterns: bumpergroup.h, hingegroup.h, batgroup.h, cordfixed.h, contactdiscpart.h.
   No private stepping path, no shapes of its own, no special case for the model inside contactsystem.cpp. Until goal 1 (to-do) deletes the old loop, ContactItem, BallStep,
   StepInIsland and AdvanceAlone stay in the interface with no logic of their own (AdvanceAlone calls GroupStep::AdvanceAlone).
b. One path. Balls touching the model are stepped by the group step, the one shared path. It gives every part the same guards:
      step length      the shared step rule (2c)
      failed solve     the step halves; past the floor, the energy step (to be removed: goal 2). Never a hand-back to stock; WARN and an on-screen notice (rule 8)
      stops            the stop search ends a step where a body reaches a hard stop (StopExcess / CrossingStops)
      energy check     ball energy plus every stored energy, each cycle (EnergyCheckStart / End); energy enters only through drives (2h)
      stock counter    a converted part that uses stock fails the driver run (stockcount.cpp); the part leaves AllowedStock the day it is converted
   A part that cannot go this way is not done: the missing piece is proposed to the user for the to-do.
c. One step rule. A part states facts about itself through StepFactList (a stiffness, an approach, a turn, a time left, a time constant, a field); the step
   length comes from the shared rule and its factors live in GroupStep::Settings. A private omega-h, share or step constant in a part is a fault.
d. One contact law. A ball touching anything uses ContactCore with the material's parameters. A new law only if the physics differs (name the law), and then in
   rubbercontact.h beside the others, shared.
   We do not rewrite physics we already have (user, 2026-10-30). A ball pressing a band onto a post, end post or stop post, IS a rubber on a post and uses the same solve as a ring. Between a post and the span a band acts as a span for a short distance, then as a rubber on a post. Band behaviour is judged against a rubber on a post in that region; the rubber-on-post rebound targets are not targets for free spans.
e. Pure SI maths with no VPX types in the model's header, so the same code runs in the offline test. VPX units only in the adapter.
f. Events go into the part's event list (or DeferEvent) and are fired after the step. No script runs inside the step.
g. Settings come from the table's physics ini through the settings reader. Environment variables are for diagnostics only, read once, and listed in
   TestingPlaybook.md. Duplicate or unknown keys raise a notification (rule 8).
   A new setting goes beside its neighbours (same ini section, struct and loader), reloads like them and is written to the log.
h. Energy enters only through named drives (coil, motor, spring preload, gravity, nudge). The model reports their work so the energy books close every cycle.


3. Stiff things
---------------
a. The stiffest contact sets the step for the whole island. For every internal contact (stops, pins, switch blades) ask first: does the motion during the contact matter?
   If not, use the impact law: restitution from the same Hertz + damping law, then a resting constraint, no stiff spring. (Proven on the bumper ring stops 2026-10-27.)
   The bounce can never return more energy than came in.
b. A part with a ball on it steps with the group; a part alone steps on its own (AdvanceAlone), with fine steps only near a stop. The engine chooses the step sizes, not the part.
c. A part with nothing driving it and nothing touching it sleeps, and SurfaceSpeedBound still covers what can wake it.
d. Steep fields count as stiff too: a force that rises sharply over a short distance (e.g. a magnet's pull near the pole) sets the step like a stiff contact.


4. Cost
-------
a. Limits: DevelopmentRules rule 5 (the one cost rule: per object and per frame).
b. State an object's cost with 1, 2 and 6 balls.
c. Measure with the test driver: report.txt and cycles.csv give the worst 7-cycle window and worst cycle, split by part (TestingPlaybook section 5); VPX_CONTACT_PROFILE finds where the time goes. Report the worst window, p99 and maximum.
   Profile before optimising: a guess at the cost is not a finding.
d. New step code: no allocation, no string building, no getenv, no dynamic_cast in loops. Existing allocations are tidied when that code is next touched (user,
   2026-10-27). Material constants computed once at setup.
e. Every model runs on the stress table before it is called done.


5. Tests
--------
a. Offline: the model alone in runs/<date>-<model>/ against a 1 us fixed-step reference: the outputs that matter, plus the energy balance (work in - losses - stored).
   Sweep the step size and report where it breaks. At every step size the energy may only fall unless a drive adds it: a rise at any step size is a fail.
b. In the engine, the checks of DevelopmentRules 3, bench first: the accuracy bench, the part's stress set (rule 10), cost in a focused driver run
   (until the performance tests, Now item 10, exist), and the snapshot self-test (VPX_SNAPSHOT_TEST=1) with 0 differences.
c. Play-speed cases: 1 m/s typical, 3 m/s extreme; two balls on the part; a ball between two parts; table restart; the part switched off.
   Plus the cases that have launched balls before: a contact found late, a ball pinched between two moving parts, a part hit while its coil drives it.
d. Use the real bench and run.ps1 with the test driver (TestingPlaybook), not the old batch_*.ps1 scripts. A new script joins the shared set with a one-line
   description, not left as a one-off.
e. Every test run writes its pass/fail report to disk the day it runs.


6. Known traps (each has cost at least one session)
---------------------------------------------------
- Stiff stop springs set the step: bumper, 85 % of the step time.
- The acceleration bound left out the part's own drive: overlap at first sight.
- A destroyed part kept balls pointing at it: crash at play end (ReleaseBat).
- A host object left in the registry across table restarts: turntable slowdown.
- A duplicate ini key silently won: magnet ball_interaction.
- Long steps create energy: bumper replay at a 100 us step launches at 10.6 m/s vs about 0.5 m/s at 1 us. Catches it: 5a step sweep (energy may not rise).
- A fix went into one copy of a copied rule (parts use a fixed 9.81 m/s2, the core uses gravity plus nudge). Catches it: none yet (2c removes the copies).
- Comment and code disagree (hitrubber.h:57 says rest factor 0.2, hitrubber.cpp sets 0.8). Catches it: none yet.
- A matrix run left no report and was missed. Catches it: 5e.
- A new model kept a route to stock: a failed solve on the cord band set a stock cooldown, ball 1.04 -> 6.15 m/s in play while the offline proof stayed exact. Catches it: none yet.
- A solver's first guess kept between steps: a step taken again from a snapshot differed in the last digits (117 of 2001 steps). Catches it: the snapshot self-test (5b).
- A stretch re-seated at the friction limit with the damper's share in it, which charges the spring with work nobody did: the old loop's ring gains at least 21.9 mJ in a 3 m/s hit (bench, 1 us), a still bat face leaves with 32 % more energy at 6 m/s. First blamed on the spring stiffening with the squash (0.6 mJ at most at 1 m/s, also fixed): measured by switching each off, runs/2026-10-30-rubber-review. Catches it: an energy book that counts what the springs hold, 5a.
- A contact law for a thick solid body used on a thin tube: full Mindlin stiffness gives too much grip, whatever E or mu is (tangential to normal frequency 1.53 for nu 0.5). Catches it: rolling rebound targets on the model, 5a.
- A seat along a tilted normal pressed the ball 1.95 mm into the playfield: 109 J stored, launch at 34 m/s. Catches it: the energy check (between cycles).
- A probe returns a surface's nearest face only: a ramp's wall was hidden by its floor, the rewinds ran out, launch at 69 m/s. Catches it: the energy check (across a cycle), runs/2026-10-30-ring-launch/case_ramp.txt.
- A section matched by name made a plain ring a band; the band model refused it for having no span, and it fell to a stock impulse with no notice on screen: seven test tables, a ball's rise 23 mm against 3.4 mm only by asking what a rise was. A refusal to model something is a route to stock. Catches it: the 'stock collision' log line; none yet that fails a run on it (to-do 1.7: 13 routes remain).
- A sliding contact's spring set the step though its stiffness is not in the motion: a ring hit took 97 steps where 24 do, and the skid after it 1686 where 300 do. Then the same rule on an impact took a steel wall hit from +1.0 % to +7.4 % of its reference, because the shear's short step was also resolving the normal hit: a step rule can be doing a second job. Catches it: what-set-each-step trace plus gs_test's exits at the rule (runs/2026-10-31-ring-cost: trace1.py, suite.py C).
- Stock's ball-ball collision moved a ball by its own last collision record, not the pair's overlap: here the Drain kicker's -8 U, capped at 2.5 U, 1.2 mm into a wall between cycles, 31 J, out at 12 m/s; which ball moved depended on heap addresses, so runs did not repeat. Catches it: the energy check (between cycles), two runs of a drive byte-identical (bench).
- A step rule that read a hit's rebound half as resting: closing = max(0, closing speed) is 0 once the balls recede, so a cycle starting there took a step 4 times too long and the pair rebounded up to 1.14. Catches it: the energy check (across a cycle), runs/2026-10-31-pile-energy/bench/pr_rule.cpp (cycle boundary swept across a hit).
- A closed energy book proves nothing about a state update whose change is booked as a loss: a cord letting go of a ball changed its length 0.35 mm and lost 10 mJ with no force, book at 0.000 uJ. Catches it: V after each state update against V before at the same position (runs/2026-10-31-cord-rethink/today/p_log.py).
- A contact could pull: fN = fe + fd with no clamp, damping past the spring at the end of a rebound; a wall set to 0.40 rebounded 0.26 at 3 m/s. Energy stayed exact, so no energy test saw it. Catches it: rebound against the law's own integral (runs/2026-10-31-static-group/gs_e.cpp).
- A rate test reads a hit's turnaround as steady, and an energy test reads a static load as a hit. Catches it: real hits replayed against 1 us (run_replay.py).
- A ball rests exactly where the push direction is ambiguous: a fold of one surface has no root at all for an implicit step. Stress tables don't rest balls there. Catches it: a grid of balls left to come to rest (mk_grid.py).
- The energy step's closure kept the energy and moved the ball 4.3 m. Catches it: a position limit in the step; the "no ball outside" check.
- A depth rise blamed on a missed contact was a stock part moving the ball: a trigger's Collide (collideex.cpp:748) adds STATICTIME * vel at hit time 0, 11 times in one cycle, 0.457 U, 35 um into the playfield. Inferred first, and wrong. Catches it: the zero rule's report by cause, then a trace.
- An exact-state check that compares speed never holds: stock adds gravity between cycles, so the ball moved by its float rounding every cycle (up to 0.13 um at y = 2000 U) and read as a fault. Catches it: the zero rule's report (nm lines on a resting ball).
- State kept between cycles outside the ball is outside its snapshot: the self-test failed on p3 with 6 engaged balls once the exact state moved to a map. Catches it: the snapshot self-test (5b).
- A byte difference is not a logic difference: removing fixed arrays changed the last digits under /fp:fast, and carried exact state amplifies them to another game. Catches it: compile both trees at /fp:precise; identical bytes prove no logic changed (bench_build.py, BENCH_FP).
- One shot's result depends on the shots before it in the same run: the same exe gave S006 605 U apart after 1.8 s with the earlier shots removed, and a ball moved 24.5 U in its first ms where the full run moved it 0 - 1 U. A difference between two builds on one shot can be that, not the law. Catches it: the same shot at a fixed 1 us in the same drive, and the shot alone (runs/2026-10-31-static-group: four_read.py, close.py).
- A shared file changed and merged with no run on one of its users: fixed arrays became grown ones, one cord path wrote past them, any table with a cord crashed at 3 s; the lead's "no band tests while the band is in flux" had dropped even a run to see that it starts. Catches it: one smoke run per user of a changed shared file before a merge, even a model not being judged; direct writes no longer compile.
- A fixed surface inherited a moving one's columns: hits are kept and reused between cycles, a fixed surface writes no columns, so the count stayed and ChooseStep indexed part -1: a crash. Byte comparisons can't see it: no moving part runs on those rows. Catches it: a test part through the engine path (runs/2026-10-31-interface-step/mk_wired.py), not only offline.
- A part's depth compared with an end depth nobody sets for parts: the zero rule read SurfaceContact::endDepth, 0 for a moving part, so the part's whole squash went into the zero every cycle ("deeper with no step"). The per-cycle book closes anyway. Catches it: the same test part; the zero rule's DepthFault lines. Any rule that reads kept state: ask who sets it for every kind of surface.
- A query that keeps state, called in the wrong order: StopExcess(zero) after StopExcess(x) wipes what a part needs for its pin. Found on paper. Catches it: mapping each private copy onto an interface before calling it done.
- A claim of "just arithmetic every part writes by hand" was wrong for one part: the cord's plate has its own tolerance and stays out of the balls' move. Catches it: same bytes after moving code into a shared rule.
- A constraint decided at the end of a step, after a switch: a coil let go with the link taut stayed on its stop for one 170 us step, took 67 uJ of 211, and a 10 ms tap gave no shot. Catches it: a cradle tap at the rule against 1 us.
- A group's limit on surfaces dropped the ones past it: six balls, ball through the playfield (the limit is gone, GroupStepDesign 17.6). Catches it: driver limit 4, the note on screen.
- Energy that leaves between cycles booked in a step: a book error with no gain. Catches it: E0 against the last E1 in the book file.
- A slope taken over a move near rounding: a chord (V1 - V0) / move at 1e-9 rad and a gradient at tolerance 1e-17 m carried noise, the solve never settled, a shorter step fell to the clean branch and steps went under 2 us. Catches it: same bytes at a 2 us floor and a 10 ns floor.
- A kept probe reused a field its next shape did not write: a wall took a gate's bend, 0.4 mJ then 1188 mJ, launch at 165 m/s. Only the real bench showed it: the offline bench never changes shapes between cycles. Catches it: real bench limit 3, the group's book.
- Viscoelastic damping pulls at the end of every contact (sqrt(d) against d^1.5). On a light part that is a throw: a gate went to 56 deg after a blocked hit. Catches it: peak swing against 1 us in the step sweep.
- A noise stop kept the move after the point it had judged, a false "solved": 0.45 uJ. Catches it: the book at the rule.
- A zero-length stop step: starting from rest with friction acting, the stop test took the start for the speed's zero, so the search ended there every step: 2 million steps at one time while a gate settled on its stop. Five shots passed; a slower one found it. Catches it: the offline run past settling, step count.
Each trap names the test that now catches it ("none yet" until one exists); the older entries above get theirs as they are reviewed.


7. Keeping what we learn
------------------------
a. The to-do (docs/unified-physics/TODO.md) holds OPEN items only, one line each; finished items are deleted and git history is the archive.
   Progress notes go in the lead log (CLAUDE.md). A hook checks the limits (.claude/hooks/check_todo.py).
b. A new trap goes into section 6 the day it is found.
c. A memory that names a file, branch or flag is checked when it is read; a stale one is fixed at once.
d. Every session reads this file first; CLAUDE.md points to it.


8. Rules for one model only
---------------------------
Magnets (user, 2026-10-18; moved here from CLAUDE.md 2026-10-27):
- Matching the FEM reference (e.g. within 10 %) is a target, not a limit; efficiency and behaviour come first.
- Do not generalize the magnet physics. It is too complex: state results for the case measured only.
- Off-axis data for magnet decisions. On-axis is the least likely pinball case; label on-axis results as such and never base a decision on them alone.
