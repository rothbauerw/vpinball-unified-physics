PLUNGER DESIGN (all sections AGREED with the user 2026-11-17; built in pieces, each measured)
=============================================================================================================
Requirements: Requirements.txt section H (agreed 2026-11-17). Hardware data: PartData.txt section 11. Rules: DevelopmentRules, ModelStandards.
Closest models (ModelStandards 1b; survey 2026-11-17): the rod is a one-axis slide on BodyPart (bodypart.h), as the Button cap is (buttoncap.h: linear spring, two
stops, ball contact through a revolved profile); the auto plunger's fork carriage is a second one-axis slide on BodyPart (no hinge, user 2026-11-17).
Units: mm, N, g, J unless stated. Values: MEASURED (user), DERIVED (lead, from measured), ESTIMATED (no data; labelled).


1. MECHANICAL PLUNGER: THE BODY AND ITS LAWS (AGREED 2026-11-17)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1a. One body. Rod, knob, tip, tip washer and clip move as one rigid body along the lane's axis: one coordinate s, the pull, 0 at rest, + away from the ball
    (pulled), - toward the ball (pushed in, palm smash). A BodyPart slide like the Button cap; the lane axis is the plunger's VPX direction in the table plane.
    Moving mass m = rod + knob + tip + washer + one third of the main spring (a spring's own mass moves with the rod as m/3 to first order):
    rod 110 (DERIVED: 3/8 in x 7-3/4 in steel), knob 10 (MEASURED typical; ball knobs 15 - 25, a setting), tip about 3 and washer + clip about 2 (ESTIMATED),
    spring 6.9 / 3 = 2.3 (DERIVED: 32.5 coils x pi x 11 mm x 0.785 mm2 x 7.85 g/cm3) -> m about 127 g.
1b. Main spring (the only drive on release; Requirements H2). Linear, F = k_m (x0 + s) toward the ball, from the spring data:
    k_m = G d^4 / (8 D^3 n), G 79.3 GPa (music wire), D = OD - d; x0 = free length - gap (preload compression); solid length = (active + closed coils) x d.
    Travel = gap - solid. Defaults (brown spring, gap 93.3): k_m 0.30 N/mm, x0 59.1, solid 32.5, travel 60.8, preload 17.7 N, full pull 36.0 N.
    Stored energy V_m(s) = k_m (x0 + s)^2 / 2: exact for a linear spring, so the energy book closes by construction (ModelStandards 2h).
1c. Full-pull stop: the main spring going solid at s = travel (Requirements H2; the user's plunger has no other stop). A steel-on-steel stop: BodyStop, the Hertz
    stop with viscoelastic restitution (ModelStandards 3a: impact law, no stiff spring). Coil-bind restitution ESTIMATED (as gates' steel stops) until measured.
1d. Outer spring (rest and return; Requirements H4). Between the knob and the housing, it pushes the knob back (s +) only while touching: F_o = k_o (c0 - s) for
    s < c0, 0 beyond. k_o about 9.4 N/mm (MEASURED: about 20 lbf at 0.375 in; linear ASSUMED). Rest s = 0 is where the two springs balance:
    k_m x0 = k_o c0 -> c0 = 17.7 / 9.4 = 1.9 mm (the user saw it hardly compressed: consistent).
    Its own solid length ends its travel: a second Hertz steel stop where it goes solid, s = -(19.05 - 8.5 - c0) = -8.6 mm (MEASURED geometry: 5 coils of
    1.7 mm wire, OD 14 - 15 mm; the formula's 9.9 N/mm at 4 active coils agrees with the push measurement). From rest to solid it stores at most about 0.5 J.
    Paper check, empty release from full pull: the spring gives 1.6 J -> rod about 5.0 m/s at rest; the outer spring would need c = v sqrt(m / k_o) = 18 mm to
    stop it, more than its 19 mm free length allows once its solid length is counted: an empty full release BOTTOMS the outer spring. So its solid stop is
    part of the model, not an edge case.
    MEASURED (user 2026-11-17, phone slow-motion video, simulator plunger, blue spring, empty full-pull release): the outer spring ends nearly or fully closed.
    Paper for that case: spring energy over the pull about 0.59 J plus about 0.07 J of main-spring push past rest; the outer spring holds about 0.52 J when
    closed: so the losses on the way are about 0 - 25 % (the video cannot say more). The user's sliding-sound question is answered: friction and rattle take a
    modest share, not most of the energy. A slow return (pull 10 mm, let go) reaches rest fully (user): Coulomb friction below the 7 N spring force near rest.
1e. Gravity along the lane, with the cabinet's acceleration (as every part): m g sin(slope) about 0.14 N toward the player at 6.5 deg: small against 17.7 N.
1f. Losses on the rod: (i) Coulomb friction in the housing, a FrictionLimit as gates have, side load = the rod's weight plus the sideways part of the tip's
    contact force, mu ESTIMATED 0.2; (ii) a viscous drag F = -c v for the rod and spring rubbing and rattling in the sleeve at speed (the sound the user hears),
    c chosen so the simulator's empty full release loses about 15 % before the outer spring (the middle of the measured 0 - 25 %), labelled DERIVED from the
    slow-motion observation, to be refined when a release is measured more closely. Both are losses only (no energy enters).
1g. The tip: a hemisphere 9.5 mm across on the rod's end (MEASURED), ring rubber (rubber ring material values: rubber_e_mpa 12 MPa, as rings), meeting the ball
    through the shared contact law (ContactCore) as a revolved profile carried by the slide, as the Button cap's top. Height on the ball: the tip's axis at the
    rod's height in the lane (from the VPX plunger's Z / Height; to settle in section 3).
1h. Paper check of a full launch (ball resting at the wall at s = 0, default plunger): the rod reaches the ball at about 5.0 m/s with 127 g against the ball's
    80.6 g; for a rubber restitution e 0.7 - 0.8 the ball leaves at (1 + e) m / (m + m_ball) x 5.0 = 5.2 - 5.5 m/s, before rolling losses (DERIVED; the
    contact law decides it in the model, this is the order to expect). A softer spring or a shorter gap scales it as the square root of the spring energy.
1i. Stiffest internal contact and steps (ModelStandards 3): the outer spring (9.4 N/mm on 127 g: period 23 ms) and the main spring (period 130 ms) are soft;
    the stiff ones are the two steel stops (impact law) and the rubber tip on the ball (the shared law's own clock). No new stiff spring.
1j. Not modelled (stated): spring surge (waves along the coils), the knob's spin, the rod bending, sound.


2. PLAYER INPUT: THE HAND (AGREED 2026-11-17; Requirements H3)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
2a. The hand is two one-sided moving stops on the rod (BodyPart's driven stop, DriveStop: a stop that moves at a set rate toward a target, carrying a body that
    rests on it; its work is booked as a drive, bodypart.h). A HOLD stop in front of the knob: it can only pull (stops the rod moving toward the ball past it).
    A PUSH stop behind the knob: it can only push (palm smash). Neither touches the rod unless the rod presses on it, so a hand never pulls and pushes at once.
    Energy enters through the hand only as the work of these stops (a named drive, as nudge; Requirements H8).
2b. Key or button (H3a): key down: the hold stop starts at the rod's position and moves back at the pull speed to full pull; the spring presses the rod onto
    it, so the rod follows. Key up: the hold stop is removed: the spring alone drives the rod (no launch rule). Pull speed: stock's PullSpeed has no physical
    unit, so a new key pull_mm_s, default 120 mm/s (ESTIMATED: a hand pulling the default 61 mm in about half a second); a setting per plunger.
2c. Cabinet sensor (H3b): the sensor's position maps to s linearly: its rest reading to s = 0, its full-pull reading to s = travel (VPX's plunger
    calibration), forward of rest to s < 0 on the same scale. Each sample moves the hold stop (s > 0) or the push stop (s < 0) toward the new reading at the
    speed between the last two samples, so the hand moves smoothly between samples, not in jumps.
2d. Release (H3b: after a release the model's spring drives the rod, the sensor's samples do not). Players do not calibrate and cabinet plungers differ, so the
    rule uses only what holds for every plunger (user 2026-11-17):
    - Only a reading BEHIND rest (pulled) can start a release. A sample counts as a release when the knob moves toward rest faster than 0.5 m/s: between a
      hand-guided return (rarely over about 0.3 m/s) and a released knob (about 0.7 m/s from a 5 mm pull on the weakest spring measured, the user's blue one;
      about 3 m/s from a full pull; stiffer springs faster). The hold stop also drops at once when the sensor reads ahead of the model's rod (the rod has left
      the hand). A sensor that reports speed (e.g. Pinscape) uses its own speed in the same test.
    - The test decides only WHETHER the knob was released; the launch comes from the table's model spring (2b, 1b), so a release seen one sample late costs
      about 10 ms of delay, not launch energy. Limit: a pull under about 2 mm may not count as a release.
    - After a release the hand stays off, whatever the samples say (the real knob bouncing past rest and back is not a grab), and re-arms when both the
      model rod is at rest (the engine's own rest test, the one that lets parts sleep) and the sensor reads rest within VPX's plunger dead zone (user's rule).
      Limit: a player who grabs the real knob while it still bounces is ignored until the rod settles (a fraction of a second).
    Stock (hitplunger.cpp, PlungerHandler.cpp, read 2026-11-17): a release when the reading drops 10 % of the travel below its furthest point in the last
    100 ms, launch speed = a fixed factor (100/13) x the pull, re-sync after a fixed timer even if the plunger is still moving. Ours replaces all three.
2e. Palm smash (H3d): a reading forward of rest is always the hand pushing, never a release, however fast. It moves the push stop forward against the outer spring; the push stop drives the rod, and the tip meets the ball
    through the contact law at the rod's speed. When the reading comes back to rest or behind, the push stop is removed. A smash is as fast as the sensor
    samples it (stated limit, H3d).
2f. Partial pulls (H3c) need nothing extra: the rod starts from where the hand let go, with that pull's spring energy.
2g. Script (H7): PullBack = key down (2b), Fire = key up, PullBackandRetract as stock's (pull, then return slowly at the pull speed with the hold stop), Position
    reads s on stock's scale. LimitBOS / LimitEOS when the rod reaches rest / the full-pull stop.
2h. A cabinet plunger on an auto-plunger table (kept from stock, user 2026-11-17): the mechanical plunger is not simulated; a pull-and-release (the 2d release
    test) or pressing the knob in past rest (a gentle palm smash, held in) acts as the Launch Ball button: key down on the release or the press, key up about
    100 ms after a release (as stock) or when the knob returns to rest after a press. Input handling, not physics.


3. THE TIP AND THE BALL (AGREED 2026-11-17, 3b changed by the user; Requirements H5)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
3a. Shape: the rubber tip as a solid of revolution about the rod's axis: a hemisphere of radius 4.76 mm (3/8 in across, MEASURED) on a short cylinder of the
    same radius back to the washer, carried by the slide (a revolved profile, as the Button cap's top, but about the rod's axis, which lies along the lane).
    The cylinder matters: see 3d.
3b. Placement: the author places the plunger, height and all (user 2026-11-17): the rod's axis, its rest position and direction are the VPX plunger's own
    placement (position, surface, height properties), as drawn, so drawn = touched. Nothing is derived from the lane. (For reference: in the lane groove a
    resting ball's centre is about 8.7 mm above the playfield top.)
3c. Law: the shared contact law (ContactCore) with the ring rubber's material (rubber_e_mpa 12 MPa and the rings' damping and friction), the ball as for every
    rubber. One contact; the ball pressed into it by the rod or rolling back into it, the same law (H5b). No launch rule.
3d. Paper check of a full launch (default plunger, rod 127 g at about 5 m/s on a resting ball, Hertz ball on rubber hemisphere, E* about 16 MPa, R* 3.5 mm):
    peak squeeze about 3.8 mm, peak force about 300 N, contact about 2.3 ms (DERIVED). 3.8 mm is close to the tip's 4.76 mm radius, past where a small-squeeze
    Hertz law holds, so the cylinder behind the hemisphere has to take part (the contact widens and stiffens), and the hit is to be checked against the 1 us
    reference in the offline test (ModelStandards 5a). A softer spring squeezes less (about 2.3 mm at 2.7 m/s, the simulator's).
3e. At rest: the lane-end wall (the table's own) holds the ball; the tip at rest touches it lightly or stands just off it, as the table places them. A ball at
    rest against the tip is a resting contact (no jitter: Requirements H5a; ModelStandards 3a resting constraint).
3f. Two balls: tip on ball, ball on ball (the existing ball-pair contact). The lane groove and walls unchanged (H5d).


4. AUTO PLUNGER (AGREED 2026-11-17, straight slide, no hinge; Requirements H6)
--------------------------------------------------------------
4a. One body sliding straight along the lane, horizontal, no hinge (user 2026-11-17: a straight drive for now, not the real crank's swing): the carriage
    with its two steel forks, a one-axis slide on BodyPart like the rod (section 1). Placed by the author, as the mechanical plunger (its VPX plunger
    object's placement, Auto Plunger set). A table may have both in one lane.
4b. Forks (user's estimates, PartData 11): two steel strips, one each side of the mechanical tip, 20 mm apart inside (3/4 of the ball), meeting the ball about
    18 mm above the playfield (2/3 up). Steel faces carried by the slide, with the steel contact law. Paper: on a ball centred 13.5 mm up, each fork's push points
    about 10 mm sideways, 4.5 mm down and 7.9 mm along the lane: the two sideways parts cancel (the cup holds the ball centred), the downward part keeps it on
    the lane floor (no hop), and 58 % of each push drives it up the lane (DERIVED).
4c. Stroke: about one ball diameter (27 mm) from first contact (user's estimate), a setting. Stops: rest (the return spring holds it there) and end of stroke
    (the coil's plunger bottoming): BodyStop, Hertz steel, impact law.
4d. Drive, stand-in for the coil (H6b): on Fire a constant force pushes the carriage from rest to the end stop and then switches off: its energy is exactly
    force x stroke = the table's auto_energy_j (ESTIMATED default 1.0 J: about a strong mechanical shot; tuned for feel until a coil model replaces it).
    BodyPart's DriveForce (as the turntable motor uses it), booked as a drive.
4e. Return: a light return spring to the rest stop (ESTIMATED); the ball, already gone, is not touched on the way back unless it rolls back into the lane.
4f. Moving mass ESTIMATED: carriage and forks about 50 g plus the coil's plunger (about 20 g) and linkage: about 80 g (a setting). With 1.0 J
    over 27 mm the forks reach about 4 - 5 m/s; the ball leaves at roughly 4 - 6 m/s (DERIVED order; the contact law decides it).
4g. Data the user does not have (all labelled ESTIMATED, settings): mass, return spring, energy. Later, if wanted: the real crank's swing and a coil model.


5. ENERGY AND COST (AGREED 2026-11-17; Requirements H8, H9; ModelStandards 2h, 4)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. Every way energy enters, and that only these add it (ModelStandards 2h):
    - the main spring and the outer spring: exact stored energies (linear: V = k x^2 / 2), so they return only what they took;
    - the hand: the work of the hold and push stops (2a), booked as a drive by the driven-stop code that exists (bodypart.h), as nudge is a named drive;
    - the auto plunger's stand-in drive: force x distance moved, booked through DriveForce while it acts, and off at the end stop (exactly force x stroke);
    - gravity along the lane with the cabinet's acceleration, as every part.
    Losses only: Coulomb friction and the viscous drag (1f), the stops' impact law (restitution from the Hertz law, never more out than in: ModelStandards
    3a), the tip's and forks' contact damping. The group's energy book (ball + parts + stored) closes every cycle; any rise is a fault.
5b. Cost estimate (to be measured, DevelopmentRules 5: 30 us in each 1 ms cycle with a ball on it, any 7 cycles under 3 ms):
    - plunger alone (no ball near, at rest): asleep, nothing (the engine's rest test, 2d);
    - pulling with a ball at the wall and not touching: the rod steps alone (one coordinate, soft springs): a few us;
    - ball resting on the tip: a resting contact; both asleep once still;
    - the launch: the tip's rubber contact lasts about 2.3 ms (3d) and sets short steps for 2 - 3 cycles: the cycles most at risk of the 30 us limit. The rod is
      heavy (127 g), not a light part like the 1 g Button cap whose contact costs 54 - 271 us (to-do 11.1), so its clock is the contact's, not the part's;
      INFERRED within the limit, to be measured.
5c. How it is measured: the offline test against the 1 us reference first (launch speed, energy book, step sweep: ModelStandards 5a), then a focused driver run
    on a table with a plunger (worst cycle, worst 7-cycle window, p99), per DevelopmentRules 3 and 5.


6. WHAT BREAKS (AGREED 2026-11-17; DevelopmentRules 7). Each case: how the design handles it, and how it is checked (M = measured in the tests,
   R = reasoned only, INFERRED until measured).
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6a. Bad settings: a gap shorter than the spring's solid length (no travel), a zero or negative rate, mass or stroke: refused at load with an on-screen notice
    naming the key (rule 8); the plunger stays at rest and pushes nothing; never handed to stock. R, then one load per case (M).
6b. A ball placed inside the tip or forks at start (rule 10b): judged on recovery: no crash, no energy gained. Stress set (M).
6c. Ball rolling back fast into a resting or moving tip (e.g. a weak launch falling back): the same contact (3c); the outer spring takes the rod's share. M.
6d. Two or more balls in the lane: tip on ball, ball on ball (3f). M.
6e. Mechanical and auto plunger in one lane: they never touch each other (forks straddle the tip; parts do not collide with parts); a ball between the tip
    and the forks meets both through the contact law. The author's placement decides whether they overlap. R.
6f. Fire or PullBack from the script while the player holds the cabinet plunger: the script's key acts as the hand (2b); the sensor's hand is ignored while a
    script pull is active. Fire with no pull: nothing to release, no launch. R.
6g. Sensor unplugged, noisy or drifting at rest: readings within VPX's dead zone are rest; a reading that jumps behind rest is a pull at most as fast as the
    hold stop moves (2c); the release test needs a real forward speed (2d). R, one input-replay test (M).
6h. Palm smash with no ball, or into a ball still rolling back: push stop and contact law, no special case. R.
6i. Nudge and tilt during a pull: the cabinet's acceleration acts on the rod and ball as on every part (1e). R.
6j. Table restart and teardown: no per-ball store kept past the play (ModelStandards trap: cleared in ContactSystem::EndTable); the plunger's part state
    rebuilt at load; the hand latch (2d) reset. M (two plays in one process).
6k. A plunger made invisible, or one not in a lane: still physical where the author placed it; the author's choice. R.
6l. Extremes: the stiffest spring listed (0.047 in wire) at full pull, an empty release (the outer spring bottoms: its stop takes it, 1d), a 25 g ball knob,
    auto_energy_j large: stress set at about twice the strongest real case (rule 10b). M.
6m. Limits written down, not handled: spring surge, the real auto crank's swing and coil (section 4), a sensor slower than about 10 ms per sample (2d, 2e).
