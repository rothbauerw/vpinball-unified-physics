3. THE DESIGN: A DRESSED DIPOLE
-------------------------------
Standard approach for magnetised particles: point dipoles plus a short-range two-body correction from the exact two-sphere solution. Three layers, all evaluated in every contact substep (like the coil force and the Hertz pair force):
 L1 MOMENTS. Every magnetic ball (Ball.Magnetic, not kicker-locked) is an induced dipole in its TOTAL local field: the coil field plus the other balls' fields. 3N unknowns, solved by a few sweeps, once per cycle (the
    moments change slowly); saturation by the same secant permeability as the multipole closure, mu(H) = 1 + Ms / (H + Ms/chi), M = 3 (mu-1)/(mu+2) H0, scalar fixed point per ball. The coil's field on an ISOLATED
    ball is thereby the same law as the cluster's (no jump when a ball joins a cluster).
 L2 TWO-BODY CORRECTION ENERGY. For linear spheres the interaction energy of a pair in a uniform field H is EXACTLY quadratic in H: U = -1/2 H.T(r).H with T = a(g) I + b(g) rhat rhat (the pair has rotational symmetry
    about its axis). So U(g, theta) = A(g) + B(g) cos^2(theta), theta = angle between the pair axis and the local field: TWO ONE-DIMENSIONAL CURVES of the gap g/R (dimensionless: any common radius works). The correction
    curves are those of the multipole law (L=10) minus those of the dipole law; they follow from two multipole runs per gap (axial and side by side): A' = -F_side-by-side, A'+B' = -F_axial, integrated inward from far away.
    The force on ball i is -grad_i of the summed pair energies, which includes the angular part (the tangential force, B(g)/r sin 2theta) and is conservative by construction (criterion 12).
    SATURATION is NOT in the table (start simple, user 2026-10-13): it enters through the moments of L1 (M <= Ms, secant mu); a saturation factor is added only if V2 shows the 10 % criterion is missed. The disc scene
    stays near-linear (mu 130-165, ~5 % effect), a pair at 0.05-0.15 T and gap <= 0.25 mm does not (linear overstates 1.2-5x: the notes of the closure work).
    The field used for each pair is the local field INCLUDING the other balls' contributions (M5: this cuts the 3-chain error from 13-30 % to 3-10 %). Planned remedy if the acceptance test needs it: use the neighbours'
    CORRECTED moments there (untested).
 L3 COIL ON A BALL. As today: the isolated-ball gradient force at the pole strength of the coil (5.3), per substep, with the L1 moment law.
Where it runs: the pair force joins PairForces of the contact system (lockstep, next to the Hertz force); L3 stays in Sources::Add. Balls of one magnetic pair that are not in the same island: the pair is within reach of
the island gather (GatherFree) when closer than the gap at which the correction is under the tolerance (4.2); beyond that the pure dipole force from the other ball's cycle-start position is used (smooth; the lag is
v dt over a distance of several mm). Balls the contact system does not own keep one force per cycle (as today; documented limit).
Cost (rule 5), MEASURED 2026-10-13 (runs/2026-10-13-dressed/bench.cpp; MSVC /O2 /arch:AVX2 /fp:fast, one core, SYNTHETIC curves - the cost does not depend on their values; compact cluster, all pairs within 3 R of gap):
      N                          2     3     4     6     8     12    16
      pair lookup, per substep   0.04  0.12  0.22  0.50  0.79  1.43  2.05  us
      L1 (field + moments)/cycle 0.2   0.3   0.4   0.6   0.9   1.4   2.1   us   (warm start 1 sweep; cold start 5-7 sweeps = a few us)
      cycle, 20 substeps         1.0   2.6   4.8   10.5  16.7  30.0  43.1  us
      cycle, 100 substeps        4.3   12.0  22.6  50.3  79.9  144   207   us
    Against the multipole law (112 / 204 / 303 / 537 us at N = 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 per EVALUATION): 20-100 times cheaper, and it runs in every substep. NOT in these numbers: the coil force per ball per substep (existing, 183 ns:
    ~15 us for 4 balls x 20 substeps), the engine integration (island gather, pair bookkeeping), the real number of substeps (the contact rules give it; 100 at rest contact), the real curves (no accuracy yet).
Step rules: aBound uses |F_mag| / m from the last substep (not the literal 60 m/s2); dRest includes the sustained magnet load along the contact normal (the Hertz depth the load presses to; the same law as gravity).

4. THE TABLE
------------
4.1 Two curves A(g), B(g) on gap/R (log spaced, from 0 up to where the correction is < 0.5 % of the dipole energy); no angle axis (exactly cos^2 theta in the linear regime), no field-level axis (linear, B^2 divided out).
    Overlap (Hertz depth, microns): extended with the slope at gap 0 (continuous; error < 1 % of 44 N over 13 um, ESTIMATE from M1).
4.2 Generation: ~20-40 multipole evaluations (axial and side by side at each gap, linear mu = 1 + chi); the energy by integrating the forces inward from far away (conservative by construction). Cost: tens of ms.
4.3 Distribution (user decision): the curves for the DEFAULT material are generated offline and SHIPPED with the engine (a small text table). They are rebuilt at table load, in memory (ESTIMATE tens of ms, no longer a
    concern), only if the ini sets a different chi/Ms; no cache file. The shipped file carries material and table version; a mismatch with the code triggers the rebuild and a playfield notification (rule 8).
4.4 Inherited accuracy: the multipole law is within ~5 % of FEM for pairs, 19 % mean / 38 % worst for the saturating held-out cases. The table can be no better. Its own interpolation error is to be shown <= 2 % against
    direct evaluations at random points.
4.5 One global ball material: [Ball] chi, ms_a_per_m in the unified ini replace the per-magnet ball_ms_a_per_m (which is read from the first magnet only); a key on another magnet raises the ignored-key notification.

5. WHAT COMES FROM OUTSIDE THE BALL LAW
---------------------------------------
5.1 Ball.Magnetic stays a per-ball switch (ceramic = off). Different ball radii on one surface are out of scope (user); a pair whose radii differ by more than a tolerance raises a notification and uses the dipole law.
5.2 Multi-body: see M5. The design is exact for isolated balls and for pairs, and approximate for a ball with several touching neighbours. Acceptance test decides whether the corrected-moment remedy is built.
5.3 DEFERRED (user 2026-10-13: "see how the simple model performs before we add more complexity"): the coil source stays as it is today (per-ball pole strength, existing plug coupling). Kept here as the plan for later:
    The coil's reaction to the balls (user decision: keep the engine's source SIMPLE). The engine keeps its few coil inputs. The assembly-specific physics (which parts are iron, brass or aluminium: bushing, sleeve,
    brackets, disc) is computed OFFLINE with the axisymmetric FEM of runs/2026-10-13-fem for the user's assembly, and gives (a) the pole strength against the centre ball's gap, (b) the field map for the outer balls.
    These replace the per-ball reluctance term and the x1.4 centre-ball coupling of the offline calibration (not in the engine). Pole strength becomes ONE value per coil per cycle from the permeances of all balls in reach,
    and the force follows from the energy with the coil current constant (adds the missing term of M6). Inputs still needed from the user: which of bushing / brackets / disc are magnetic (a magnet test), and the
    dimensions from the drawing. NO measurements of the plug exist; the video gives a cross-check only: the outer balls need an inward pull of ~1.5 N at 250 rpm and ~2.5 N at 325 rpm. Results stay marked UNVERIFIED
    against hardware. The FEM is axisymmetric: exact for the centre ball on the plug, the outer balls only feel the field. A plug smaller than the ball (15-20 mm against 27 mm) is not a flat plane: no half-space image shortcut.

11. R1 PROTOTYPE (2026-10-13; runs/2026-10-13-dressed/r1.cpp, output in r1_out.txt): FINITE-SIZE FACTOR OF THE ENGINE'S OWN COIL FIELD. R1 must work for ANY coil (user): the table is therefore built AT TABLE LOAD from each magnet's

 field, circuit term and ball saturation and multiplies its dipole force by rho (rho depends on the SHAPE of the field and on R/a, not on its strength, while the ball is unsaturated).

 CONVERGENCE [measured]: for a ball 2 mm or more above the face the ratio is converged in L (L=6, 10, 14, 18 agree to 3 digits). With the ball ON the face (z = R) a charge sits on the ball's surface, the multipole series
 does not converge (rho_z 2.39 / 2.41 / 2.45 / 2.59 at L = 6 / 10 / 14 / 18): the ratio there is ill-defined in the charge model. Proposal: tabulate from a gap of 0.5 mm (the engine's own gap floor in the circuit term) and
 hold the gap-0.5 mm row below it (continuous).
 NOT HANDLED YET (rule 7): (1) flanged return poles (returnDistMm > 0) break the axisymmetry: a 3-D table or a coarser per-axis scheme; (2) air-core coils use a |B|^2 table of current loops, not charges: the field expansion about

 the magnet's own (r, z) frame, rotate the force; (5) ball saturation inside the pole field (B > 0.57 T): rho from the linear law applies up to there, the engine's min(.., Ms) stays; (6) build cost at load (grid ~40 x 40 = 1600 single-ball
