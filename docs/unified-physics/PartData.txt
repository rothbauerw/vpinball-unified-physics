Given by the user 2026-10-26, to be used when work on flipper physics continues. Typical pinball hardware, not one specific machine unless stated.

- **Crank arm: 7/8 in (22.2 mm), not 1 in.** Any model or ini value that used 1 in is wrong.
- **Flipper: 2.4 in (60.96 mm) from pivot to the centre of the tip radius.**
- **Plunger (solenoid core) outer diameter: 5/16 in (0.3125 in, 7.94 mm)** typical. Cross-section 0.0767 in^2 = 4.95e-5 m^2. Standard raw steel plunger bar length 2.33 in (59.2 mm).
- **Nylon coil sleeve** the plunger slides in: inside diameter about 0.340 to 0.345 in (8.64 to 8.76 mm).
- **Bobbin inner diameter** (where the wire starts): 0.380 to 0.400 in (9.65 to 10.16 mm).
- **Standard 49.5 mm (1.95 in) flipper bobbin: winding length between the flanges 43.0 to 44.0 mm (1.70 to 1.73 in).**
- **Plunger material for calculations: AISI 1018 or AISI 12L14 low-carbon steel.**
- Reference the user gave for solenoid force equations: https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/solenoid-force-calculation-equations-and-design-guide

**Why:** the user wants these as the input numbers when flipper physics resumes.
**How to apply:** check current flipper/coil model values against this list first and report any that differ before changing them (rule 1: no edit without data). The 5/16 in plunger is typical; the Gorgar measured values in [[project-gorgar-return-spring-and-series-coils]] and [[reference-flipper-inertia-method]] stay as they are unless the user says otherwise. [from memory note reference_hold_coil_breakaway_force]
rothbauerw measured (2026-10-03) the force to break the plunger away from the coil stop: 5.75 lbf at 2 in from the flipper pivot, Fish Tales coil FL-11629, HOLD winding only, 48 V. He treats it as a ballpark for all pinball hold coils.

Converted with the model geometry (lever 23.75 mm at 41.5 deg): about 54.7 N at the plunger, about 56.6 N coil holding force including the ~1.9 N return spring. Assumes force perpendicular to the bat, applied quasi-statically toward rest.

Refinement (2026-10-03, after watching the video): pull was perpendicular and steady, not an impulse. The flipper breaks away at ~4.5 lbf; the scale then jumps to 5.5-5.75 lbf as it moves (read as a dynamic effect, possibly scale lag). Working value 4.5 lbf = ~44.7 N coil force = x0_hold ~0.0045; upper bound 5.75 lbf = 56.6 N = x0_hold ~0.0036. Figures above use the 5.75 upper bound.

Implication: hold force at closure scales as ~1/x0 in the model. x0_hold = 0.01 gives 20.2 N; about 0.0036 gives 56.6 N. The earlier "believable" x0_hold 0.04-0.07 and the 2.9-4.1 N real-hold estimate are almost certainly wrong. Force is very localized: ~7% of the closed value at 0.35 mm from the stop.

Decision (2026-10-03, later): adopt x0_hold = 0.0048, scaled from the 4.5 lbf breakaway at 2 in (not the 5.75 lbf upper bound, which gave 0.0036). The earlier 0.0045 figure above was the rough conversion; 0.0048 is the scaled value to use.

**Why:** resolves the item 6 contradiction (model needs ~20 N to hold the plunger on the stopper through the rebound) in favor of a small x0_hold.
**How to apply:** use x0_hold = 0.0048 in gorgar_model.py (still 0.01 in the file as of 2026-10-03, not yet changed or rerun); see [[reference_x0_range_by_coil_power]], [[project_gorgar_return_spring_and_series_coils]].

[from memory note reference_flipper_inertia_method]
Method (script: `runs/2026-10-05-harness/bat_inertia.py`): the VPX flipper outline is the convex hull of a base circle (rb, at the pivot) and an end circle (re, Lc along the axis) = union of circles r(s) = rb + (re-rb)s. Treat the bat as a uniform plate: I_pivot = m k^2, k^2 = (integral of r^2 dA)/A about the vertical axis through the pivot. The outline gives only the shape factor; the MASS is the physical input (weigh a real bat).
Gorgar (rb 20.75 U, re 11.5 U, Lc 114 U, U = 0.53975 mm): area 1332 mm^2, k = 33.0 mm, k^2 = 1.091e-3 m^2. So I = m * 1.091e-3 (m in kg).
Current ini `i_bat = 2.299e-5` kg m^2 (hitflipper.h iBat, gorgar_model.py:47 "same as Fish Tales - rothbauerw's call") = a uniform bat of 21.1 g. 14 g -> 1.53e-5, 25 g -> 2.73e-5, 35 g -> 3.82e-5, 50 g -> 5.46e-5.
Other inertias: `i_crank` 6.81e-6 separate; plunger adds plunger_kg * lever^2 (~1.6e-5 reflected); ball adds 0.08 * s^2 (1.4e-4 at 0.64 of the bat, 3.8e-5 at 0.28) = ~6x the bat at 0.64.
Caveats: uniform density is crude (steel/plastic bat + lighter rubber ring). The empty stroke only pins torque/inertia, and the coil constants were fitted with 2.3e-5, so changing i_bat means re-checking the empty stroke against the real upstroke (gorgar/gorgar_model.py) and the harness (runs/2026-10-05-harness).
Plan (deferred by user, 2026-10-05): replace i_bat with `bat_mass_g` (default 21.1 g so behaviour is unchanged), engine computes I = m k^2 from the flipper's own outline; weigh the real bat for the true value. Related: [[gorgar-trajectory-ab-2026-10-04]], the 2026-10-02 rule that the table supplies only position/geometry/rest angle (see [[gorgar-two-body-state-2026-10-02]]).

[from memory note project_gorgar_rest_stop_bounce]
Rest-stop bounce (item 10). Nothing changed in gorgar_model.py yet (backup gorgar_model_pre_reststop_2026-10-03.py exists). All experiments are in the return-only script.

## Terminology / decisions
- Say **elasticity** (not "restitution"/zeta). Work from elasticity.
- Crank lever arm rests on the rubber stop from phi=0. Stop acts on flipper+crank inertia only (plunger is decoupled until a pin hits a hole wall).
- Link damping unchanged for now: free-play zeta 0.1, stiff 0.12; rubber stop gets its own elasticity.
- User does NOT want automatic fits right now; wants single comparisons shown as plots. "Decoupled" is wrong wording for the no-coupling case: pins are free by default; call it "no far-wall coupling".

## Data
- runs/2026-09-28-rampup0/gorgar_rest_bounce_data_2026-10-03.csv (25 pts). Raw: approach ~1.1 deg/ms, min -0.74, apex +6.69 at ~40 ms. User's numbers: min -1.4, apex 5.25 (from phi=0). Aligned column = 0.895*raw - 0.74 (user: "assume some measurement error"). Aligned: min -1.40 at ~11.5-12 ms, apex 5.25 at ~40 ms, back to ~0 at ~72-75 ms, slight second dip. Real data has NO hump at 15-20 ms (also checked the Gorgar rebound video); a slight faint second oscillation only. Other flipper has similar pattern, shorter timing.

## Return-only model: gorgar_return_bounce.py
- execs gorgar_model.py defs (up to `def load`), coil off, flipper falls from end stop under spring, cached state at 9.53 deg. Stop options: linear Kelvin-Voigt (elasticity->zeta), Hunt-Crossley (`hc_n=`), hysteretic Walton-Braun (`hyst=True`: load K*pen, unload K/e^2). Experiments tweak `_ns` globals (PRELOAD_FORCE_N, K_SPRING_N_PER_MM re-solved by brentq for 60 ms return, SLOP_MM, C_CONTACT_N_S_PER_MM) then call `fall_to` to reset `S_START`.
- Plots in same folder: gorgar_return_bounce_fit/Kscan/gap2/preload/gap1_pre03(_e01)/HC/hyst/V .png.

## Findings
- Spring-only fall reaches 9.53 deg at -1.07 deg/ms vs data -1.02 (spring good).
- Old stop K=100 N*m/rad was ~10x too stiff for a flipper-only picture; onset must be phi=0 (earlier +0.9 deg rest was a fitted onset artifact; spring torque at phi=0 is only ~0.005 N*m).
- Return spring preload: 0.4 N (K_spring re-solved 0.0480 N/mm) fixes timing (apex 44 ms, back to 0 at 64 ms) but lowers the apex; 0.3 N gives K_spring 0.0549. User is leaning toward 0.3 N; not yet written into the model.
- Gap: 6 deg = 2.49 mm (23.75 mm/rad lever). Plunger moves ~0.5 m/s at phi=0 (preload-insensitive). Plunger KE 7.8 mJ vs flipper 6.4 mJ at 8 ms (total 14.2 mJ). Video suggests gap is eaten at about max rubber compression (~2.5-3 deg at that speed).
- THE PROBLEM: whenever the plunger reaches the far wall (any gap, any stop model), it kicks/drags the flipper -> hump, late trough (14-16 ms vs 11.5), apex 7-20 deg at ~65 ms. Smaller gaps (1-2 deg) are much worse (elastic far wall returns plunger momentum). Hunt-Crossley did not help; hysteretic stop helped (K~25, e 0.3: trough -1.5, apex ~7) but timing still late.
- With NO far-wall coupling (gap huge, no damping) the V is sharp: trough -1.34 at 10.1 ms, apex 3.6 at 34 ms (close to data) - but that run hides the plunger's 7.8 mJ (it just runs away), so it is not physically complete.
- User ruled out: larger gap (hump still occurs later), lossier far wall, plunger slowing/lagging (only delays hump). Plunger mass: 41.3 g (7/16 in x 2.25 in steel, 5 % groove trim; user, 2026-10-04). Open question: where does the plunger's energy go in the real machine without producing a hump on the flipper? Ideas not yet tried: how the real plunger decelerates/disengages (what it hits), link far wall acts on crank vs plunger geometry, coil/hold residual force, friction, plunger lag seen on one video.

## 2026-10-04 resolution of the hump (session 2)
- Plunger hump was because the link contact (20 N/mm, zeta 0.12) is too soft/slow: plunger energy reaches the flipper AFTER it leaves the rubber. Added to gorgar_return_bounce.py: `bounce_onebody()` (flipper+crank+plunger as ONE rigid body, I_eff = I_FLIPPER + m_p*lever^2, same as the 60 ms spring calibration) and hysteretic stop. One-body gives sharp V, trough matches, apex monotonic in elasticity: with preload 0.3 N, hysteretic stop K~40-45 N*m/rad (trough -1.4) and elasticity ~0.27 (interpolated, not run) gives apex ~5.25 (apex time ~54 ms vs data 40, still late). Two-body link converges to one-body as link K >= 300 N/mm (needs dt <= 0.01 ms); a much lossier link (K 100 N/mm, zeta 0.5-1.0) also removes the hump (apex 3.7-4.5 at ~48-50 ms).
- USER DECISION: after watching more video, the RIGID BODY is the correct assumption at the rest end, and possibly everywhere: the free play may be in the shaft bushings (flipper base/coil shifting), not the link pins; the 6 deg gap in the model may just happen to account for that at end of stroke. Open: how to carry this into gorgar_model.py (options: rigid/stiff link only near the rest end, keeping the 6 deg gap + 20 N/mm contacts for end of stroke; or restructure so the end-of-stroke compliance is a bushing/stop compliance on a single body).

[from memory note project_pinball_coil_scenarios]
User, 2026-10-14. Coil scenarios in pinball that the ball-magnet law must serve:
1. Iron-core coil BELOW the playfield; the core never touches a ball. COMMON. The playfield is usually routed out to bring the core closer; the core face sits AT LEAST 6 mm below the surface (user 2026-10-14; corrected from a first guess of 1/3 of the thickness).
2. Iron-core coil with the plug AT playfield level, locking balls to the core. Single ball COMMON; multiple balls (the disc/turntable case) LESS COMMON.

The FEM so far used 2 mm and a 38 mm core: redo with this.
Typical sizes (user 2026-10-14): iron plug winding 38 mm long on a 19 mm core (some modern ones 1/2 in = 12.7 mm core); air-core winding ~20 mm long, 32 mm bore.

User knows of NO case where the iron core face is as large as the ball or larger (typical core 19 mm vs ball 27 mm), so an infinite-iron-plane (mirror image) model is never exact; its error must be measured vs FEM.
