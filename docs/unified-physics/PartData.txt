1. FLIPPERS AND SOLENOIDS
-------------------------
[from memory note reference_flipper_measurements_2026-10-26]
Given by the user 2026-10-26, to be used when work on flipper physics continues. Typical pinball hardware, not one specific machine unless stated.

- **Crank arm: 7/8 in (22.2 mm), not 1 in.** Any model or ini value that used 1 in is wrong.
- **Flipper: 2.4 in (60.96 mm) from pivot to the centre of the tip radius.**
- **Plunger (solenoid core) outer diameter: 5/16 in (0.3125 in, 7.94 mm)** typical. Cross-section 0.0767 in^2 = 4.95e-5 m^2. Standard raw steel plunger bar length 2.33 in (59.2 mm).
- **Nylon coil sleeve** the plunger slides in: inside diameter about 0.340 to 0.345 in (8.64 to 8.76 mm).
- **Bobbin inner diameter** (where the wire starts): 0.380 to 0.400 in (9.65 to 10.16 mm).
- **Standard 49.5 mm (1.95 in) flipper bobbin: winding length between the flanges 43.0 to 44.0 mm (1.70 to 1.73 in).**
- **Plunger material for calculations: AISI 1018 or AISI 12L14 low-carbon steel.**
- Reference the user gave for solenoid force equations: https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/solenoid-force-calculation-equations-and-design-guide

[from memory notes reference_hold_coil_breakaway_force and reference_x0_range_by_coil_power]
- Hold-coil breakaway (user measured, 2026-10-03): Fish Tales coil FL-11629, HOLD winding only, 48 V, steady pull perpendicular to the bat at 2 in from the pivot. The flipper breaks away at ~4.5 lbf; the scale then reads 5.5-5.75 lbf as it moves (likely scale lag). The user treats it as a ballpark for all pinball hold coils.
  Through the model geometry (lever 23.75 mm at 41.5 deg, ~1.9 N return spring included) 4.5 lbf is ~44.7 N of coil force.

[from runs/2026-09-28-rampup0/moment_of_inertia.py, eos_only_fit.py, gorgar_model.py and memory note reference_flipper_inertia_method]
The flipper's inertia about its pivot is the sum of its parts. Every mass and size is a per-flipper setting; the values below are one real flipper's, not defaults for all.
- Bat (user measured, 2026-09-29; the model script calls it the Fish Tales bat): 28 g without rubber (plastic bat + steel shaft), 36 g with rubber (rubber 8 g). Shaft: steel, 3 in x 0.25 in, on the pivot axis. Bat: tapered, base diameter 19 mm, tip diameter 8 mm, 75 mm pivot to tip. Rubber mass about uniform along the bat, slightly more at the base.
  Method: shaft = cylinder on its own axis, I = m r^2 / 2 (steel 7.85 g/cm^3 gives its mass); plastic = solid frustum, base to tip diameter over the length, mass 28 g less the shaft, integrated about the pivot; rubber = 8 g spread along the bat, shaped heavier at the base. Result 2.299e-5 kg m^2 (2.583e-5 with uniform rubber): the i_bat in use.
- Crank: steel plate 0.25 in thick x 0.5 in wide x 1.5 in long, pivot 0.25 in from one end (source of the sizes not recorded). Method: plate mass from steel density (~24 g), I = m L^2 / 12 + m d^2, d = 0.5 in from the plate centre to the pivot; width term left out. Result 6.81e-6 kg m^2 (i_crank).
- Plunger (user, 2026-10-04, Gorgar): steel, 7/16 in diameter x 2.25 in, less 5 % for the link groove = 41.3 g (Fish Tales: 1/2 in x 2.5 in = 63.1 g). It slides in a line, so it adds m (dx/dphi)^2 at the pivot, which changes with angle through the crank and link geometry.
- Link: plastic, 19.05 mm (0.75 in) pin to pin (user's estimate; the crank, not the link, changed to 7/8 in). Mass not measured: ESTIMATED (lead, 2026-11-07, sizes the user judged close enough): nylon bar 1.25 in long (pin to pin + 0.25 in each end) x 0.5 in wide x 0.25 in thick (1.14 g/cm^3), less two 1/8 in pin holes = ~2.8 g. Method: the standard slider-crank split, half the link mass moves with the crank pin (adds m/2 r^2 to the inertia at the pivot) and half with the plunger (added to the plunger mass).
- Outline method (runs/2026-10-05-harness/bat_inertia.py), for a bat without measured parts: I = m k^2, k from the VPX flipper outline (base circle, end circle, length). Gorgar outline: k = 33.0 mm.
- The coil constants were fitted with i_bat 2.299e-5: a new inertia means rechecking the empty stroke against the real one.

2. GATES AND SPINNERS
---------------------
[from memory note project_gate_spinner_rebuild_data_2026-10-14]
User, 2026-10-14. Gates and spinners are modelled SEPARATELY.

**Gate types (4):** long plate, plate, wire W, wire rectangle. One-way or two-way. User sent photos: wire W, a deep trapezoid/U wire (a variant of the wire rectangle), a wide flat wire rectangle (hooks at the ends), and a plate gate in its bracket (Marco).
- Wire: 0.040-0.055 in (1.02-1.40 mm); any estimate in range is fine. Mass: ESTIMATED (lead) from diameter x estimated length, steel. Wire width follows the gate width.
- Plate + long plate: trapezoids, 0.5-0.8 mm thick (0.020-0.031 in), ~1-1/16 in (27 mm) wide at the top. Long plate: bottom = half the top, ~1-1/16 in long, LIP at the end, 2.5-3 mm, bent to a flat face for the ball to bounce off when one-way; the lip is vertical when the plate hangs 15 deg from vertical (bent 15 deg relative to the plate). Plate: bottom ~2/3 of a ball width. Plate: ~20 mm long (user's estimate).
- Pivot height: a VARIABLE, typically 1 to 1-3/8 in. Wire gates hang low enough to strike a rolling ball near its centre.
- Pivot: NO bushings; all pivot on wires through holes in the bracket. Resistance depends on whether a switch is attached.
- Stops: metal stops or the bracket hitting the plate/wire. Gravity returns the gate to its start angle. Plate gates: a metal stop against the plate in both directions (allows one-way travel). Wire gates: a bend in the wire outside the pivot hits the bracket and blocks the opposite direction.
- Rest: two-way hangs straight down; one-way hangs at an angle, default 15 deg, adjustable.
- Switch: actuated by a bend in the wire outside the pivot; fires when the gate is open far enough to let the ball pass (maybe slightly before); little resistance (gate nearly fully open when it fires).
- Solenoid hold: the coil moves an actuator that holds the gate open. Model as a simple script open/close with a specified open angle; NO coil modelling.

**Spinner data (user 2026-10-14, Marco photo of a blade with a Z-bent shaft wire):** OFFSET SHAFT: the wire is bent so the pivot axis is off the plate's centre, gravity pulls the blade back toward vertical after every spin; damping and the switch sometimes stop it short of perfect vertical. Thickness ~0.8 mm (0.032 in). Width varies (a variable); height typically 1.25 in (31.75 mm). Pivot height like gates (1 to 1-3/8 in), strikes a rolling ball from its mid point to the upper half. Shaft diameter like the gate wire. Damping = a VARIABLE (spin-down varies per machine). The bracket is NOT collidable (ball cannot touch it on the playfield; use a primitive if wanted, maybe later). Switch: LEAF or OPTO. Leaf: spring resistance as it closes, gives it back as it opens -> the spinner shows acceleration/deceleration cycles during the spin. ESTIMATED (lead, photo read, not measured): blade ~39 mm wide, Z-bend step ~6 mm; steel blade 39x31.75x0.8 = ~7.8 g.

**Spinner answers (user 2026-10-14):** shaft offset ~6 mm CONFIRMED, IN THE PLANE of the blade (axis above the blade centre). Leaf switch: ~3/4 in (19 mm) lever arm of very thin conductive metal (probably copper); some spinners use a MICRO SWITCH instead. Switch force default: decide by FEEL in testing (user guesses my ~10 gf estimate is too high); expose it as a key.

5. MAGNETS AND COILS
--------------------
Coil data reference: https://mayapinball.com/papers/pinball-coil-data/ (see section 1).
[from memory note project_pinball_coil_scenarios]
User, 2026-10-14. Coil scenarios in pinball that the ball-magnet law must serve:
1. Iron-core coil BELOW the playfield; the core never touches a ball. COMMON. The playfield is usually routed out to bring the core closer; the core face sits AT LEAST 6 mm below the surface (user 2026-10-14; corrected from a first guess of 1/3 of the thickness).
2. Iron-core coil with the plug AT playfield level, locking balls to the core. Single ball COMMON; multiple balls (the disc/turntable case) LESS COMMON.
3. Air-core coil pulling a single ball into the core, then releasing it. COMMON.

Typical iron-core geometry (user 2026-10-14, "usually, but not always"): the winding's top end sits ~6 mm below the core face (about 1/3 of the playfield thickness); core length = winding length + ~6 mm; CORRECTION: cores sometimes also extend BELOW the winding (threaded fitting for height adjustment) -> model it only if it changes results significantly.
Typical sizes (user 2026-10-14): iron plug winding 38 mm long on a 19 mm core (some modern ones 1/2 in = 12.7 mm core); air-core winding ~20 mm long, 32 mm bore.

User knows of NO case where the iron core face is as large as the ball or larger (typical core 19 mm vs ball 27 mm), so an infinite-iron-plane (mirror image) model is never exact; its error must be measured vs FEM.

Gorgar magnet (user, 2026-10-10): 28 VDC, 3/4 in core, 650 turns 22 AWG (R 3.2 ohm computed), 3/4 in playfield, on ~750 ms, bracket ~2.25 in square angled to the playfield underside (newer tables: one side only); some machines have the core reach the playfield surface.

User decisions (2026-10-10/11): the coil is current-driven (not just on/off); strength, falloff and range come from inductance, resistance and dimensions, not tuning. Real behaviour (user video): a ball locks onto a flush core with little oscillation.
