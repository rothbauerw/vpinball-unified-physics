NEW PHYSICS REFERENCE  (rubber-contact branch)
==============================================

What this file is
-----------------
A reference for every physics feature added on this branch beyond stock Visual Pinball: what each one models, how to switch it on, its settings, its script
interface, its diagnostics and its known limits. It is updated whenever a feature is added or changed (see "Change log" at the end).
The full list of settings, with units, typical ranges and limits, is the commented vpx_physics.ini (the reference copy is tables/basic/vpx_physics.ini);
this file explains the models and ties the pieces together. If the two disagree, the code wins: tell the maintainer.

Design rules
------------
* Physical laws, not patches. Each model is built from the real mechanics (Hertz contact, coil circuits, tensioned cord, magnetic circuit). Nothing is faked with curve fits when a physical law exists.
* One contact law. Rubbers, posts, walls, primitives and the playfield share one force model (Hertz + damping + friction, spin and rolling), tuned per material.
* Cost matters. Every model is stepped by the physics engine in small bounded pieces per 1 ms cycle, and idle objects cost nothing. Cost is measured, not assumed.
* Everything stays tunable from an ini file. The table file itself is never changed.

Switching it on
---------------
1. A table runs the unified engine only with  unified = 1  under [Features] in its physics ini (2026-10-21). No ini, or no unified key = stock VPX physics with every
   new model off (no mixing of stock and new). The ini is <table>.physics.ini next to the table (MyTable.vpx -> MyTable.physics.ini); vpx_physics.ini in the folder
   is a testing fallback (it applies to every table in the folder); VPX_PHYSICS_INI=<file> names the file (test harness). VPX_UNIFIED and VPX_PHYSICS_MODEL are no longer read.
2. With unified = 1 every model runs with its defaults; the other [Features] keys switch single models off, and the other sections hold settings. It is the only
   settings file (the older flippers.ini, rubbers.ini and contact.ini are no longer read).
3. Switch features off in [Features] with  <key> = 0  (a missing key means on, for every feature).
Reload: [Contact], [Rubbers], [Slings], [Flippers] and the rubber/sling sections reload while the table runs (about once a second). Switches that decide which objects use a
model are read when the table loads; [Magnet:<name>] sections reload when the file is saved (a coil keeps its voltage and current).
Log lines say what loaded and what was ignored (a misspelt key or section is reported once as a warning).

Units
-----
Ini values are in the units their names say (mm, ohm, mH, N, kg, degrees, V, microseconds). VPX units: 1 U = 0.53975 mm, 1 VPT = 10 ms, ball velocity 18.53 = 1 m/s,
1 mass unit = 80 g (a standard ball).


1. FLIPPERS  ([Flippers], [Flipper:<name>])
-------------------------------------------
Two-body model of a real pinball flipper: the bat and crank are one rigid body, the plunger is another, joined by a link with free play. Coil circuit (power and hold
windings in series or parallel, EOS switch, flux-conserving switch-off), plunger/coil stop, return spring, rubber rest stop, hand-off between two-body and rigid
one-body states. Wiring series | parallel | single.
Settings: v_supply, r_power/l_air_power/l_arm_power/x0_power and the hold-winding set, link and crank geometry, masses/inertias, spring, stops, slop.
The hold coil's x0 was corrected from a measured 4.5 lbf breakaway force.
Flipper nudge: when a flipper hits its end of stroke, the other flipper's pivot gets a one-shot x pulse (nudge_target, nudge_gain, nudge_rise_ms, nudge_fall_ms).
The drawn flipper follows the pivot. A hit during a running pulse is ignored.
Series wiring runs on the coil's one energy law (coilmodel.h CoilModel::OverLoop, 2026-11-02): the circuit and its force over the solved move, so the coil's work equals what the circuit supplied at any step length.
Before EOS the power winding and the shorted hold winding are two uncoupled loops, the law once for each; after EOS, or with the coil off, one loop, L the two windings' sum. Parallel and single wiring run on the same law (2026-11-03): parallel = each winding its own loop on the supply, in the diode's loop when off; past EOS the power winding's loop is open, its current 0 and its field to the switch; single = the power winding alone. CoilAdvance is deleted. A bat on its up stop moving as one body (coil just let go, current still flowing) stays on the stop while spring + ball + coil (through the link and crank) press it in (PinCheck, 2026-11-03).
L's curve runs on past the closed end to frac = -x0 / 2 (x0 the hold winding's), for trial moves in the solve; a step ends past the stop only by the stop's tolerance.
Status: reference values for a Gorgar (Bally 1979) flipper; tested in play.

2. BALL / RUBBER CONTACT ON THE FLIPPER BAT  (rubber_* keys in [Flippers])
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The ball is pressed into the bat rubber and pushed back out by the shared rubber law of the group step (GroupStepDesign 19). The bat, its coil and its stops are one part of the group (src/physics/batgroup.h, moving-part
wrapper contactbatpart.h, host ContactPartHost, adapter in hitflippermodel.cpp): balls and bat move in one solve, the contact's reaction on the bat is in that solve, and only the coil and the pivot's nudge add energy.
An island with a bat always goes to the group: it has no old loop, no impulse and no stock path. Gates, spinners and turntable discs are group parts too since 2026-11-02. No moving part is left on the old loop.
The bat alone is the same group with no balls, asleep while nothing changes. The touch's release gap is rubber_release_gap_mm. The flipper's Hit / Collide event and the rumble come from the bat's touch (closing speed), after the step. Ball control does not select a ball the bat hit (to-do).
Ignored keys: [Features] flippers = 0, [Features] flipper_rubber = 0 and rubber_contact = 0 are read and ignored, with a notification. rubber_release_steps and rubber_substep_us are gone: each logs "ignored line".
On screen when it happens (rule 8): a step with a bat could not be solved (the rest of that cycle is lost), a step taken on energy alone, the group gaining over 0.1 mJ beyond its drives in a cycle.
With [Features] unified off the table is stock and the stock flipper runs.

3. RUBBER RINGS, POSTS, WALL AND PRIMITIVE RUBBERS  ([Features] rubbers, surface_rubbers; [Rubbers], [Rubber:<name>], [Wall:<name>], [Primitive:<name>])
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Static post and wall rubbers on the same contact model as the flipper bat. The engine steps the contact (ContactEngage / the contact system's step / ContactRelease): a bounded
amount of work per cycle, force and torque only; gravity, playfield and other objects stay with the engine. Walls and primitives flagged  rubber = 1  in their
[Wall:<name>] or [Primitive:<name>] section use it too (surface_layer_mm, edge_radius_mm). Slow and resting contact use the model as well (slow_contact),
with a larger step for a ball at rest (rest_substep_us, rest_speed_mm_s) and a bounded step for a rolling ball (roll_travel_um). Bounce loss: alpha_s_per_m
(default 0.18, grows with impact speed); the flat loss from tan_delta (normal_loss) is off by default since 2026-10-29 (a pinched ball still settles, measured). Flippers nearby carry the rubbers around their ball (a bat rubber step solves nearby rubbers together).
Sideways law (2026-10-31, every contact on ContactCore and groupstep.h Item): a spring kt = 8 G* a x sideways stiffness, a damper tan_delta sqrt(kt mt) (damping ratio tan_delta / 2: the rubber's own loss, for nu 0.5 tension and shear lose alike), spring and damper together within mu N. Mindlin-Deresiewicz: a patch that grows holds the force, one that shrinks holds the stretch, so what the spring holds only falls.
sideways_stiffness  ([Rubbers], [Rubber:<name>], [Wall:<name>], [Primitive:<name>]; default 0.1, limit 0.01 - 100): multiplier on Mindlin's stiffness, which assumes a thick solid body. A ring's section also shears and turns on its post, in series with the patch: calculated about 0.14 - 0.25 from assumed geometry, not measured; 0.1 is a fit to the user's rolling rebound (0.97 / 0.96 / 0.87 / 0.64 at 0.1 / 0.32 / 0.86 / 3 m/s) on a ring at 35 U, mu 0.8.
rubber_sideways_stiffness  ([Flippers], [Flipper:<name>]; default 0.33, limit 0.01 - 100): the same for a bat's rubber, a strip lying on the bat. rubber_e_mpa 12 is the normal stiffness of a thin confined strip; confinement does not stiffen shear, so G is the plain rubber's (E about 4): 4 / 12 = 0.33. Nothing softer is in series. Cross-check on the strip's own shear, G pi (a + h/2)^2 / h, a = 2.3 - 3.7 mm: 0.33 - 0.46. Calculated, not measured.
Keys that no longer act (old files still load, one line in the log): shear_zeta, shear_scale, rubber_shear_zeta, rubber_shear_scale, slow_contact, ball_contact (unified engine). The old band models' keys: see 4. Hard surfaces, the playfield, the disc, gates, spinners, bumpers and ball pairs are not rubber: they keep sideways stiffness 1 and their old damping (ratio 0.2, a guess, standing in for micro-slip).
Status: tested at ball mass 1 and 100 (pinch, flat face, cradle). A stale ini key loss_ref_hz is gone.
ring_model  ([Rubbers], or one rubber in [Rubber:<name>], [Wall:<name>], [Primitive:<name>]; default 1 since 2026-10-30, the user's word, not yet run on a stress table; added 2026-10-30, GroupStepDesign 14): 1 = an island with a ball that can touch this surface within the cycle goes through the
group step, the solve the band's cord uses (groupstep.h), with every surface in the ball's contact; 0 = the old loop. Not for hard surfaces, the playfield or bands. An island with a moving part stays on the old loop, and so
does one over 8 balls or 32 surfaces (shown on screen once). A step that cannot be solved: the cycle is retaken on the old loop (shown once), never stock. A ball found inside such a surface is not seated: its squash counts from zero until it has
backed out. Measured on Rubber24 / 25 only, not on the stress table: runs/2026-10-30-one-solve/notes.md. Limits: GroupStepDesign 14.11. Not yet the default.

4. BANDS  ([Features] bands; band keys in [Rubbers] / [Rubber:<name>])
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A rubber stretched between posts is a band: the fixed-height cord, stepped with its balls on the group step (src/physics/cordfixed.h). It is the only band since 2026-11-01. The old node band and the old cord (bandcord.h) are saved at git tag old-bands-2026-11-01.
Detection: every Rubber object at load. Collidable on and at least one straight free span (band_curve_radius_mm, band_min_span_mm, band_posts): a band. Otherwise a ring, with no notice. There is no band key.
  A [Sling:] section still names the plate; a section on a rubber with no straight span or with Collidable off: a ring, WARN and on screen, never stock.
  The log for each rubber: "Rubber <name>: band|ring, collidable, visible", and for a band its posts, spans (length, how far off the chord) and "values at load" (section, ring, tension).
Thickness: band_section_mm (0 to 25 mm, overrides, for physics and drawing), otherwise the rubber's Thickness in the editor. With the override the post's groove stays where it is: the cord's centre, and the drawn band, move out by the change in radius.
Ring: band_ring_in (inner diameter, inches, overrides; 20 at most). Otherwise automatic: standard sizes 3/4 in, then 1 in and up in 1/4 in steps. Tension = EA (loop / (pi (inner diameter + section)) - 1). Among the sizes within 35 - 60 N, the one nearest 47.5 N, the middle of the range. None within: the size whose tension is nearest to the range, WARN in the log.
Other keys: band_stops (posts behind the band; named table objects allowed), switches (blades the script reads), band_stretch_alpha_s_per_m, band_twist_scale, band_twist_loss, band_ref_hz, band_plate_reach (default 1 since 2026-11-05: a sling band takes and releases a ball at the plate's stroke at the cord, not a fixed 30 mm; change log 2026-11-05), per rubber or in [Rubbers].
Keys that no longer act (old files still load, one WARN in the log per key): band, band_model, band_prestretch_pct, band_tension_n, band_node_zeta, band_ring_zeta, band_rest_mm, band_substep_us, band_shear_zeta, band_node_mass_g, band_max_ms, band_flipper_margin_u, band_follow, band_follow_mm, band_node_mm, band_ringing, band_lock_z, switch_gap_mm, switch_min_closed_ms, sling_min_closed_ms, sling_plate_contact_zeta.
Script: Rubber.BandSwitchCount, Rubber.BandSwitchClosed(index); events <Rubber>_SwitchClosed(index) and _SwitchOpened(index).
Limits: a script that turns Collidable on during play gets the ring's law on that rubber, never stock (inferred, not run). A straight-span rubber can't be made a ring except by Collidable off, or contact = 0 for stock. Rubber.BandDeflection and the other Band* readings, and the sling's coil readings, stay at 0 or their defaults on the cord; the BandHit event's value is 0 (code read). A stop post has no top; a stop goes on the span whose line is nearest, within 30 mm.

5. SLINGS  ([Features] slings; [Slings], [Sling:<rubber name>])
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A sling is a band with a kicker plate behind it: plate on a pivot, a coil with plunger, link and return spring (same mechanics as the flipper), two switch blades
that close when the band is pushed back, and a driver that gives the coil one pulse. The plate and blades are table primitives (collision off, pose set in memory).
Script: Rubber.SlingTrigger() fires one coil pulse; events SlingFire and the band switch events.
The coil runs on the coil's one energy law (coilmodel.h CoilModel::Over, 2026-11-02), through the link's chord, so the plate is given exactly the plunger's work and that work equals what the circuit supplied at any step length.

6. PLAYFIELD  ([Features] playfield_contact, default on; [Playfield])
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The playfield (plane or playfield_mesh primitive) is a surface of the force model: one Hertz + damping law for impact, bounce, rest and rolling, with a
published, UNVERIFIED steel-on-lacquered-wood material (e_mpa 7875, alpha 0.5, tan_delta 0.05, mu = the table's friction). The plane host is +-100000 U wide.
Resting depth about 0.6 um; no creep or sawtooth. Rolling resistance on the playfield is the [Contact] rolling_resistance (a floor under the rubber hysteresis law, which gives
almost nothing on hard wood): a ball rolling at 0.1 m/s slows at 0.02 m/s2 as on the stock contact. Kickers with a hole, and bands, hand the ball over (see Limits).
Playfield material "feel" is parked until measured playfield-acceleration data is available.

7. CONTACT FRICTION  ([Features] contact; [Contact])
------------------------------------------------------
[Contact] static_model (default 1 since 2026-11-03, was 0; added 2026-10-31): 1 = every static surface (walls, primitives, ramps, playfield) takes a ball to the group step, as a rubber does with ring_model. Moving parts and old node bands keep their island on the old loop. Measured on the bench at 1, on the old node band: no launch, no failed step, energy never rises at any step 2 us to 1 ms (GroupStepDesign 17). Not yet run in VPX. At 0 walls, primitives, ramps and the playfield stay on stock; rubbers are on the group step either way, so everything in the group step's law reaches them (change log 2026-10-31, static surfaces).
   A depth a ball gained with no step (a position move does no work) is taken by the contact's zero, at 0 and at 1, on every contact stepped this way: nothing is stored and the ball goes on. A script or the test driver placing a ball, and a hand-over from the old loop, are silent. Any other cause is a fault, on the playfield once per cause and surface and in the log as "Contact: ball ... deeper in ... with no step" (up to 20 per cause and surface). Causes: moved outside any step (a stock part: trigger, kicker, edge, locked ball), already inside at its first touch, the surface's faces in reach changed. The group has no limit on balls, shapes or unknowns; a cord keeps its own 8-ball limit (others left out, with a WARN).
[Contact] roll_smooth_rad_s (default 0.05, was 2 until 2026-11-01): the rolling torque in the group step is smoothed through zero spin as tanh(spin / s), s in rad/s. Any value from 0.001 to 100 works.
[Contact] roll_model (default 1): 1 = stick springs, group step only (below). 0 = the old law, exactly as before 2026-11-03: the rolling and twist torques smoothed through zero spin (roll_smooth_rad_s, tanh(spin / 2) for twist).
   roll_model 1:
   each contact holds a rolling angle (in the contact plane) and a twist angle (about the normal) on the patch's own Hertz springs, tilting (4/3) E* a^3 and torsion (16/3) G* a^3
   (G* = 0.35 E*, nu 0.3), a damper beside each; it sticks while the trial torque is within muRr R N (twist: 3 pi / 16 mu a N) and rolls (twists) at that limit beyond, taken as the
   shear spring is. A resting ball under a load below the limit stays at rest; the spring only stores and returns energy. Old-loop contacts keep their tanh terms.
[Contact] pivot_model (default 1; added 2026-11-04): the pin friction of a 1-dof part (gate, spinner, wire switch; bodypart.h). 1 = a stick spring at the pin: the pin's twist s in its hole on
   Mindlin's patch spring K = 8 G* a r_pin^2, a = (3 N r_pin / 4 E*)^(1/3), steel E* 115 GPa, G* 23.8 GPa (no key), stepped as the rolling spring (trapezoid torque), with the contact damper c = 0.4 sqrt(K I) beside it (as the ball's shear spring); it holds while the
   torque is within mu_pivot r_pin N and slides at mu_dynamic_ratio times that. Slip and a softening spring are booked as loss; resting on a stop lets the spring go (loss). No stuck state,
   slip-direction flag or turn event. 0 = the old law, byte-identical (measured on the wire bench). The disc (turntable) holds at friction_nm and slides at mu_dynamic_ratio x that, its spring the shaft's torsion G J / L (1/4 in steel, 30 mm, about 420 Nm/rad; no key); spinner: see below (design
   runs/2026-11-04-pivot-friction/design.md). Read at the start of a play.
[Contact] eps_rest_um (default 0.1 since 2026-11-05 cleanup, was 0.01; added 2026-11-05): the position error a steady contact's step may carry (step_model 1 and step_swing_cap; was fixed at 0.01 um). An
   estimate, not measured: to be swept on the 750-hit bench.
[Contact] step_swing_cap (default 1 since 2026-11-05 cleanup; added 2026-11-05; needs step_model 1): the phase error of a steady contact's mode is at most its whole swing 2A
   (the midpoint step keeps the mode's energy), so a mode with 2A <= epsRest (10 nm) sets no step limit; the other rules (arrival, impact, slip, parts, hMax) still bound
   the step. Else as step_model 1. At the wire switch impacts (L1, 3380-3640 ms) 707 of 1704 rule-7 wire steps had 2A <= 10 nm (A inferred from h). Off: unchanged.
[Contact] shear_step_motion (default 1; added 2026-11-05, on by default the same day): an unsteady contact's shear clock by the motion, the same law step_model 1 gives the normal one. The shear spring's
   stretch is bounded by Coulomb, A = mu N / Kt at the expected depth dE, so h_T = max(omegaH / omT, min(sqrt(12 eps_rest / A) / omT, hMax)), and h_T = hMax when 2A <= eps_rest;
   the step is min(normal clock, h_T). Normal clock, arrival and steady contacts (rule 7) unchanged; rule 2 when h_T binds. A weak press (the light wire) no longer holds the step
   to omT; a hard rubber/steel hit keeps it (A large). Uses the one Coulomb mu. Off: today's path exactly. Log: the contact-settings line shows "shear_step_motion 0/1".
   VPX_STEP_RULE column A_um: mu N / Kt at dE for the item that set the step, in um, written on or off. Design: runs/2026-11-05-shear-step/design.md section 4.
[Contact] term_arrival (default 1; added 2026-11-05, on by default the same day): with hinge_piece_sum, each term of a contact (a wire piece, a shared joint, the faces) not yet pressing (d_t < 0)
   gets the arrival rule of any contact: dDot_t = -v . n_t + slope_t * rate (the term's exact gradient), and when closing h <= max(0.5 * -d_t / dDot_t, hs_t) (rule 3,
   or 1/2 when hs_t binds). hs_t is the term's own approach clock, the one an untouched contact gets: unsteady, normal clock omegaH / omN at the depth its closing speed
   reaches (the same energy estimate, the contact's law and reduced mass), and the shear clock as shear_step_motion gives it. Not the contact's hs: a resting contact's
   hs can be hMax (step_swing_cap), which hid a piece arriving while the ball rests on another (measured 2026-11-05, runs/2026-11-05-term-arrival-test). The union's depth sees only the deepest piece, so a second piece arriving inside a step was invisible to the step choice.
   Uses the terms the step choice already probes (no new probes). A contact the union says is out of reach is kept when one of its terms can be reached in hMax.
   Off: today's path exactly. Log: the contact-settings line shows "term_arrival 0/1". VPX_STEP_RULE column term (last): the term index that set the step, -1 if none;
   delta_um, dDot and omN are then that term's (A_um stays the contact's).
[Contact] hinge_piece_sum (default 1 since 2026-11-05 cleanup; added 2026-11-05): 1 = a hinge part's contact (wire switch, wire gate, spinner, plate gate) is the union of its pieces:
   every touchable wire piece of a side (+1), every joint shared by m pieces (-(m - 1)), every flat face (AddPlate quad, its two triangles) (+1), every line two of them share (a plate's fold edge, a wire along a plate edge) as a capsule of the smaller radius (-1), U = sum s_t U(max(d_t, 0)) (wire_sum's energy, on for every
   hinge part with this key). Each term is stepped by its own chord and its own exact (Gonzalez) gradient, so a hand-over between pieces in one step no longer puts the misfit
   on the light part (measured 7.6 um at the wire switch impact, runs/2026-11-05-wire-d0; paper ~6e-4 um per term). Damping, friction and twist act once per contact along
   the summed elastic force; the damping's rate is the motion along it (loss exact for any direction). Step choice and arrival use the union depth. Inside a step's attempt
   only the pieces the ball can reach are probed (2026-11-05, near pieces): at the attempt's start each contact lists the pieces whose distance bound is within the ball's
   travel 2 |v| h plus the part's turn 2 |rate| h (times the ball's distance from the axis) plus 0.1 mm, and never less than the far test's bound; every probe of the attempt
   (start, middle, end, every Newton try) uses that list, in the pieces' order. After the solve every piece off the list is checked off the ball at the move's middle and end
   (a distance bound); if one is not, the lists are built again with 4x the reach and the attempt is solved again. A piece off the list has depth < 0 at start, middle and
   end, so the sum, forces and energy are those of all the pieces. Outside an attempt (step choice, events) and in an energy step or the far test's second solve every piece
   is probed. No term limit (a contact's terms: 12 in the Hit, more on the heap). Diagnostic VPX_HINGE_NEAR_ALL=1: every list = all pieces (same exe, A/B).
   Not covered: the massless wire (its spring is in the part's U*); a wire end lying on a plate face (no overlap term: a WARN names it at load). Off: unchanged.
   Log: the [Contact] line shows hinge_piece_sum.
   Design: runs/2026-11-05-hinge-piece-sum/design.md. Diagnostics: (VPX_WIRE_D0_TRACE removed 2026-11-05) one 'term' line per pressing term (its depths and misfit D).
[Contact] damp_dir_slope (default 1 since 2026-11-05; added 2026-11-05): with hinge_piece_sum, Newton's slope of a contact made of terms takes the turning of the damping's direction
   G = Fel / |Fel| (the pressing terms' summed elastic force) and the change of the damping rate dd = G . dq through G: h dG/du = (I - G g^T) K / fe, K = sum_t h tk_t tg_t tg_t^T.
   Added to the normal block: (fd / fe)(I - G g^T) K + (cD / (h fe)) G (dq - dd g)^T K; the second part goes on N's slope (shear limit) too. The force is unchanged; only Newton's
   rounds can change. Off: the old slope exactly. Log: the [Contact] line shows damp_dir_slope. Design: runs/2026-11-05-damp-dir-slope/design.md; test runs/2026-11-05-damp-dir-test.
[Contact] part_guess_accel (default 0; added 2026-11-06): 1 = a moving part's (BodyPart: wire switch, gate, spinner) first guess of its move carries its last step's
   acceleration, (rate + a h / 2) h with a = the force of the move taken (Eval's at it, the balls' load included) / mass, as a ball's guess does. Newton's start only: the law and
   the solved step are unchanged. Kept across cycles (unlike the ball's, cleared each cycle): it is in the part's snapshot, so a retaken cycle is exact. Off: rate h, as before. Log: the [Contact] line shows part_guess_accel.
[Contact] step_model (default 1 since 2026-11-05 cleanup, was 0; added 2026-11-04): how a steady touching contact (rule 7 of the group step) sizes its step. 0 = omegaHRest 2.4 / omega at the
   depth a 1 cm/s approach would reach (unchanged). 1 = by the motion in its mode: the midpoint step keeps the mode's energy at any h, its error is phase, so
   h <= sqrt(12 eps / A) / omega, omega at the real depth, A = sqrt((closing rate / omega)^2 + (net normal push / (m omega^2))^2), eps 0.01 um (estimate, no key),
   never shorter than rule 0, at most hMax. Arrivals, impacts, slips and every part's own step limit (pin stick spring) unchanged. groupstep.h ChooseStep.
   Spinner under 1 (2026-11-04 piece 3): no stock Damping; pin mu 0.3 (mu_pivot sets it); blade air drag 0.5 rho Cd W w|w| (a^4 + b^4) / 4 (rho 1.2, Cd 1.2, no key);
   leaf / micro switch rubs at every angle (leaf_force_n x mu x cam_r, in the pin's limit), its viscous loss off; opto: no contact.
[Contact] sleep (default 0; added 2026-11-03), sleep_speed_mm_s (default 0.1), sleep_hold_ms (default 100): pile sleep. With sleep = 1 a group of touching balls whose every ball has stayed under sleep_speed_mm_s (travel and the surface speed of its spin) for sleep_hold_ms, touching only parts at rest, every coil current steady and no contact deeper than 0.05 mm, sleeps: its balls keep their place and contact states, their speed and spin go to 0, and nothing steps them until a wake (a ball or part reaching the group, a drive, a magnet solve that moves its forces, a ball removed). Sleep and wake each write one INFO line. Design and limits: runs/2026-11-03-pile-sleep/design.md.
The object's Friction is the static mu; dynamic mu = mu_dynamic_ratio * static; rolling resistance coefficient; twist (spin about the contact normal).

Ball-to-ball contact (added 2026-10-11; on the unified engine always on since 2026-10-31: ball_contact no longer acts there)
Balls the force model holds (on the playfield, a disc, a post) are stepped together when they touch: at every substep each ball's surface forces, the Hertz contact between
the balls (the same law as the rubbers: Hertz + Hunt-Crossley damping + Mindlin friction + twist, equal and opposite) and the magnets are summed, then all balls move.
A free ball (flying) that can reach a held ball this cycle joins the cluster, so a ball landing on a held ball is solved with it. The stock ball-ball impulse and position
push are skipped for those pairs; a pair of two free balls, or a ball owned by a flipper or a band, still uses the stock collision (the narrow guard in HitBall::Collide stays
for those). Clusters chain (A-B-C), so a row or a stack is one coupled system.
  ball_contact          1 = on
  ball_e_mpa            Young's modulus of the ball steel, 200000 (the pair uses half of it)
  ball_nu               Poisson ratio, 0.3
  ball_alpha            Hunt-Crossley damping, s/m (rebound ratio about 1 - alpha * v), 0.05
  ball_mu               steel on steel friction, 0.15
  ball_rolling_resistance  rolling resistance of one ball on another, 0.003 (torque = coefficient x reff x N against the balls' relative spin in the tangent plane; the pair had none before 2026-10-12,
                        so a ball rolling round a magnet-pressed neighbour never lost that spin). Same class as the playfield's rolling_resistance.
  ball_tan_delta        loss tangent of the steel pair, 0 (elastic) by default: a constant loss factor (damping ratio tan_delta / 2) so a slow bounce loses energy too, which alpha cannot do
                        (rebound ratio about exp(-pi tan_delta / 2); 0.03 gives 0.95)
  ball_substep_us       smallest substep a pair may need, 2 us. The pair's step comes from its stiffness at the compression it reaches (resting depth, or the depth of an impact at the
                        closing speed): h = omega_H / omega with omega = sqrt(2 E* sqrt(Reff delta) / m_reduced), omega_H 0.2 for an impact and 0.8 at rest, at most 100 us
  ball_rest_substep_us  no longer used (the stiffness rule above replaced it); still read so old files load
  ball_reach_mm         balls closer than this (plus what they close in the cycle) are stepped as one cluster, 1
  island_reach          1 = a ball and a moving part share an island only if the ball can reach the part's real surface within the cycle (reachable set: group speeds, part range of motion or drive); 0 = any part in the ball's near list (default 1 since 2026-11-05, added the same day; runs/2026-11-05-island-reach/design.md)
  ball_pair_reach       1 = two balls also share an island when their gap can close within the cycle: gap < (V_i + V_j) T + 0.1 mm, V = sqrt(2 E / m) of the group's kinetic energy (spin included) + forces + 2x each reached part's surface speed (BallsMayMeet / BallSpeedBound, also the sleep wake test); needs island_reach = 1; 0 = the pairwise ball_reach_mm + 2|vi-vj|T rule only (default 1 since 2026-11-05, added the same day)
  arrival_step          1 = arrival-time substeps for islands of balls and static surfaces (section 12), 0 = the uniform substeps (default 1 since 2026-10-13; the scenario matrix and the seeded statistical metric passed)
  arrival_depth_um      the deepest a contact may start before the substep is taken again (rewind), um, 2; the larger of this and a quarter of the compression the impact will reach
Script ball-ball collision callbacks and the rumble still fire (for closing speeds above 0.25 U/VPT at first contact).
Test (three-ball cradle, flat table, 1 m/s): momentum right after the hit 0.99 of the start, ball 3 leaves at 0.95 (stock: 0.80 impulse restitution); then 5/7 when sliding turns to rolling.
Trace: VPX_CONTACT_TRACE (earlier name VPX_RING_TRACE, still read) prints ContactPair (steps, max overlap, max force, end gap) per pair per cycle and the island size on the ContactTrace lines.

8. MAGNETS  ([Features] magnets; [Magnet:<name>])   -- added 2026-10-10
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
An electromagnet (coil on an iron core, optional mounting-bracket yoke) pulls the steel ball.
Script (table-global, like Nudge):
   Magnet "M1", 28                 supply voltage in volts; 0 = off. A call made while the script loads is applied when the physics starts.
   amps = MagnetCurrent("M1")      coil current in amperes
   MagnetDuty "M1", 0.5            PWM drive: mean voltage = duty x rated_volts (-1 .. 1); the coil's L / R time constant (5 - 30 ms) smooths a PWM of a kilohertz or more
Model (src/physics/magnet.h):
 * Coil: N turns, round wire; resistance from the winding (or resistance_ohm, measured); inductance L = N^2 / reluctance; current follows L dI/dt = V - R I
   (exact exponential each 1 ms step); with V = 0 it decays through R (freewheel diode).
 * Flux = N I / reluctance. Reluctance = far pole + near pole. A pole radiating into space has reluctance 1/(8 mu0 a) (a = pole radius); the near pole
   adds a parallel-plate term mu0 A / gap as the ball nears the face, so the pull rises steeply close in. With a bracket, the far pole is the bracket flanges.
 * Eddy-current braking of the ball's spin (added 2026-10-12): a steel sphere turning at w across a field B feels the torque (2 pi / 15) sigma R^5 B^2 w against the part of w across B
   (a spin about the field lines induces nothing). B is the field INSIDE the ball: the coil field times 1 + 2 min(1, mu0 Ms / 3|B|), i.e. up to 3 (a permeable sphere gathers the field).
   Applied in the contact substeps as an exponential decay of w across B. Keys: ball_eddy (scale, default 1, 0 = off), ball_conductivity_s_per_m (4.5e6, chrome steel). Hysteresis loss is not modelled.
 * IRON CORE, core_model = cells (DEFAULT since 2026-10-18; src/physics/magnetcore.cpp): the core's answer to the ball from tables built at load, per magnet:
   - core: 4 x 30 axisymmetric cells of saturating steel (b_sat_t, initial susceptibility 500), coil on a 1 mm bobbin round the core, its build from wire_mm and turns,
     winding length core_length_mm, the core reaching core_exposed_mm (default 6) above the winding to the pole face; solved by Newton (coil alone and with one ball on the axis);
   - bare field: coil + coil-only core read out smoothly into an (r, z) table above the face to 127 mm; the ball's pull in it = linear ball (mu 200) to order 4 on an inner sphere;
   - core reaction: the on-axis reaction R(h) (cell solve with the ball, 6 gaps 0.1-10.1 mm) carried off the axis by the equivalent height h* of a linear flux-tube law
     (face + exposed side, shortest air path; P(x, h) = P_axis(h*)): down = R(h*) dh*/dz, toward the axis = R(h*) dh*/dx;
   - ball saturation: factor on the total from the linear gap field at h* (FEM fit, 3 cores x 3 currents, gaps 0.05-1 mm);
   - rim rule (down force only): walking outward from 5.5 mm inside to 2.5 mm outside the face edge, each point must lie between its corrected inner neighbour and the
     outer value, else it is interpolated linearly (removes the saturated-rim overshoot of the linear tubes);
   - tables per operating level: operating_volts = list of coil voltages (default rated_volts; as many as wanted, about 0.2 s load each, built on parallel threads);
     at run time the force is interpolated linearly in the coil current between the levels (0 A = no force; above the top level extrapolated);
   - per ball per cycle: a table read (0.1 us offline) within 30 mm of the axis and 30 mm above the face; beyond it the order-4 pull in the bare field (about 9 us).
   Accuracy (c19 core, 3D FEM with a saturating ball, NI 2000 / 6200 / 11100): down force within 0.91-1.14 on the axis, the 45 deg approach and at 4 and 12-18 mm offset,
   rim 8-10 mm 0.93-1.05 at NI 6200-11100 but 0.57-0.59 at NI 2000 (the rim rule cuts a real small rise there; accepted); centring 0.6-1.3 (best beyond the edge).
   Limits: a bare rod only (return_* yoke keys are ignored with an on-screen notice; core_model = circuit keeps the old model with yokes); tables for the standard
   13.49 mm ball (another size: notice, approximate pull); gaps below 0.1 mm read as 0.1 mm; one ball per core (two balls sharing the face steel are not modelled;
   ball pairs: BALL-BALL below); the rim-corrected down force and the centring force do not come from one energy (a ball circling the rim may gain
   a little energy: to check in play); static (no eddy currents in the core); only the c19 core checked against FEM.
 * BALL-BALL (all core models): induced point dipoles, each sized from the field at its centre (the old model), between the GATED balls only: a ball takes part where an
   energised magnet's field at its centre is >= 0.665 mT (a touching pair in that field pulls ~8 mN, ~1 % of the ball's weight). Known errors (offline, c19 NI 6200 vs 3D FEM):
   neighbours of the plug ball get about half the pull, and two balls side by side on the face repel weakly (FEM: they attract, ~19 N). The multipole model
   (ball_model = multipole, 2026-10-19) was REMOVED the same day (over budget from 4 balls up; orbiting balls gained energy because its pair force was held
   for the whole 1 ms cycle). Its code is kept in git commit c7921fc7f. A lumped magnetic network was built (2026-10-20, never committed) and removed the same day;
   the successor is Option A (docs/unified-physics/BallMagnetOptionABuildPlan.txt): BEING BUILT. Step B1 (2026-10-20): core_model = cells magnets with the axis up use the s19
   Option A code again (magnetballs.cpp, unchanged) for the gated balls; known issues of that code stand until steps B2-B7 (pair fix only below 1 mm,
   cost over budget from 4 balls, force held for the 1 ms cycle: orbiting balls may gain energy).
 * IRON CORE, core_model = circuit (the model before 2026-10-18):
 * Field: the pole face is a charged disc (24 point charges); the opposite pole is one charge behind the coil, or, with a bracket, charges on the flanges.
   B and grad|B| are summed from the point charges; the field is finite at the face.
 * Ball: induced dipole, M = min(3|B|/mu0, Ms); force = ball volume * M * grad|B|; acceleration = force / (steel mass * Ball.Mass).
 * Applied in HitBall::ExternalVelocityIncrement; inside the ring/playfield contact substeps it is re-evaluated at each substep position (the pull follows the ball;
   evaluating it once per cycle fed energy into a ball swinging in the well). Zero cost while no coil carries current. Balls farther than 5 in (127 mm)
   from the pole face are skipped: the pull there is below rolling resistance.
POSITION CONVENTION: for the iron-core magnet x, y, z is the centre of the pole face (the end of the core facing the ball) and the axis points out of the face toward the ball; for
the air-core coil (type = aircore, below) x, y, z is the centre of the coil, the middle of its length, and the axis is the direction of the bore.
Settings (full documentation in the [Magnet:<name>] section of vpx_physics.ini): x, y, z (pole face, table units; z = 0 playfield surface, -35.3 U under a 3/4 in
playfield), axis_x/y/z, turns, wire_mm, resistance_ohm, core_diameter_mm, core_length_mm, core_model (cells | circuit), core_exposed_mm, operating_volts, b_sat_t, ball_ms_a_per_m, ball_eddy, ball_conductivity_s_per_m, rated_volts (default 48; the supply MagnetDuty is a fraction of), return_fraction (0 bare rod .. 1 perfect
iron return), and for a bracket: return_pole_distance_mm, return_pole_width_mm, return_pole_length_mm, return_pole_count (1 | 2 | 4), return_pole_angle_deg, return_pole_depth_mm.
Reference coil (Gorgar, estimate): 650 turns of 22 AWG, about 3.2 ohm computed (4.5 ohm was a high estimate), 3/4 in iron core, 28 V DC, on for about 750 ms.
Status: built and tested on the Rubber Test 2 table. The pull-in times are not yet checked against a real machine; the flux model, the bracket pole positions and
sizes are estimates. Not measured: per-step cost.
A ball released near the pole swings through it and settles by rolling resistance and the normal load the pull adds (the vertical component of the pull is
   a load on the playfield: friction scales with it). Eddy-current braking of a spinning ball is modelled (above); magnetic hysteresis loss in the ball is not.
Air-core coil ("ball lock" or coil gun), type = aircore (the old name solenoid still works): a coil with no iron the ball rolls through. Same circuit (R from the winding, L = mu0 pi a^2 N^2 / (l + 0.9 a)),
same script calls. Field: the full field of the winding, 3 layers x 8 rings of current loops (exact elliptic-integral loop field). Ball response (2026-10-17): the lone ball's
multipole response to order 4, not a point dipole. For a steel ball (M = 3H in a uniform field) the energy is E = sum_l a^(2l+1) Q_l (l = 1..4), Q_l = 2 pi mu0 (2l+1) <p_l^2>,
p_l = the degree-l part of the coil's scalar potential about the ball centre; Q_1 is the old dipole law (F = V M grad|B|). Q_l belongs to the field alone, so one table serves every
ball radius. The table (1 mm grid over axial and radial distance, 127 mm each way) holds grad Q_1..Q_4 per A^2 and Bz, Br per A; Q_2..Q_4 come from the coil field on a sphere round
each node projected on spherical harmonics (quadrature chosen per node; exact near the winding and on a 4 mm lattice elsewhere, far nodes take Q_l/Q_1 from the lattice), built
over threads at load (C1: 33-41 ms on 16-20 threads, 182 ms on one; the dipole table took 8.4 ms; the time is logged). A step is still one table lookup: F = I^2 sum_l a^(2l+1) grad Q_l.
Ball saturation: the response is scaled by min(1, mu0 Ms / 3B) at the ball centre (exact for the dipole term, approximate for l >= 2). Accuracy (C1, 650 turns of 0.7 mm, 40 mm,
32 mm bore, 48 V, offline, runs/2026-10-17-aircore): on the axis the dipole was 10 % too strong inside the coil and 14-21 % too weak just outside it (26-50 mm from the centre);
order 4 is within 0.1 % of the converged series on the axis and within 1.7 % of order 8 in force at every point checked (worst beside the winding's outer corner); the table's
force (1 mm differences, bilinear) is within 0.2-0.45 % of the analytic order-4 force on the axis and within 1 % beside the winding. The energy to pull a ball out from the centre
changed by only 0.3 % (the dipole errors cancel), so lock strength is about the same; the force profile along the bore is what changed. A ball inside the winding block (nothing
solid there; no tube or track) keeps the dipole term only, because the series does not hold with steel in the winding: the engine raises a playfield notification and a log WARN
once per table start. Toward the centre of the bore. Keys: x, y, z (the CENTRE of the coil, the middle of its length), axis_x/y/z, coil_length_mm,
bore_diameter_mm, turns, wire_mm, resistance_ohm. Test (28 V, 650 turns of 0.7 mm, 40 mm long, 32 mm bore, slope 6 deg): a ball rolling in at 0.3 m/s is held when the coil
is switched on at the centre; with the coil on from the start it speeds to about 1 m/s in the bore and leaves with the energy (a conservative well). Limits: a ball in the coil is held along the axis but not sideways (Earnshaw), so it needs a track or tube; to lift a ball off the table the coil must sit above it with its lower end clear of the ball top (z = 50 for a ball at z = 25), so the centre is at least 50 + half the coil length (about z = 92 for a 40 mm coil). The ball's effect on the inductance and the back-EMF of its motion are not modelled; the same ball-loss limits as the iron magnet.
Guide tube and stop (2026-10-11, air-core only): x, y, z is the coil CENTRE. A plastic tube as long as the coil sits in the bore (outside diameter = bore_diameter_mm, wall tube_wall_mm = 1/16 in) and confines the ball (impulse contact: tube_restitution 0.5, tube_friction 0.2, spin included; no bounce below 0.05 m/s). A stop across the bore at stop_position_mm from the coil centre (signed along the axis; default the end of the bore) ends the ball's travel. Keys tube (default 1), stop (default 0) = 1/0; tube_visible = 1 draws the tube and stop as translucent surfaces (default 0). The air-core field-table build time is logged at load.
Winding housing (2026-10-17, part of the tube: same keys): with tube = 1 the winding sits in a solid housing (a bobbin) so a ball can reach the field only through the
bore. Its size comes from the winding: outer radius = bore/2 + winding build (layers x wire_mm) + tube_wall_mm; end flanges tube_wall_mm beyond the winding at each end, the
tube running through them. Tube and housing are one solid (in the coil's meridian plane the block from the tube's inside to the housing's outside): the ball touches it
where the distance from its centre to that block is under its radius, so the inner wall, outer wall, end flanges and rims all hold, with tube_restitution and
tube_friction. tube_visible draws the housing with the tube (for placement and demonstration; drawn at its true position, also the part below the playfield).
Test (C1 on Rubber Test 2, runs/2026-10-09-slow-contact/run_ac_house_2, aircore_housing.vbs): a ball rolled at the side (coil off, and coil on) and an off-axis ball
rolled at an end flange stop on the housing (rebound about 0.5) and never come within the housing; the on-axis pull-in is unchanged; no "ball inside the winding" notice.
Limits: no funnel (a ball not lined up with the bore bounces off the flange); impulse contact once per physics cycle, so a ball faster than about one housing wall
build per cycle (about 10 m/s for C1) could cross the solid; the housing is a plain cylinder (no mounting bracket).
Known limits: flux sees the ball only through the gap term; force evaluated at the ball centre; no yoke saturation; bracket flanges are rectangles parallel to the pole face.

10. TURNTABLES  ([Features] turntables; [Turntable:<name>])   -- added 2026-10-11
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A motor-driven disc set into a hole in the playfield (or a lower / upper playfield), like the Whirlwind discs. Defined in the ini for now (an editor object later).
ONLY USED WITH [Features] playfield_contact = 1 (the disc is a surface of the playfield's force model and the playfield is cut round it); with it off the sections are ignored and a warning is logged.
Script (table-global, like Magnet):
   Turntable "T1", 12              motor voltage in volts (signed: negative reverses); 0 = motor off, the disc coasts down
   TurntableDuty "T1", 0.5         PWM drive: mean voltage = duty x supply_volts (-1 .. 1)
   rpm = TurntableSpeed("T1")      speed in rpm, positive clockwise and negative counter-clockwise (seen from above), whatever the direction key
   deg = TurntableAngle("T1")      angle in degrees (0 .. 360), measured clockwise from screen right plus ref_angle_deg, whatever the direction key: it counts up while the disc turns clockwise, down while it turns counter-clockwise
Model (src/physics/turntable.h; the disc as a moving part on the group step: src/physics/discgroup.h, contactdiscpart.h, since 2026-11-02):
 * Disc: inertia I (a solid disc of mass_g, or inertia_kgm2), viscous bearing drag b from ramp_down_s (max_rpm to 5% of it, motor off, no load; the exponential never quite stops).
   [Contact] pivot_model 1 (default; 2026-11-04): the inertia is derived, not a key. The disc slides at T_slide = mu_dynamic_ratio x friction_nm, and the friction alone gives the spin-down:
   I = T_slide x ramp_down_s / (0.95 w0), w0 = max_rpm; drag b = 0. Floor: I is at least the disc's own, a 1/8 in steel plate of radius_mm (fixed in code); when the floor applies the
   drag b makes up the rest of ramp_down_s. The motor holds max_rpm against T_slide (Ts = k w0 + T_slide). mass_g and inertia_kgm2 no longer act (one WARN). So inertia scales with
   friction_nm at a fixed spin-down: friction_nm sets how firmly the stopped disc holds against a ball. Log: "Turntable <name>: inertia ... sliding torque ... drag ...; floor applied / no floor".
   pivot_model 0: as before (mass_g / inertia_kgm2, drag from the full friction_nm).
 * Motor: DC motor, linear torque-speed curve: T = Ts V / supply_volts - k w. max_rpm is the speed the disc settles at with supply_volts and no load, so the running speed is
   proportional to the voltage. Ts follows from ramp_up_s (time to 95%) or is given as stall_torque_nm. V = 0 is an unpowered motor (drag only). A PWM drive is its mean
   voltage: the winding inductance and the disc inertia filter the switching.
 * Friction: a constant (Coulomb) torque friction_nm of the bearing and gear train opposes the rotation and holds a still disc until a ball's push (or the motor) exceeds it. The motor torque
   is raised by it so max_rpm is still reached at supply_volts, and the viscous drag is solved so ramp_down_s still holds with it (if the friction alone stops the disc sooner than ramp_down_s the coast-down is that sooner time: the log says so; more inertia lengthens it); a motor voltage below what overcomes friction does not turn the disc.
 * Balls: the disc is a moving part on the group step (DiscGroup::Part on BodyPart, one unknown, as gates and spinners; DiscModel::Engine for the engine), with the shared
   contact law (Hertz + damping + Coulomb friction). Its points move at w x r, so friction carries a ball along; the equal and opposite force and the couples of every ball on the disc turn
   the disc in the same solve (the group step; an island with a disc always goes to the group). With no ball near, the disc advances alone and sleeps when the motor is off and it is
   still. Its shape is probed as a flat disc (and a side when it stands proud) with the rim edge rounded like any face edge (edge_radius_mm), not as 96 faces. The disc has the same rolling resistance and twist (drilling friction) as every surface; the twist acts on the ball's spin relative to the disc, so a ball resting on a turning disc is slowly spun up about the vertical axis to the disc's speed (about 5 s at 250 rpm, set by the contact patch). Load, slip, spin-up and throw-off are results, not settings; mu is the texture.
 * Hole: the playfield (plane or mesh) is cut round the disc (radius + gap_mm, 1/16 in) for the contact, so a ball over the gap rests on the two rims. A disc higher than the playfield
   (z > playfield_z) has a vertical rim. The playfield slope acts as everywhere else (gravity in the table frame).
 * Magnets and other forces act on balls on the disc as anywhere; the disc does not act on a magnet.
Settings: x, y (centre, table units), z (height of the disc top, default = the playfield surface nearest outside the disc's edge, i.e. flush: the median over 24 directions, a direction with no surface has no say, so a mesh hole of any size or place is fine), playfield_z (the surface the hole is cut in, default = the playfield surface at the disc centre: found from the playfield faces, a mesh playfield's top is not always at 0), radius_mm 76.2, gap_mm 1.5875,
direction cw | ccw (what a positive voltage does, seen from above; default cw), mass_g 800, inertia_kgm2 (0 = from the mass), max_rpm 250 (Whirlwind: 200 - 300), supply_volts 12,
ramp_up_s 1.0 (rest to 95% of max_rpm), ramp_down_s 4.0 (max_rpm to 5%, motor off; not shorter than ramp_up_s), friction_nm 0.02 (hold-still torque; 0 = a ball moves a still disc freely), stall_torque_nm (0 = from ramp_up_s); old names free_rpm, rated_volts, spinup_s, coast_s still read, mu 0.6, ref_angle_deg 0 (angle reference, degrees clockwise from screen right), visible 0, gap_visible 1,
marker spokes | stripe | dot | none (visible only: a pattern that turns with the disc, so the spin can be seen; a plain disc looks the same at every angle).
rolling_resistance 0.03 (added 2026-10-12): rolling-resistance coefficient of a ball on the disc surface (torque = coefficient x R x N). A textured rubber disc rolls a ball far harder than the wooden playfield (0.003,
[Contact] rolling_resistance); a negative value uses the [Contact] one. It is what lets a ball on a spinning disc reach the disc's speed instead of settling at a fraction of it.
Limits: geometry and motor keys are read when the table loads (no hot reload); the playfield TEXTURE is not cut, the gap is drawn as a dark ring above it; all values are UNVERIFIED
estimates (the 800 g platter, 0.6 friction, 1 s spin-up). Test of the friction: a ball sliding across the still disc at 1.5 m/s turned it 21.5 degrees with friction_nm 0 and 0.3 degrees with 0.02. Test (blank table, 250 rpm): 95% speed in 0.999 s; a ball dropped at 100 U from the centre dips the speed about 3% and
rides the disc at the no-slip speed (rim speed / 3.5); 60-throw fall-through audits over the rim and gap of a flush and a raised disc: none below the playfield.
Cost: one ball on a disc adds about 5 us to the mean collision step (measured, one ball, unfocused harness).

12. ARRIVAL-TIME SUBSTEPS  ([Contact] arrival_step)   -- added 2026-10-13
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Since 2026-10-31 the rule looks at every face or segment of a surface the ball can reach in the step, not only the nearest (ArrivalS).
Why: a substep that carries a ball into a surface is wrong whatever happens afterwards. The contact force is computed at the START of a substep, so a step that reaches a surface with no force on the
way in starts the next one already inside it, and the force law (Hertz, tens of N per micrometre) then pushes the ball out with energy it never had. Measured on a launch seen in play: a ball
leaving a flipper's tip fell 2.5 U onto the playfield and was stepped once per cycle (1 ms); it sank 28 um with no force, the next substep held 247 N for 1 ms, +3 m/s (21.8 m/s in play).
Rule (islands of balls and static surfaces; islands with a bat or band keep the uniform substeps for now (gates, spinners and bumpers joined 2026-10-27, turntable discs 2026-10-28); a gate or spinner sets them from its own rule, section 13):
 * At the start of every substep each ball is probed once against the surfaces of its contact (the probe serves the step choice and the forces).
 * A surface that is not touching bounds the step by the time to get within 0.5 um of it: distance, closing speed and the acceleration the ball can pick up (gravity and nudge, 60 m/s2 with a
   magnet, last substep's contact force), with the integrator's own displacement for a constant acceleration (semi-implicit Euler moves the ball a h^2, not a h^2 / 2). Inside the 1 um touch gap
   the step lands on the surface exactly. A ball with nothing near takes the rest of the cycle as ONE step.
 * A touching surface bounds the step by its stiffness at the compression it will reach (Hertz k = 2 E* sqrt(R delta), the depth from the speed of the contact motion, either direction): omega * h <= 0.2,
   or <= 0.8 for a quasi-static contact (not moving, not deeper than 3 x the depth its weight presses it to). At most 100 us. A rolling contact is also limited by roll_travel_um.
 * Ball pairs keep their own rule (their step for the cycle is the pair rule's), as the cap of the loop.
 * Rewind: a substep that started a contact deeper than max(arrival_depth_um, a quarter of the compression the impact will reach), or that tripped the model's range, is taken again from its start
   with the step cut to the share before the touch (state restored: balls, shear springs and touching flags, pair states, queued events). Every rewind is logged (WARN, once per 500 ms):
   which contact, the depth, how far away the substep started, the closing speed, the old and new step. The aim is no rewinds; the log says what causes the rest.
Measured (drop test, rest, rolling and magnet-pair scenes against the uniform steps): the same impacts (restitution to 1%: 0.830 against the converged 0.829 on the first impact, 19 contact substeps
instead of 65; large impacts 0.771>0.513 / 1.228>0.698 / 2.017>0.914 on both), the same resting heights and positions, cost at or below (rest 8 -> 8 us per cycle, rolling 15 -> 7, drop 13 -> 7,
worst cycle 209 -> 116 us), at most 26 substeps in a cycle (201 on every impact before), 0 rewinds in all of them. Rewind test (VPX_ARRIVAL_NOSPLIT): 181 rewinds, the same impacts.
Bugs found on the way, each from the log or the trace: the impact limit chosen from the CLOSING speed fell to the loose limit on the rebound (restitution 0.93 instead of 0.83); the loose limit at the
turning point of an impact stepped through the force peak; the arrival bound used a h^2 / 2 for a semi-implicit integrator (3 um late on every long approach, found by the rewind log).
Not done: islands with moving parts (a bat's own surface speed and acceleration need a bound of their own; bat contacts step at 10 - 20 us today); the unlogged case of a contact starting
inside a moving surface; ball-ball arrival (the pair rule's step is kept).


13. GATES AND SPINNERS  (unified engine, VPX_UNIFIED=1; [Features] hinges; [Gates], [Gate:<name>], [Spinners], [Spinner:<name>])   -- added 2026-10-15
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Design and measurements: docs/unified-physics/GateSpinnerDesign.txt, docs/unified-physics/GroupStepDesign.txt 21. Code: src/physics/bodypart.h (one body on one coordinate: mass, speed, stops, friction), hingegroup.h (shape, gravity, pin friction), gategroup.h, spinnergroup.h, contacthingepart.h (engine side), contactgate.h,
contactspinner.h, contacthingebody.h (masses, the switch crank), contactpartsini.h (keys). A gate or spinner is a physical part of the contact system, moved with its balls in the group step's one solve (2026-11-02): no old loop, no stock collider, mover, line segment or bracket circles.

14. JET BUMPERS  (unified engine; [Bumpers], [Bumper:<name>])   -- added 2026-10-26, on the group step 2026-11-02
Design: docs/unified-physics/JetBumperDesign.txt, runs/2026-10-31-bumper-design/design.md, docs/unified-physics/GroupStepDesign.txt section 20. A bumper on a unified table is always this model, moved with the balls in one solve on the group step: no other path, no hand-back to stock. Stock bumpers exist only on a unified = 0 table.
[Features] bumpers = 0, VPX_BUMPERS=0 and the keys physical, own_step_us, spoon_mass_g, spoon_travel_mm are IGNORED: a WARN in the log and one notification name them.
A skirt that rocks on its base rim (3 coordinates, a spring under it), a stem whose tip pushes a spoon (a massless spring on the follower distance, not a body), a steel ring on a plunger driven by the coil (AE-23-800 by default), a fixed centre body. The
ball touches the skirt, the ring and the body with the contact law of every other surface; the kick comes out of coil, ring mass and contact, nothing is added to the ball. Only the coil adds energy. The stock hit circle, ring animation and skirt animation are not used.
Switch: closed when the follower distance reaches switch_close_mm (default 0.79375 mm = 1/32 in, user 2026-11-02; up to 3.18 mm = 1/8 in = less sensitive); Hit fires when it closes (no speed threshold), then
Switch(1); Switch(0) when it opens.

Script: Bumper.Solenoid enabled[, volts[, duty]] (external: coil on/off; pulse: True fires one pulse; held: ignored with a notification). Read-only: RotX, RotY (skirt tilt, degrees),

CoilCurrent (A). Event Bumper_Switch(state) 1 closed / 0 open; Bumper_Hit as before (HitEvent property respected). Collidable = False: the bumper is not there at all (no contact, switch,
coil or events; parts at rest; Solenoid refused). Radius, Force, Threshold, Scatter, HeightScale, RingSpeed and RingDropOffset are IGNORED (one notification per table start).
