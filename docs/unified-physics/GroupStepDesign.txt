GROUP STEP DESIGN: the engine moves balls and parts together (ProjectAssessment-2026-10-27.txt item 0 b)
==========================================================================================================
Started 2026-10-29 with the user, one piece at a time. Replaces the band wiring of UnifiedContactDesign.txt 12.3 (written for the old node band).
Decision (user, 2026-10-29): no part moves its own balls. The engine moves every ball and part of a touching group together, with the band's discrete-gradient
solve (docs/unified-physics/BandCordDesign.txt section 3: forces from exact energy differences, losses on mid-step speeds, drives as counted work). The band cord (src/physics/
bandcord.h, proved offline) is the first part. Gates, spinners, bumpers, the turntable disc and the flipper bats keep today's coupling (force out, reaction a step
later) until each moves over with its own offline proof (to-do 1.3).

History, build logs and replaced sections: archive/GroupStepDesign_moved.txt. Status and dates: git history.

REQUIREMENTS (what the group step must do; reviewed with the user 2026-11-07)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. One path: every ball that touches anything, and every moving part, is stepped by the group step. No private stepping, no stock for converted objects.
2. Energy by construction: energy enters only through drives (coil, motor, spring preload, gravity, nudge). No step adds energy, at any step length.
3. Always solve: every step converges. A step that cannot solve is a fault, not a cost; no ball may ever stick.
4. Cost: at most 30 us in each 1 ms cycle for an object with its ball(s), worst cycle counted; frame budget in DevelopmentRules 5.
5. Accuracy: within 5 % per hit of a 1 us fixed-step reference on the 750-hit bench (the limit itself is open: to-do 8.1).
6. Repeatable: the same inputs give the same result; the snapshot self-test shows 0 differences.
7. Once per cycle: the group step runs once per 1 ms physics cycle.


THE MODEL
---------

== A. What a part supplies and the interface ==

1. What a part supplies
-----------------------
The part describes itself; the engine does all the moving.
   1. Bodies: what can move (positions, angles) and the mass or inertia of each; inertia may depend on position (the sling plate's I(phi)).
   2. Stored energy at any position (springs, cord stretch, squash), with the EXACT energy difference between two positions, not only a force: that is what keeps
      the books closed at any step.
   3. Stiffness now: a rough value, for the step length and the solve's first guess (the Newton Jacobian).
   4. Losses: forces from mid-step speeds that can only remove energy (damping, friction, rolling resistance, with their limits).
   5. Drives (coils, motors): forces whose work is counted.
   6. Contacts with balls: where a ball touches and how close it is (geometry). The law is the shared rubber / steel law restated as stored energy plus losses.
   7. Hard stops (the sling's coil stop): the step ends exactly on them; they are inelastic.
   8. An end-of-step update that changes no energy (the band re-routing round a post: the cord length is C1 through it, so no energy jump).
   9. Save and restore of its state, and its events (as today: SaveState / RestoreState, the event list).
The engine: forms the touching groups, picks the step length, solves the group's moves together, runs the shared guards and the energy books.
Fixed surfaces (playfield, walls, posts) supply only 6 and 7, with no bodies. Ball-ball contact is a 6 too. A ball on the band that also rolls on the playfield and
touches another ball is in one solve, with no lag between contacts.

18. One interface for moving parts
A moving part on the group step states: its coordinates (each free, with an unknown, or given, moved by a drive or a stop); for each surface it carries, how the
contact depth and the contact point's speed change as each coordinate moves; its step facts (stiffness, approach, arrival, turn, time left, time constant, field),
from which the engine picks the step length; and its stops, which end or shorten a step. The work a given move does on the balls is booked as a drive; a part
books only the change of its own stored energy. The engine does the solving. Code: groupstep.h (GroupStep::Part, StepFactList), contactmovingpart.h.


== B. How groups form ==

2. How groups form
------------------
Balls and parts that can touch within the cycle are solved together as one group (contactsystem.cpp, island_reach and ball_pair_reach, both on by default).
A ball joins a moving part's group only if it can reach the part's surface within the cycle. Two balls share a group when their gap can close within the cycle at
their speed bounds, or when they are on the same band. Fixed surfaces (playfield, walls, posts) link no balls: they are contacts of each ball inside its group.
Open (to-do N5): the reach must come from physics, speed plus force; today a touched gate counts its whole swing (81 mm on Gate001) as one cycle's travel.


== C. Contact: push-back and sideways grip ==

4. The contact's push-back (normal direction) as stored energy plus losses
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stored energy: the squash law as a closed-form energy at any depth (Hertz + the thin bonded layer at the thickness left; the same formula the band uses); post and wall rubbers get the identical law; steel (playfield, metal walls, ball-ball) is plain Hertz, the same formula with no layer.
Losses: every damping term is a force from the mid-step speed, so it only removes energy: the rubber's squash damping (alpha, 0.18), the hard surfaces' calibrated
viscoelastic damping and their flat loss (normal_loss).
A contact found in the middle of a step cannot give back more than went in: the step uses the exact energy difference.

5. Sideways grip
----------------
Every contact of the shared law keeps the sideways (shear) spring: post and ring rubbers, rubber walls and primitives, the flipper rubber,
and steel (playfield, metal walls, ball-ball; there the spring is stiff and the touch short, so the effect is small). Rubber gives sideways about two thirds as
much as inward; a ball gripping a post on a glancing hit winds the spring up and is pushed back as it leaves (spin reversal, the superball effect). Dropping it
would change how balls leave posts with no data to justify it.
Energy: the wind-up is stored energy (its stiffness follows the squash depth, inside the energy, so nothing leaks as the ball leaves); slip past the friction
limit is a loss that can only remove energy. Twist and rolling resistance stay losses.
Whether the band now uses the same spring law is to be checked (to-do 3.0).

16. A slide does not need its spring resolved
   16.2 The law. Coulomb, with the spring's energy. A spring within mu N holds at most (mu N)^2 / (2 kt). A slip whose 1/2 mt u^2 is more than that cannot be stopped by the spring, so the contact slides, and its force is mu N whatever kt is. So: slip speed above mu N / sqrt(kt mt) = at the friction limit. A contact that sticks cannot read as sliding, because its vibration's peak slip is omega_t x stretch, at most that speed. No state and no constant; it needs position and velocity only, so the engine's group, rebuilt each cycle, carries nothing new.
   Proof (MEASURED, main's headers, rolling ball, ring at 35 U): 93 - 100 % of the steps the playfield's spring set follow a sliding step (0.32 - 3 m/s). Fixed 1 us, the playfield's shear stiffness x 0.25 / x 1 / x 4: rebound .9604 / .9608 / .9611 at 0.32 m/s, .8933 / .8934 / .8935 at 0.86, .6765 / .6757 / .6754 at 3.
   16.3 The rule. Only the steady test changes (Group::ChooseStep, Settings::slideFree): a contact is steady when its compression rate is within vSteady and its slip is within vSteady or at the friction limit. A steady contact takes omegaHRest on its normal stiffness, as before.
   An impact stays strict, shear included. A first version left the shear out of impacts too and was dropped on data: gs_test wall1_a0 went from +1.01 % to +7.43 % of its 1 us exit, because the shear's shorter step was also what resolved the hard normal hit.
   16.4 Back to sticking. Below the limit speed the spring can catch the slip. If the slip is then within vSteady the old steady rule takes it (playfield at rest: the limit speed is 0.92 mm/s against vSteady 50 mm/s, so there is no strict band between them). If not, the strict rule comes back. The change happens at most one step late, inside an implicit step whose energy is exact. Trace at 3 m/s: slip .1317 -> .0083 m/s under load, next steps rule 2 at 30 us.
   16.5 Hunt-Crossley over the step. With longer steps the damper sampled at the mid-step force lost too little, as h^2 (rebound high). Its coefficient now takes the mean spring force over the step (the chord the step already computes): the loss is 1.5 alpha x (V1 - V0) x the rate, the law's own integral at a steady rate. No new evaluation, c >= 0, the energy book unchanged, 1 us results unchanged. Item::DampC.
   16.9 Since 2026-11-05 the shear spring's step follows the motion ([Contact] shear_step_motion, on); under review in to-do N11.


== D. Step length, guards and the hard step ==

6. Step length and the guards that stay
---------------------------------------
Step length by accuracy only (the step cannot make energy at any length): the stiffest body of the group, omega h <= omega_h (0.6 from the band sweep; steel
impacts get their own value from their offline proof); a surface being closed on is reached in about two steps; a ball turns at most 2 deg per step round a band
or post; a step ends exactly on a hard stop and at the end of the 1 ms cycle.
Guards that stay: engagement (closing speed plus the part's speed bound); the sweep against tunnelling through thin walls; the rule for a ball found already
overlapping a surface (the seat); halve-and-retry from the saved state when a step's solve does not settle; the group's energy books closing every cycle, a
failure shown on screen (rule 8).
Guards that go for groups on the new step: the extra rewinds and depth checks that existed only to catch energy being made.
Today's step settings ([Contact] step_model 1, step_swing_cap, shear_step_motion, term_arrival, damp_dir_slope; all on) refine these rules; they are under
review in to-do N11.

15. A hard step, with no stock
   15.1 A ball joins the cord before it touches. Today a band is left out of the near pass (contactsystem.cpp: o->m_set.band), so a ball joins only when the stock hit test finds it. CordNear takes it when it can reach the cord within two cycles, or lies within the release gap, every ball included, one held for the playfield too. The bench starts the group before touch and books no gain at the join; the engine starts it at the hit and gains (53 of 134 joining cycles on Gorgar rose, MEASURED; that the late start is why is INFERRED, to be tested).
   15.2 No stock for a cord. HitRubber::HitTest returns no hit for a band on the cord, so Collide, its stock impulse, the cooldown, the seat refusal and the give-way to a flipper cannot be reached. Routes 1 - 4 of 12.2 go for a cord. A ball near a flipper now stays with the cord: the group treats that flipper as still.
   15.3 A hard step. A step that cannot be solved no longer lets the ball fly on. The balls stay as they are for the rest of the cycle (time lost, at most 1 ms a cycle, no energy can appear) and the next cycle tries again. On screen once (rule 8). If it fails 20 cycles in a row the cord lets go, which only takes energy away (a net, not the fix).
   15.4 The band's Hit event, fired by Collide until now, comes from the cycle a ball first comes onto the cord (closing speed along the cord's push), after the step.


== E. Surfaces ==

17. Static surfaces on the group step (on by default since 2026-11-03: [Contact] static_model)

17.1 Push only. Law: Hertz with no adhesion; a contact pushes and cannot pull. N = max(0, fe + fd). The spring's chord goes on being counted: normal loss is (N - fe)(d1 - d0), which is cD (d1 - d0)^2 / h while it pushes, and all the spring's release once damping would make it pull. Where it does not push, the Jacobian's normal term is 0. The energy balance holds exactly either way, so an energy test cannot see the fault: a rebound against the law's integral does. Wall set to 0.40 at 1 m/s: 0.40. At 3 m/s: 0.342 (law 0.341). At 6 m/s: 0.306 (law 0.305).

17.2 Steady by motion. A contact is steady only if its motion says so: depth rate within vSteady (0.02 m/s), net force over the contact's time 1/omega within vSteady, and slip within vSteady or at the friction limit. A contact not touching yet counts as steady only if closing at most vSteady. A rate alone reads a hit's turnaround as steady.

17.3 A fold. Two parts of one surface pressing a ball (the arms of a fold, a concave corner of a mesh) are two Hertz contacts, and their energies add: one depth d = (dA^2.5 + dB^2.5)^0.4 on depths from zero, gradient (dA^1.5 nA + dB^1.5 nB) / d^1.5, a part's share fading as its nearest point nears its end, so it leaves with no jump (rubbercontact.h FoldSum, ContactSurface::ProbePatch, Hit::grad). With one nearest face the push direction has no root at a fold and an implicit step cannot rest there. Resting on a fold: 0.00002 m/s, no creep. At static_model 0 it changes nothing on p1, p3, gp2 and rings (byte-identical with and without).

17.4 The zero, by cause. Law: moving a position does no work, so it may store nothing. A depth rise with no step goes to the contact's zero; the energy is not stored and there is no launch. Who moved the ball decides what it means. Silent: placed by a script or the test driver (ContactSystem::NotePlaced); a hand-over from the old loop (RingHandIn; deleted with that loop). Fault, rule 8, once per cause and surface on screen: moved outside any step (a stock part), inside at first touch, the faces in reach changed (DepthFault). Measured causes so far: a trigger's forward move (collideex.cpp:748), a locked ball's HitBall::Collide, LineSeg::Collide at the table edge, HitPoint after a ball placed inside a band. Each goes away when its part is converted.

17.5 Exact state. Writing the state to VPX's floats does no work, so it must not move the ball. On the static path the exact position stands while the position's floats are unchanged; speed and spin are what the engine has now plus what rounding took off them when written (stock adds gravity between cycles, so comparing them would never hold). It is part of the ball's snapshot.

17.6 No limits. No limit on balls, shapes, items, unknowns, parts or a part's size (Part::MaxDof). Storage is sized in Group::Size, AddShape, AddPart and Configure, between cycles, never in a step. Shapes and parts go in only through AddShape and AddPart: a direct write does not compile. Every index into the group's storage is checked in any build without NDEBUG (the offline benches). A cord keeps BandCord::kMaxBalls (8): past it the other balls are left out with a WARN; to lift it the cord needs its per-ball arrays sized the same way. 18 touching balls: all on the group, no failed step, EnergyStep 0, energy never rises, top speed the shot's own 2.00 m/s.

17.7 Solver. The round looks at the residual first once a move is below tolCheckMove (tolRes 1e-10, with no factorization); contacts far from touching are skipped and re-checked at the end of the solve (FarStillFar); sparse LU is the only linear solve. EnergyStep's explicit trial may not move a ball further than maxMoveM: its closure kept the energy and moved a ball 4.3 m without that.

14. Rings on one solve
User: reusing the code was a main reason for the rewrite: a ring, a rubber wall or primitive and a band's post use one law and one solve.

   14.1 Which islands. An island where a ball is on a rubber surface (ring, wall or primitive with rubber = 1) goes to the group step, by the same path as an island with a cord: the code in CordIsland already takes any ContactSurface as a shape, so no new contact code and no new law. Switch: [Rubbers] ring_model, on by default (contacthost.h).
   14.4 Hand-over between the loops. (Until the old loop goes, goal G1.) A touch crossing from one loop to the other keeps its shear stretch: st on one side, gsXi on the other, the same stretch. A touch the group has no history of (it arrives inside) counts from zero (Item dZero, already built, 13.4) for every surface, not only posts, so no energy can appear there; a ball handed over mid-squash loses that squash, a dead hit at worst. Rule 7: the joining-cycle gains are not explained (Gorgar 53 of 119 over 1 uJ, largest 2.2 mJ): find the cause first; this may or may not be it. Do not assume.
   14.5 Seat. For a surface going to the group, zero replaces the seat (no position snap), which removes route 3 of 12.2 for it.
   14.6 Collidable. A stop follows the object's Collidable, read live, so a script can switch it. An end post comes from the band's own centre line and stays.

   14.12 The sideways law (2026-10-31, user's approval; runs/2026-10-30-rubber-review, runs/2026-10-31-rubber-law). What 14.11 inferred about the old law's energy was wrong: it is mostly the re-seat with the damper's share. The same law now in both copies: patch rule, no force along the compression for the spring, damper inside mu N, damping from tan delta, sideways stiffness key. The layer at the thickness left, against full thickness in ContactCore, remains a difference (old flipper law: no wall at the rubber's thickness).

13. A band on a post is a rubber on a post
   User: an end post, and a stop post once the band touches it, use the same solve as a ring; we do not rewrite physics. Between a post and the span the band acts as a span for a short distance, then as a rubber on a post, very similar to one (ModelStandards 2d).

   13.1 The contact. Own posts and stop posts become group Shapes, so each ball on one is an ordinary Item. The part no longer computes force for them. Probe: the depth the part uses today (R + r less the distance to the cord's centre line at the post), the normal, the ring's EffectiveRadius; layer as a ring's (layer_mm 0 = the tube's diameter); RubberParams from the same [Rubbers] / [Rubber:<name>] values. Squash code for these is deleted.
   13.2 Friction: one patch, one Coulomb limit. The grip on the cord takes mu x the cord's push only (not the squash); the Item's shear spring takes mu x its own normal force. Together they never exceed mu x the whole push. While a post carries the push, the held arc does not grip the surface as well (that would count the same friction twice).
   13.3 Span to post: no blend, no switch, no distance constant. The ball deflects the cord until the post's Item starts to carry it; the force moves over as the squash grows. CALCULATED, Rubber004 (R 13.5, r 2.16, centre line radius 6.6 mm), head on, deflection of the cord at the ball before the post takes part: 10 mm past the tangent point 2.4 mm, 15 mm 5.8 mm, 20 mm 12.4 mm; at 0 - 5 mm next to nothing. This sets the "short distance". To be measured against a ring.
   13.4 Energy. Item energy is in the group's books. The squash-from-zero rule (bsZero, 12.1, 297 mJ gain without it) moves into the Item, so every surface in the group has it: no stored energy without the work that stored it. A ball stays in the group until the Item's energy has come back (CordGap reads the Item). Between cycles the cord keeps each ball's touch and shear stretch per post, as SurfaceContact does for surfaces. Energy enters only through gravity and the coil, as now.
   13.5 Plate and walls. The moving plate needs a moving-surface Item with the plate's angle in the solve; not in this step. They stay on the part but call the Item's law functions, so no copy of the law is left. The plate has no shear spring until then: exception, on the to-do, model not called done.


== F. The parts ==

19. The bat on the group step

19.1 Moving shape. The bat's outline is a surface whose pose comes from the part's coordinates (BatGroup::Part, batgroup.h: angle, plunger, pivot x). Its Hit fills the columns of 18.1, so the contact's reaction is in the solve. A ball and the bat move in one solve; a bat with no ball is the same group with no balls (GroupStep::AdvanceAlone), asleep while nothing changes.

19.2 Prescribed motion. The pivot's nudge is a coordinate with no unknown: a given move, a drive, carried inside the step, its work on the balls counted (18.2). Coil and nudge are the only drives; the contact, the link's free play and the stops remove energy.

19.3 Far-surface bound. A far surface takes no probe until the ball has moved its gap: a distance cannot shrink faster than the ball moves (FarStillFar). Same bytes, about 10 % cheaper on a pile; not the big lever.

19.4 An island with a group part has no old loop. Whatever else is in it, it goes to the group (IslandHasGroupPart in StepBalls). A step that cannot be solved loses the rest of that cycle, no energy can appear, and says so on screen once.

19.5 What the bat kept of its own: the 1e-5 rad chord (below it the error is under the rounding), winding settled at 1e-3, the solver's maxMove and reach. Turn 2 deg, coil share 0.25, field share 0.25 are Settings, through StepFacts. All else went to the shared rule, one floor of 2 us (the report's table).

20. The jet bumper on the group step

20.1 A surface's Hit needs the depth's exact gradient. The step's correction is second order in the move only if the Hit's normal and grad are the depth's true gradient. A swept radius that tapers makes the normal alone wrong by the taper, and the correction loads the lightest coordinate (the skirt's tilt) with it. Revolve::Near carries gr, gz for this.

20.2 A seat takes the discrete gradient in its own arguments, not one mass-weighted Discrete. A seat's V is a function of the depth a and the tilt's A2. Its gradient is taken in those two, the mean of the two orders of moving them, then each goes onto the coordinates by its own chord. A Gonzalez over all coordinates spreads V's mismatch by mass weight, and near the seat's onset, where the move is as large as the overlap, the tilt, 3,700 times lighter in the weights, took a steep false force from a z move. Measured: with this, no energy step line on stress p1, which had one on a bumper before (notes, last section); the ball's path changed too, so one run.

20.3 The coil's circuit is solved over the trial move (CoilModel::Over). Current and force are a function of the step's start and the move alone, so a retake is identical and the solve holds them. The force closes supplied - lost in R - change in field = F dx at any step length. The coil is the only drive.

20.4 KKStep. Viscoelastic damping over a step is the law's integral over the move at a steady rate, as Hunt-Crossley already is. At the mid-step depth alone it rises as sqrt(d) from a touch's start, a slope with no bound, where Newton stalls. It changes every group contact with gammaKK above 0.

21. Gates and spinners on the group step

21.1 One-unknown parts. BodyPart: one body on one coordinate, its mass, speed, stops and friction. Its row is m (2 dq / h - 2 v) = h Q, so energy closes at any step. The derived part gives stored energy, its own dampers, the friction's limit and its surfaces. It fits a body with one unknown and fixed mass (gate, spinner, disc). It does not fit the bat (inertia varies, two unknowns) or the ring (one coordinate of four, coil force on the solved move); what they could share is the stop's impact and rest law.

21.2 Stops as events. The step ends on a stop; the stop's own Hertz + damping contact gives the rebound from a table by speed. A body whose rebound would not leave the squash its pressing force holds rests, with no unknown. Friction: a body it stops has no unknown while the other forces stay within the limit, so friction's work has one sign. A driven stop is a given move; the part books the stored energy and friction it pays for, the group books its work on the balls.


21.4 V split. Each side of a wire W's V is its own contact, so its own Item. Nearest-only has a force step in the ball's sideways place: no root (114 halvings, 10-31).

21.5 The bend in the Item. Hit::kb: a massless spring in series, far softer than Hertz (wire leg at 25 mm about 7.7 kN/m against 6 MN/m). V(d) = min over u of Hertz(u) + 1/2 kb (d - u)^2. Taken when a touch starts, held while it lasts, across cycles too (SurfaceState::kb, as the radius), so the contact's energy is one function of depth. A probe starts with no bend. 0 for everything else, which then runs the same code path.

21.6 tolEndRes. A step counts as solved only with its residual within this, and a noise stop keeps the point it read: a noise stop could land past a light body's steep onset, 0.45 uJ (10-31). A part asks through Part::EndResidual, Group::Configure takes the largest, 0 with no such part: a table with no gate or spinner keeps its bytes (six_0 md5). A cord, bat or bumper in one group with a gate or spinner takes it for those cycles.

22. The turntable disc on the group step (reviewed with the user 2026-11-07)

22.1 One unknown, nothing stored. The disc is a BodyPart (21.1): one body turning about its vertical axis, the unknown its turn over the step. A disc turning about its own axis maps its surfaces onto themselves: no depth changes with the turn, so it never closes on a ball by its own motion, has no stored energy and no stops.

22.2 Motor. Powered: I dw/dt = Ts V / Vrated - kTot w - Tf sign(w). The motor's torque-speed line is split into a held torque whose work is counted (the drive, the only one) and its slope as a viscous loss. Ts and kTot come from free_rpm at rated volts and either the stall torque or the spin-up time (95 % in three time constants). The script sets the voltage; the speed is an output. The winding's inductance is left out (milliseconds against a 1 s spin-up).

22.3 Bearing. Unpowered: I dw/dt = -b w - Tf sign(w). Tf is the bearing and gear train's Coulomb torque with BodyPart's stick rule: a still disc stays still until the other torques pass it, then slides at mu_dynamic_ratio times it. The stick spring is the 1/4 in steel shaft's torsion, G J / L over 30 mm (user 2026-11-04). The inertia is not a key: the friction alone takes the free disc from free_rpm to 5 % in ramp_down_s, I = Tslide ramp_down_s / (0.95 w), at least that of a 1/8 in steel plate of the disc's radius; only if the floor applies does a viscous drag b make up the rest.

22.4 Balls. A ball meets the disc through the shared surface law with the surface moving at w x r: the disc is a carried surface of the group, and the ball's reaction turns the disc in the same solve. Shape: a solid cylinder from its bottom up to the top face (a flush disc is the top face alone); past the rim the edge counts as rounded to edge_rho, as any face edge. The playfield gets a hole of the disc's radius plus its gap (default 1/16 in), carved in the playfield's contact.

23. The wire switches on the group step (reviewed with the user 2026-11-07; requirements: Requirements.txt section C; paper design: runs/2026-11-03-switch-requirements/design-wire.md. Not finished: the solve does not converge near touch, to-do N1)

23.1 A gate on its side. A rigid steel wire fixed to the tip of a micro switch's lever, turning with it about the lever's pivot below the playfield: one body on one hinge axis (a HingeGroup part, 21.1), the axis across the lane. Mass and inertia come from the wire's real shape in steel.

23.2 Spring, stops, pivot. The micro switch's lever spring is a preloaded torsion spring, tau = -k (theta - a), a < 0 its free angle beyond the rest stop, so it holds the wire up. k and a are set by the vertical force at the wire's peak at first touch and at full travel, the wire's own weight included. The requirement sets the force: a ball at rest against the wire on a 3 deg slope rolls over it and closes the switch, so the force at first touch is under m g sin(slope) / tan(contact angle). Stops: rest (wire up) and full travel, with the peak below the playfield so the ball meets no step; both the Hertz steel stop gates use. Pin friction and gravity, with the cabinet's acceleration, as gates.

23.3 The switch. It closes when the peak has travelled close_mm and opens when it rises back release_mm past that point: the micro switch's differential travel, not a timer, so one pass closes it once. Hit on close, Unhit on open.

23.4 Shape. The shape is the real switch's wire (from the user's photos and parts drawings, to-do N2), not VPX's mesh, which does not match it; the drawn mesh is built to match, so drawn and touched are the same. It is held as the wire's centreline, joined into capsules of the real wire's diameter: smooth normals for a ball rolling along it, and cheaper than triangles. Scale 1 is the real size; the wire's peak sits peak_mm above the playfield whatever the author's z, and the part below the surface is out of reach. Each side of a V is its own contact (21.4); out of its plane the wire bends as a cantilever from the lever tip (21.5). The Inder switch is a flat steel blade standing on edge: the mesh's triangles are its faces, with plate bending.


== G. Bands ==

8. How it plugs into the engine
-------------------------------
1. The band becomes a part object: cord, sling plate, coil and switch blades, settings from the shared rubber keys. It is the only band model since 2026-11-01 (the old node band was removed).
2. The cycle is unchanged except for one fork: islands form as today; an island holding a band goes to the group step instead of today's fixed 5 us steps;
   every other island goes where it goes now.
3. The group step, per 1 ms: gathers the island's balls, the band and each ball's contacts (playfield, walls, posts, other balls); steps them together to the
   cycle's end under the guards of piece 6; writes the balls back; fires the events after (band hit, switches, the sling's own fire).
4. Forces from outside the description (magnets, the table nudge) enter as drives: held over
   the step, their work counted in the books.
5. A band with no ball rings down and its plate moves by itself with the same solve and no balls.

10. Step 1b: the band cord in the group step
--------------------------------------------
10.1 How the band joins the solve, and the ball's height   (approved by the user 2026-10-29)
   One solve. Each ball keeps its 6 unknowns (move and spin); the band adds no ball unknowns; the sling plate's angle is the one added unknown (none for a band
   without a plate). The band reads each ball's centre projected into its plane (the playfield plane at the cord's height: a fixed linear map), and its forces and
   stiffness are mapped back to 3D the same way.
   Height is in the energy (user: "This is a 3D simulation and bands can throw or push a ball into the playfield depending on the height they are placed"): the cord
   stays at its own height between its posts; round a ball the path's clearance is sqrt((R + r)^2 - dz^2) (R ball radius, r cord radius, dz = ball centre height -
   cord height), so the cord's length and stored energy depend on the ball's height, and the push comes out along the 3D line from the cord's centre line to the
   ball's centre: a cord below the centre lifts the ball, above it pushes the ball into the playfield (the playfield contact takes it in the same solve), level gives
   no vertical push. The backstop squash (posts, stops, plate face) is measured along the same 3D line. Still one function of the positions: the exact step and the
   energy books are unchanged; cost one square root more per ball on the band.


10.3 The step length with a band in the group   (approved by the user 2026-10-29)
   The band's rules join the group's ChooseStep (the shortest wins): a ball on the cord at omegaH 0.6 on the cord's stiffness and at most 2 deg of turn round it a
   step; a squash on a post, stop or the plate face reached in about two steps and stepped at 0.6 while it is struck; the plate's springs at 0.6, at most 2 deg of
   turn a step, a step ending exactly on the coil stop. The steady rule applies to the band's squash too: touching with its compression rate and slip rate within
   vSteady (0.05 m/s) it takes omegaHRest (2.4), as every steady contact does. The cord itself is soft and never sets short steps.

11.28 The cord's twist
   Law: at the contact the ball's surface moves R bb, the cord's centre S bc round the ball (S = R + r), and the cord rolls about its own axis by dpsi: without slip S bc - r dpsi = R bb. Stationary energy gives one contact force f: the pull on the arc S f, the twist's mid torque k psi = r f, the ball's torque R f. Past mu N it slides, f at the limit against the slip; the loss is f x the slip. All arcs on one cord in one quasi-Newton solve. k = G J (1 / L1 + 1 / L2), G = E / 3, J = pi r^4 / 2, L1 and L2 to the next post or ball, fixed at the join so that the energy is exact; the twist stored at a leave (almost nothing) is booked. Energy stays exact: rise at most 0.00002 uJ, |book| at most 0.00005 uJ.
   User (2026-10-30): a twist can travel partway into a post, not past it. So the twist's length is the run plus part of the post's wrap (half ASSUMED), and the stiffness stays near the calculated value: the whole-loop case (scale 0.1) is out. Added on the private copy, with the rubber's own loss on the twist (c = k tan delta / 2 pi f, as on the stretch).

WHAT IT CANNOT HANDLE (reviewed with the user 2026-11-07; [measured], [reasoned], [calculated] or [open] as marked)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. A surface's curvature is held fixed within a step; a ball crossing from one mesh face to the next takes an averaged force direction for that step (energy
   stays exact; edge_radius_mm rounds the edges). [design]
2. Cost grows with the group's ball count (a pile of six is one solve of six), and each touching contact carries its sideways wind-up in its state. [design]
3. A slide under a ring that is letting go takes steps of about 290 us, so the playfield's spring is under-resolved: rise 0.017 mm at 3 m/s against 0.001 mm
   at the 1 us reference. [measured, bench, 2026-10-31]
4. Steel impacts: whether the step rule is accurate enough is open (to-do N6). [open]
5. If a band step cannot be solved, the ball is held still for up to 20 ms, then let go: a visible hitch. Any trip is a fault and fails a test run (to-do N10).
   [reasoned]
6. Stock still moves balls at triggers, kickers, edges and locked balls until those are converted (goal G3); reported, nothing stored. [measured]
7. The cord cannot slip up or down along a ball (the grip holds while the contact line's tilt is under about 42 deg, dz up to about 11 mm) or along a post; past
   that the model keeps it at its height. [calculated]
8. A resting ball on a band that is struck, or on a sling as it fires, keeps the relaxed step until its rate passes vSteady: one step late, energy safe.
   [reasoned]
9. A ball placed inside a ring by a script counts from zero (no launch) and can stay inside the ring. [reasoned]
10. Jet bumper, never run: held and external coil, Collidable switching, two balls on one bumper, two bumpers together; the ring stops' rebound-by-speed table
   is open. [as of 2026-11-02]
11. Gates and spinners, never run: script hold, Collidable, snapshot self-test, spinner switches, two-way and plate gates, two balls. Cost on a hit 100 - 330 us
   per cycle against the 30 us limit. [measured, 2026-10-31]
12. A band on a post: a ball inside the loop meeting a stop with no cord between; a stop has no top (a cylinder at any height); two balls on one post; the
   cord's twist and the post's shear on the same ball; a ball leaving and rejoining the group must not add energy. [reasoned]
13. Ball pairs: sideways state and hit events are not carried between cycles. [not checked since 2026-10-30]
