BAND AND SLING AS A MASSLESS CORD, STEPPED SO THAT ENERGY IS KEPT BY CONSTRUCTION   (design approved by the user piece by piece, 2026-10-29)
==========================================================================================================================================
The first model built on ProjectAssessment-2026-10-27.txt item 0 (energy by construction). It replaces the band chain of runs/2026-10-29-band (src/physics/
contactbandpart.h, BandChain in rubbercontact.h), whose explicit stepping made energy in one place after another (UnifiedContactDesign.txt 12.5; to-do 1.3).
Offline first; engine wiring (UnifiedContactDesign 12.3) waits until the offline proof passes.
Code: src/physics/bandcord.h (pure SI). Offline test: runs/2026-10-29-band-cord.
MEASURED = from a run on disk. CALCULATED = from the settings by hand. INFERRED = from reasoning, not checked. ASSUMED = a value chosen without data.


1. What the band does for the ball (user, 2026-10-29)
------------------------------------------------------
1. Hit and rebound: right exit speed and angle, with the rubber's loss.
2. Rest and roll: a ball lies or rolls along the band.
3. Sling kick: the plate pushes through the band; only the coil adds energy.
4. Posts: the band wraps its posts and comes off them.
5. Switches: the blades close when the band bends far enough.
6. Any number of balls on one band.
7. One span of a loop touching another: an ordinary contact, no special rule (the old "no bounce" rule was a patch for the old model).


2. Bodies and energies (approved 2026-10-29)
--------------------------------------------
The cord: massless, no body of its own. Its shape is the shortest path round its posts, the balls pressing on it, the stop posts behind it and the plate's face.
   Pulleys of the path (centreline clearance radius; + = kept inside the loop, - = outside):
      own posts            + (post radius + cord radius)
      stop posts behind    + (stop radius + cord radius)
      plate face ends      + (cord radius), at the two ends of the face; the cord lies flat on the face between them
      balls                - (ball radius + cord radius)
      a ball seen from the other span of its loop (item 7: the back span pressed by the dented front span)  + (ball radius + 3 cord radii), only while the ball dents the loop
   A pulley joins the path when a straight run of the path crosses it from its own side, and leaves when its wrap angle goes below zero. The path's length L is a
   function of the positions only; it is continuous and has a continuous gradient where a pulley joins or leaves (the wrap angle is zero there).
Stored energy of the cord: V = EA / (2 L0) (L - L0)^2 for L > L0, else 0 (a slack cord). EA = rest tension / prestretch; L0 = the loop's rest length / (1 + prestretch).
   One tension round the whole loop: the posts do not hold the stretch (approved: "the cord's pull stays the same all along its length").
The force on a ball (and the torque on the plate) is the tension pulling round it: -dV/dL x dL/dq. dL/dc of a pulley = (direction of the run into it) - (direction of
   the run out of it).
Moving bodies: the balls (position in the band's plane, speed, spin) and the sling plate (one angle; the plate and the coil's plunger move as one body through the
   link: the sling's link has no slop, sling_slop_deg 0; inertia I(phi) = plate + plunger x lever(phi)^2).
Backstops (the cord squashed between a ball and something hard): ball against the cord on an own post, on a stop post, or on the plate's face. The same law as a
   post rubber (user, 2026-10-29): rubbercontact.h ContactCore's Hertz + thin bonded layer + Hunt-Crossley damping, with the layer taken at the thickness LEFT
   (approved 2026-10-29):  F = kH [delta^1.5 + beta reff^1.5 delta^3 / (h - delta)^3],  kH = 4/3 E* sqrt(reff), reff = the ball on the cord's round cross-section,
   h = the cord's diameter (layer_mm 0), beta 0.33 (unchecked), damping alpha 0.18 s/m with no flat loss (the post-rubber law, user 2026-10-29). Rubber keeps its volume, so a layer pinched between steel only flows sideways, harder the thinner it is:
   the stiffness rises without limit as delta nears h, and the cord can never be squashed through. At small squash it is today's law (h - delta ~ h).
   Stored energy (closed form, for the exact step): kH [0.4 delta^2.5 + beta reff^1.5 W], W = 1.5 h + 3 h ln(h/u) + h^3/(2 u^2) - 3 h^2/u + u, u = h - delta.
   Why: the first offline sweep (2026-10-29) with plain Hertz let a 3 m/s ball squash the 5.4 mm cord 6.2 mm deep on a stop post (MEASURED, stop3); CALCULATED for
   that cord, ball stopped by the squash alone: 3 m/s 7.5 mm -> 4.6 mm (126 -> 960 N peak), 8 m/s 15.5 mm -> 5.0 mm (470 N -> 16 kN), 0.3 m/s unchanged 1.2 mm.
   These begin exactly where the cord path between the ball and that object runs out (the clearances add up), so the squash takes over where the cord can give no
   more. The shared law itself gets the same thickness limit later (to-do 1.20; posts, walls, flipper rubber: engine checks needed).
Energy in: the sling coil (drive), gravity, nudge (engine only).
Energy stored: cord stretch, backstop squash, ball and plate kinetic energy (spin included), the plate's return spring and rest stop.
Energy out: the rubber's stretch loss (viscous tension eta L'/L0, eta = EA tan_delta / (2 pi ref_hz)), backstop damping, friction / twist / rolling resistance at the
   ball's contact, the plate's pivot damping and rest-stop damping, the coil stop (inelastic, as today).
Friction (approved 2026-10-29): at the ball's contact with the cord, normal force = the band's whole push on that ball (cord tension + backstop). The cord's surface
   is treated as still along its length (it only moves sideways with the ball), so the friction's reaction goes into the posts. Coulomb with stick: while the ball
   grips, the friction holds the contact from slipping at mid-step (no work); past mu N it slides, the impulse chosen by the maximum-dissipation rule, so its work is
   never positive. Twist (about the contact normal, limit mu N a_twist) and rolling resistance (limit rr R N) the same way, on the spin only. Rolling resistance acts
   about z only (rolling along the cord): spin about the cord's own direction moves the contact point across the band's plane, which is slip, taken by the friction
   (2026-10-29). Friction and rolling resistance are solved together each round (maximum dissipation over both limits): solved one after the other they undo 5/7 of
   each other every round when both hold (MEASURED: ratio 0.715 per round, up to 60 rounds; CALCULATED for a solid ball: 2.5 / 3.5 = 0.714).
   Lost against the old model: the rubber surface's springy grip (shear wind-up giving spin back).
Switch blades: sensors only (no force). A blade reads where the cord crosses the line through its rest point toward it.


3. The step (approved 2026-10-29)
---------------------------------
The balls and the plate move together over each step h, solved for the end state (a discrete-gradient / energy-conserving step; Gonzalez 1996, Laursen and Chawla 1997):
   positions:  q1 - q0 = h x (v0 + v1) / 2 (the plate through its momentum p = I(phi) omega: phi1 - phi0 = h x (p0 + p1)/2 x (1/I0 + 1/I1)/2)
   momenta:    m (v1 - v0) = h x (force), with every stored energy's force taken as its discrete gradient:
               cord        (V(L1) - V(L0)) / (L1 - L0) x gradL~, where gradL~ = gradL(midpoint) + a correction along the move so that gradL~ . (q1 - q0) = L1 - L0 exactly
               backstops   the same with the overlap delta in place of L
               plate spring, rest stop, inertia's change with the angle: exact differences over the step (one angle)
               gravity     constant: exact
   losses:     act on the mid-step rates (L1 - L0)/h, (delta1 - delta0)/h, (phi1 - phi0)/h and the mid-step slip, always against them: they can only remove energy.
   coil:       its torque at mid-step (current integrated exactly over the step: an exponential with the coil's L/R and back-EMF) times the plate's turn: booked as work.
So, when the step's equations are solved:  energy at the end = energy at the start + coil work - losses, at any step size. The energy book is not part of the step;
it is the test output that proves it (and later the engine's on-screen safety net).
Solver: Newton rounds on the end positions (an approximate stiffness: the cord's stretch and bend stiffness, the backstops, the plate's springs), the friction impulses
   updated between rounds; repeat until the equations are solved to 1e-12 m/s on the balls (1e-9 rad/s on the plate). If the rounds do not settle, the step is retaken
   at half the size (the solver's own rule, not a fix for the physics).
Touch and release: no event handling; the path's length changes smoothly. The coil stop is the one event: a step that would carry the plate past it is shortened to end
   there, then the plate's speed is lost (inelastic) and it stays on the stop until the net torque pulls it off.
Step size: accuracy only, never stability. The engine's choice (h -1 offline): h <= omega_h / omega for every body (omega from its stiffness now), the cord's bend at a
   ball may turn at most turn_max per step, a backstop being approached is reached in no fewer than about two steps, and h <= 1 ms. omega_h 0.6 (user, 2026-10-29, from
   the omega_h sweep 0.3 - 1.2: the backstop squash of the sling hit set most steps; 0.6 gives the sling hit 25.8 us per contact ms against 37.1 at 0.3, exit
   speed within 1.7 % of the 1 us run, every energy check passed at every value) and turn_max 2 deg (first guess).


4. Offline pass/fail (approved 2026-10-29)
------------------------------------------
Cases: the 14 cases of runs/2026-10-29-band (hits at 1 and 3 m/s at 0, 30, 60 deg and at 0.2 / 0.8 of the span; the 31.6 mm band; two balls; a contact found late;
   a stop post behind the band; the sling at rest fired by the script; the sling hit at 3 m/s firing itself) plus three balls on one band, a 4 m/s centre hit that
   pushes one span into the other (the 3 m/s hit may already do so), a ball rolling along the band into a post, a ball resting against the band, and six balls on
   both spans of a ring (cost).
Step sweep: 1 us reference; 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000 us; the engine's choice.
Pass (each run on its own):
   1. no losses, no coil: energy stays at its start value within 1 uJ for the whole run, at every step size;
   2. normal losses, no coil: energy never rises at any step (sum of the per-step rises <= 1 uJ), at every step size;
   3. coil on: energy rises only by the coil's work (same 1 uJ);
   4. no ball passes through the cord;
   5. every step's solver finishes (rounds per step and halvings reported);
   6. cost: time per 1 ms of game time with 1, 2 and 6 balls (reported).
Reported only, decided with the user after the data: exit speed and direction against this model's own 1 us run (old limits 5 % and 2 deg; the sweep gives the
   largest step inside them), and against the old model's 1 us runs (runs/2026-10-29-band/report9.txt) to show what the massless cord changes.
Stopping rule: one run of the whole set; if anything fails, stop and report; no patching (the user decides when the time comes).


5. Cost estimate (INFERRED, to be measured)
-------------------------------------------
Per step: 2 path builds per Newton round (end and midpoint; a path of up to about 10 pulleys: one sqrt and two atan2 each), 2 - 4 rounds, a dense solve of 2 x balls + 1
unknowns. About 2 - 5 us per step with one ball. The steps are set by accuracy: with the cord alone (no backstop) the stiffest motion is the ball on the cord,
roughly omega = sqrt(3000 N/m / 0.08 kg) = 190 rad/s at a deep dent (CALCULATED from EA 286 N, 129 mm span), so omega_h 0.3 gives about 1.5 ms: the 1 ms cap and the
turn rule set it. Expected well under the 30 us per 1 ms target with one ball; a ball squashed on a post (Hertz) needs short steps while it lasts.
State for the snapshot: balls (taken by the engine), the plate (angle, momentum, current, pinned, driver), the path (up to 30 pulleys, 16 bytes each): under 1 KB.


6. What it does not handle or would break (rule 7)
--------------------------------------------------
Checked offline (by the sweep): touch / release, posts on and off, the stop post, the face pressing the cord, the back span (item 7), several balls, late contact.
Open:
- Band mass left out: about 1.1 g of cord moves with an 80 g ball on the stress table's 129 mm band (CALCULATED: 2.7 mm cord, 1100 kg/m3, a third of the span), so up
  to about 1 % of exit speed. The cord cannot vibrate after the ball leaves: it is straight at once (band animation will show that).
- No squash of the free cord: the first touch is soft (a string), so the peak force is lower and the contact longer than the old tube contact. Measured against the old
  1 us runs.
- One tension round the whole loop: a hit on one span stretches the whole loop, softer than a span held at its posts. The friction treats the cord as held (both
  idealizations are stated; real posts grip partly).
- Two balls pressing toward each other through both spans of a loop (ball - cord - cord - ball) have no squash between them: they could pass. INFERRED rare; the
  offline six-ball case keeps them apart. Open.
- A ball inside the loop (only if a table places it there) never touches: reported by the through check.
- A wrap angle may not change by more than half a turn in one step (it is followed from the step's start). A ball would have to move about 30 mm along the cord in one
  step: 8 m/s x 1 ms = 8 mm (INFERRED safe).
- Planar (superseded 2026-10-29 by GroupStepDesign.txt 10.1: the ball's height is in the cord's energy, so the band lifts or presses the ball): the cord stays at its height; a ball above or below the cord's centre meets it with its cross-section at that height (engine adapter). A ball riding over the
  band, or the band lifting, is not modelled.
- Hard hits on a post squeeze the cord to 85 - 90 % of its thickness: very stiff near the end of the squash, so short steps there (cost; energy safe at any step).
  beta 0.33 now sets where the stiffening starts: unchecked. A band over a post that has its own rubber ring: treated as one layer (the cord).
- The plate: link rigid (sling slop 0), the coil stop inelastic (as today), switch blades with no force. The old two-body hand-off of SlingPlate is not used.
- Ball-ball contact is not in the offline test (the engine's ball pair does it in the island).
- Engine wiring (12.3): one island holds the band's balls and plate; the shared loop's engagement / release / rewind and the playfield: not built.
  The band reads its rubber values (alpha_s_per_m, tan_delta, e_mpa, mu, beta_layer) from the same [Rubbers] keys as the post rubbers (user, 2026-10-29):
  one shared value, no band-only key for them; a [Rubber:<name>] section may still override one rubber.
- A step that will not solve is halved: the worst case is a cost question, measured by the sweep.
