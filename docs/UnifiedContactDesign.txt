UNIFIED CONTACT SYSTEM - DESIGN (draft 2026-10-11, for review before code)
==========================================================================

Save point: branch save/pre-unified-contact (commit 4216f7e34, local only). The old stepping stays on disk until the new system has parity.

1. Goal
-------
One contact system owns every ball. Stock VPX does not handle any ball: no hit-time search, no impulse collisions, no position pushes, no static-contact pass.
Each physics cycle every body gets ALL the forces acting on it summed before anything moves, so forces balance at every instant:
ball-surface, ball-ball, ball-flipper, ball-band, ball-disc, magnets, gravity, nudge, coils, springs, motors.

2. Model: bodies, interactions, islands
---------------------------------------
Body        anything with state that moves: Ball (3D, spin), Bat (one rotation about the pivot, coil + spring + stops), BandNode, Disc (one rotation),
            SlingPlate, Plunger, later targets and gates. SI units inside. Holds mass or inertia, state, a force/torque accumulator, and a pointer to the VPX object
            it writes back to. Static geometry is not a body: it is a Surface.
Surface     static geometry that bodies touch (playfield mesh or plane, post and wall rubbers, wall and primitive meshes, ramps). Provides Probe(body) -> nearest
            point, normal, curvature, material. Replaces the HitRubber "host" role (the playfield and the turntable are not rubbers).
Interaction a pair (body, body) or (body, surface) that can exert force: a ContactLaw instance plus its persistent state (shear spring, pair state).
Source      anything that is not a pair: gravity, nudge, magnets, coil torque, spring, motor. Adds to one body's accumulator.
Island      connected component of the interaction graph (union-find each cycle, like the ball clusters). An island is stepped on its own substep.

3. The step (replaces PhysicsSimulateCycle once stock is removed)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
ContactSystem::Step(cycle):
  1. Sync: read state of every VPX object that owns a Body into the Body (position, velocity, spin, flipper angle, ...).
  2. Broadphase: persistent candidate lists (Verlet lists): per body, the surfaces and bodies within reach + margin; rebuilt only when a body has moved more than
     margin/2 since the last build (not every cycle, not every sub-interval).
  3. Islands: union-find over interactions that can touch this cycle (gap < reach + 2 * vrel * cycle).
  4. Per island: h = f(stiffest contact omega, fastest approach, speed cap so no body moves more than a fraction of its radius per substep); n = ceil(cycle / h).
     for each substep:
        clear accumulators
        every Interaction: evaluate the law on the CURRENT states, add +F to one body and -F to the other (Newton's third law by construction)
        every Source: add its force
        integrate every body in the island (semi-implicit Euler)
  5. Write back to the VPX objects; fire events (see 5).
There is exactly one call per 1 ms cycle: no splitting at hit times, no per-owner passes, no ownership flags (m_bandOwner, m_rubberOwner, m_rubberAdvanced go away).

4. Contact law interface (force or constraint)
----------------------------------------------
Every law implements   Evaluate(const View &a, const View &b, double h, State &st, Out &out)
Out is either
   Force      {F on a, point, torque on a, torque on b}        (compliant: Hertz + damping + Mindlin + twist + rolling resistance, today's ContactCore)
   Constraint {rows: normal and friction Jacobians, bias, impulse bounds}   (rigid; NOT implemented in phase 1; the interface only reserves it)
Phase 1 uses Force for everything: the validated model. A rigid solve for the stiff pairs (steel-steel, steel-wood) is added later only if measurements show the
stiff contacts need it (cost or stability), as impulses in the same island step after the compliant forces have been summed into the predicted velocity.
The alternative for the stiff pairs, an implicit (soft-constraint) integration of the same spring, also fits behind the same interface.

5. Events, sensors and mechanisms
----------------------------------
Events (script Hit/UnHit, hit speed, ball-ball callback, rumble, EOS/BOS, switch changes) are DEFERRED: during the substeps a contact-state change appends a record
(type, object, speed, time inside the cycle); after write-back the queue is dispatched in order. Scripts never run inside the step (they may move or delete balls, kick,
change solenoids). Cost: up to 1 ms latency between a hit and the script's reaction (decided 2026-10-11: acceptable).
What an object is:
   Opto / pure sensor   overlap test, event, no force (an optical switch)
   Mechanical switch    a Body with a spring (micro-switch, wire trigger, rollover): the ball deflects it with real force; the switch closes at a deflection threshold;
                        the event comes from the body's state. Not every trigger has force and not every one is a sensor: both kinds exist and are chosen per object.
   Kicker               sensor + mechanism: capture (a hold constraint) and eject (impulse source)
   Spinner, gate        Bodies with one rotational DOF (like the disc)
   Drop target          Body (solenoid reset); stand-up target: Surface + switch (a mechanical switch behind it)
Rendering, the editor UI, VR, ball control and the script properties read only the public VPX state, which is written back at the end of every cycle (audit of all
consumers of physics state is step 0).

6. Naming (code follows what it models)
---------------------------------------
RingAdvanceAll -> ContactSystem::Step      RingBall -> (disappears: every ball is in the system)      HitRubber host surfaces -> ContactSurface
m_bandOwner/m_rubberOwner/m_rubberAdvanced -> removed                  ContactCore -> HertzMindlinLaw (file rubbercontact.h keeps the law)
New files: src/physics/contactsystem.{h,cpp}, contactbody.h, contactsurface.h. The old files stay until their function has moved.

7. Engine selection and migration
---------------------------------
Stock physics stays in the repository and stays selectable; old tables keep working unchanged. The engine is chosen PER TABLE, stored in the .vpx (decided 2026-10-11),
at table load. Unified tables are one-way: "Convert to Unified" in the editor (alternate UI that manages all unified features: materials, flippers, rubbers, bands, slings,
magnets, turntables, ...) writes the unified configuration INTO the .vpx and keeps a backup of the original file. All unified settings live in the .vpx, not in an ini
(the ini was a convenience for building the features without touching .vpx files; it is retired for unified tables). Open: how the new records are tagged in the .vpx
(older VPX versions must ignore them) and the editor panel layout.
The unified engine takes geometry from the table parts through a new interface (each part supplies its contact surfaces / bodies) instead of the stock HitObjects.
Decided simplifications inside the unified engine: no hit-plane playfield (the implicit mesh playfield is the only playfield; a plane is a degenerate mesh); no stock hit
search, impulses, position pushes or static-contact pass. More to drop as we find them (flag each when found).
DEV FALLBACK (decided 2026-10-11): until an object type is converted, the unified engine still runs the stock hit search for it; problems from that mix are ignored
until that type's migration step. The fallback is removed after step 5.
Steps (each compared with the save point in the same harness):
  0. Audit of every consumer of physics state (renderer, VR, ball control, kicker/bumper/trigger parts, script properties, plugins); engine switch at table load.
  1. Bodies + islands + step loop with balls only: playfield mesh, rubber posts and walls, the disc, ball-ball, magnets. Parity with the save point on: tt14_m28_d0,
     cradle3, rest_flat, drop, slow4, pinch. (gsw15f, the kicker-capture test, moves to step 5; the sling regressions move to step 3.)
  2. Flipper as a Bat body (coil, spring, stops, rubber contact, nudge).
  3. Band nodes and sling plates as bodies.
  4. Generic mesh contact for walls, primitives and ramps; part-to-surface interface.
  5. Kickers, targets, mechanical switches, plunger, gates, spinners.
  6. Alternate editor UI and .vpx persistence (can start earlier for the keys that exist); stock stays available for old tables.

8. Performance rules
--------------------
Persistent candidate lists; one probe per interaction per substep; islands at rest run at the stiffness-limited step or sleep (no force change, no motion for N cycles);
no allocation in the step (reused buffers); measure the per-cycle maximum, not only the average (p99 histogram is on the parking lot).

9. Known risks / unknowns
-------------------------
* Stiff steel contacts need 2-6 us steps in compliant form (cost); mitigation as in 4, decided by measurement.
* Mass ratio ball : bat in one island (bat driven by coil): explicit integration of the bat with a stiff rubber contact needs the same small step as the ball.
* Fast balls (plunger shot, 10+ m/s): the speed cap on the substep is the only tunnelling protection; verify against thin posts and walls.
* Every stock object type needs a force-model equivalent before step 6; until then the flag stays.

10. Units (decided 2026-10-11)
------------------------------
Real-world units at every boundary (files, UI, new script properties): metric canonical (mm, m/s, N, g, degrees, us), imperial only as a display setting of the editor UI.
VPX units exist only in the sync/write-back adapter and for legacy script properties (kept as they are; real-unit properties are added beside them). Ball mass in grams.
Names carry physics only (no machine names).

11. Overlap at first sight: the standard for every model   (AGREED with the user 2026-10-26, point by point; point 1 built, the rest not built)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why. A ball found inside a surface the first time the contact system looks is not a contact with a history: its compression is an artefact of how late it was found. A force law applied to that
depth gives a force the real contact never had (Hertz on steel: 169 um is 37 kN). Measured in the user's session of 2026-10-26 (Rubber Test 2, 35 bumper engagements): 33 started with no overlap
(out speed <= 1 m/s, force <= 1.5 kN); the two that started overlapped (70 and 169 um) left at 1.8 and 4.5 m/s with 7 and 68 kN, from approaches of 0.29 and 0.33 m/s. In the 169 um case the engage seat
(ball moved back along its own velocity, surface not moved) made the overlap grow on each of 3 passes (55, 68, 169 um) against the slanted steel ring. Unified solutions never use stock (user rule):
every case below is resolved inside the contact system.

1. PREVENT (agreed, BUILT 2026-10-26). Engagement uses the closing speed relative to the surface: ball closing speed + the surface's bound; engage when gap < closing x 2 x cycle + release gap; the host-box
   prefilter and the face refresh widen by 2 x bound x cycle. Every part answers ContactMovingPart::SurfaceSpeedBound(cycle) (pure virtual): its present speed plus what its own drive and springs can add in a
   cycle. Hinge parts: (|omega| + (gravity torque + cams + springs) / I x cycle) x reach + a driven stop's rate x reach. Bumper: ring = |v| + (coil force at full current, plunger closed + return spring) / m x
   cycle, skirt rim likewise; a sleeping bumper counts the ring's acceleration (Solenoid can start it). Static surfaces answer 0. A held ball keeps its engagement while gap < release gap + bound x cycle
   (the 2 mm 'moving away' clause grows by the same). Arrival-time steps (BallStep) use the same bound. Not covered: the turntable disc's rim speed; a surface faster than its stated bound (the report shows it).

2. REWIND THE ISLAND (agreed, not built). An overlap found at engagement was born inside the last sub-interval. Find the time t* in it when the gap was zero with BOTH the ball and the part where they really
   were (the part evaluated in a scratch copy of its saved state), restore the whole island to t* (the part, every ball touching it, anything coupled), and step it forward with the new ball in it. The contact
   starts at zero depth with the real velocities and the force law does the rest. Nothing is seated; no velocity is edited. A second ball is handled the same way: the island is rewound, not the ball alone.
   * Order inside a sub-interval (agreed): Step (held balls, parts) -> UpdateDisplacements (free balls) -> engagement / overlap check with the rewind -> dispatch of ALL queued events (the step's queue and the
     part events now fired inside PartAdvance). Nothing has run when a rewind happens, so there is nothing to undo, and no latency is added. This is the order of stock VPX.
   * Save / restore (agreed): ContactMovingPart::SaveState / RestoreState, dynamic values only, no allocation. Parts first (bumper, gate, spinner). An island with a bat, band or disc keeps the ball-only
     rewind until that type implements it (reported, never stock).
   * Cost (agreed): save every sub-interval if the measured cost is low (snapshot_us column in VPX_CONTACT_TIMING, judged by the worst cycle); otherwise only when a free ball is within reach of a part (part
     box + ball radius + (ball speed + bound) x 2 cycles). A sleeping part saves its flag only.
   * Stock (agreed): stock collision impulses are applied at the END of the sub-interval, after the check, so none falls inside the rewound span.
   * Chained replays (agreed): rewind to the EARLIEST first contact among the pending overlaps (progress is guaranteed); bisect on the gap, part in a scratch copy, to arrival_depth_um (2 um); the new body joins
     the island and the existing arrival-time stepping handles later contacts in the replay; a further boundary replay only for yet another free body found overlapping (cap = free balls and parts near + 1).
     Not covered: a ball through a thin rim and out again inside one sub-interval (point 1 prevents it).

3. VELOCITY (retired): point 2 edits no velocity.

4. (withdrawn: no stock hand-back, no stock safety net.)

5. REPORT (agreed, rule 8). Always on, each a WARN in the log and a playfield notification once per cause per table start: (a) every replay (log at most once per 500 ms: part, ball, t*, depth, count;
   notification when more than two replays happen in one sub-interval); (b) an island that cannot be fully rewound yet (a bat, band or disc in it): the skip; (c) the force detector (a contact force far above what the
   relative closing speed of ball and surface explains, with the part's state), built; (d) every no-past seat (point 6).

6. A BALL WITH NO PAST (agreed, not built): a ball created inside a part, teleported into one by a script, or inside a part when Collidable turns True (gap already negative at the start of the sub-interval).
   Minimum translation, for this case only: move the ball along the normal of the deepest overlap to touching distance, repeated over every surface it overlaps, up to 8 passes; velocities unchanged; no
   stock; the playfield is just another surface of the loop (the 'seat would put the ball under the playfield' refusal goes away). If the ball is squeezed and does not fit, the remaining overlap is real and the
   force law carries it. Leaves open: ball control that drags a ball into a part is pushed out every cycle like a hard wall; a ball past the far side of a thin sheet is seated through to the wrong side.

What it would break (rule 7): walls and rubbers that use the ball-only rewind seat (results change slightly: re-run the matrix); the order of events within a sub-interval (parts' Hit/Switch after the stock collisions
of the same sub-interval is not the case: they move to after UpdateDisplacements, as stock); tables whose scripts depend on part events firing before the free balls move.
