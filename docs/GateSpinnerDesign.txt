GATES AND SPINNERS - REBUILD DESIGN (draft 2026-10-14, for review before code)
==============================================================================

Status: design only. Nothing here is built. Revision 2 (2026-10-14): the user's review decisions are in section 0. Hardware data: the user, 2026-10-14 (photos of wire W, wire rectangle, deep rectangle variant, plate gate in its
bracket, spinner blade with its offset shaft). Values I estimated are marked UNVERIFIED and become settings.

0. Review decisions (user, 2026-10-14, revision 2)
--------------------------------------------------
- Hit fires when a ball STRIKES the gate (or spinner), not when the switch closes. One hit event per strike for now (two hit speeds for different events: liked,
  later). Sounds are a separate project.
- Switches fire their own events, SwitchClosed / SwitchOpened, as the band switches of rubbers and slings do (slings have them: a sling is a band with switch
  blades). Gates and spinners get the same events; the spinner keeps Spin as well.
- Wire gates: a setting for where on the ball the wire strikes: hit_offset_mm, distance from the ball's centre (+ up), default 0.
- Leaf and micro switches act alike through their spring forces: one spring-switch model with two presets.
- Pivot height default 1.25 in (31.75 mm). A lower pivot (1 in) is allowed for WIRE gates: the wire swings past 90 deg and the ball passes under the axis (the
  wire has no cross bar at the axis; the pins only reach the bracket). A plate gate's axis wire spans the plate, so a plate gate needs pivot >= 2R + wire.
- Draw the physical geometry (not the stock meshes).
- Gates and spinners swing with nudges.

1. Goal and decisions (user, 2026-10-14)
----------------------------------------
- Gates and spinners are rebuilt so that the unified contact system handles them COMPLETELY: no stock mover, no stock collider, no stock fallback, no stock angle
  limits, bounce factors, gravity factor or damping multiplier.
- Each is a physical model of the real part. Gates and spinners are different parts and are modelled separately (they share only the one-axis body code).
- No time-window rules (the 200 ms gate Hit filter goes). Events come from the state of a physical switch or stop.
- The old hinged plates are not tested or repaired. The user observed in play that they ADD ENERGY at some collision angles and that both parts drop the frame rate.
- Rules that apply: DevelopmentRules 2 (physical laws), 5 (cost by the worst cycle), 7 (what breaks), 8 (on-screen detectors).

2. What the 2026-10-12 version is (it is removed)
-------------------------------------------------
Ball-plate contact through the contact law on a two-triangle plate (new); everything else stock: GateMoverObject / SpinnerMoverObject own angle and speed, gravity
factor, damping, angle limits and the bounce at them, and the events; HitGate / HitSpinner stay in the quadtree; the plate size comes from a ball clearance rule
(axis at least 58 U up, plate down to 2 U above the floor); 200 ms Hit filter; Collidable copied once at setup (gate) or not at all (spinner).
INFERRED (from reading, not measured; the user asked not to test the old version): the substep choice does not see the plate's small effective mass (a 2 g plate at a
20 mm lever meets an 80 g ball), so the plate's side of the contact can be under-resolved; that is one candidate for the energy gain. The rebuild handles it by
construction (section 6.3) and the energy detector (section 9) catches whatever else does it.

3. The body: one rotation about a fixed axis (shared by gates and spinners)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
State: angle theta (rad) and angular speed omega (rad/s) about the axis E (horizontal, along the part's length). theta = 0 is the part hanging straight down;
theta > 0 swings the lower edge toward N, the direction a ball moves when it passes from the front. SI inside.
Mass properties from the real shape (section 4/5): mass m, centre of mass at distance d from the axis (in the body frame, angle theta_cm), inertia I about the axis
(sum over wire segments and plate areas: integral of rho * r_perp^2). Nothing is set by hand except the geometry and the material.

Torques, all summed in the accumulator before the body moves (Newton 3 with the balls):
  a. Gravity:       tau_g = -m g d sin(theta - theta_cm) (the pendulum that returns a gate and settles a spinner). g along the playfield slope frame, as for the balls.
  b. Pivot friction: the wire turns in a hole in the bracket (no bushing). Coulomb: |tau_f| <= mu_pivot * |N_pivot| * r_wire, N_pivot = the force the pins carry
                    (weight + the balls' contact forces + switch force). Stick or slide, the same rule the ball contact uses: in a substep the body sticks when
                    the other torques cannot overcome the static limit; it slides with the kinetic value otherwise. No speed cutoff.
  c. Viscous loss:   tau_v = -c * omega. Gates: default 0 (the pin friction and the stops dissipate). Spinners: the user's spin-down setting (section 5.4).
  d. Stops:          each stop is a contact between a part of the body (a bend in the wire, the plate edge) and a static piece of the bracket, with the
                    Hertz + Hunt-Crossley law (steel on steel) at the stop's lever arm. The stop decides the rest angle of a one-way gate and the bounce at full
                    open. No angle clamp, no restitution factor applied to omega.
  e. Switch:         the switch's own force law (section 4.6, 5.5): zero for an opto, a spring for a leaf or micro switch.
  f. Hold actuator:  gates only (section 4.7).
  g. Balls:          the reaction of every ball contact in the substep (contact point x force + couple), already provided by ContactSurface::ApplyReaction.

Integration: semi-implicit Euler in the substeps of the island the body belongs to (balls touching it), as the bat and the disc. A body with no ball in reach steps
on its own (section 8). Write-back once per cycle: the part's public angle (render, CurrentAngle) and its events.

4. Gates
--------
4.1 Common geometry and settings
  pivot_height_mm   axis above the surface the gate stands on (the part's Surface). Default 31.75 (1.25 in, user); typical 1 to 1-3/8 in. Wire gates may sit
                    lower than the ball (section 4.6); plate gates need pivot >= 2R + wire (load-time notification otherwise).
  width             the part's Length (the wire or plate width follows the gate width).
  one_way           the part's TwoWay property (inverted). Live: changing it in the script moves the stops (section 4.5).
  rest_angle_deg    one-way gates: the angle the gate hangs at, held by its reverse stop. Default 15, per gate. Two-way gates hang at 0 (no setting).
  open_stop_deg     the angle where the bracket/stop limits the forward swing. Default from the part's OpenAngle (stock AngleMax), else 90 (UNVERIFIED).
  material          steel: E 200 GPa, nu 0.3, density 7850 kg/m^3. Ball-gate friction and the damping of the ball contact from the part's Friction and
                    Elasticity (as every hard surface, HitRubber::HardSurfaceE).
  mu_pivot          0.3 (steel wire in a steel hole, UNVERIFIED).
  wire_d_mm         1.2 (user: 1.02 - 1.40, any value in range is fine). Also the pivot pin diameter.
  The four types are the part's DrawStyle (GateType): wire W, wire rectangle, plate, long plate.

4.2 Wire gates: a bent rod
  The ball meets the wire through the tube contact (ProbeTube: a circular cross-section of radius wire_d/2 swept along a polyline), the same probe the rubber
  posts use; only the material is steel. The centreline is built in the body frame and transformed by theta every substep (rigid: one rotation of the vertices).
  Hang depth D: the lowest wire strikes a rolling ball at hit_offset_mm from its centre (+ up, default 0, user): D = pivot_height - R_ball - hit_offset.
  Setting depth_mm overrides it.
  Wire W (photo 1): pivot pins at both ends, two outer legs down to depth D, a middle V that rises to w_apex * D (w_apex default 0.45, UNVERIFIED, from the photo),
           bottoms at +-width/4. Length about 130 mm at width 38 mm, mass about 1.2 g at 1.2 mm (8.9 mg/mm).
  Wire rectangle (photo 3, shallow): pivot pins, two legs down to D, a straight bottom bar across the width. About 110 mm, 1.0 g.
  Rectangle variant (photo 2, deep trapezoid): the same with bottom_width_frac < 1 (legs lean in) and a larger depth. Settings depth_mm and bottom_width_frac.
  Stop bend: a short crank in the wire OUTSIDE the pivot (beyond the bracket); it hits the bracket and blocks the opposite direction (one-way). Lever arm
           stop_arm_mm 5 (UNVERIFIED); the contact is steel wire on the bracket edge.
  Switch bend: a second crank outside the pivot that pushes the switch actuator (section 4.6).

4.3 Plate gates: a thin trapezoid hanging from the pivot wire
  Plate:       27 mm wide at the top (1-1/16 in), bottom 2/3 of a ball width (18 mm), 20 mm long, 0.5 - 0.8 mm thick (default 0.65), steel. 1.8 - 2.8 g.
  Long plate:  27 mm top, 13.5 mm bottom, 27 mm long, plus the LIP: 2.75 mm (user 2.5 - 3), bent 15 deg relative to the plate so it stands vertical when the plate
               hangs at the 15 deg one-way rest angle; a flat face the ball bounces off from the back. 2.2 - 3.4 g + lip.
  Contact: the plate is a slab: its mid-surface triangles (the triangle-soup probe: face, edge, corner) with the contact distance reduced by half the thickness, so
  edges are rounded with radius t/2 (a real sheet edge, not a knife). NEW: the triangle probe gets a thickness offset (today it has none).
  Stops: metal stops against the plate in both directions (user); the reverse stop sets the one-way rest angle; the forward stop is open_stop_deg.

4.4 Rest and return
  Two-way: hangs at 0 under gravity, swings to +-open_stop_deg.
  One-way: rests at rest_angle_deg in the opening direction, held there by the reverse stop (gravity pushes it back toward 0, the stop holds it). A ball from the
  front swings it open; a ball from behind presses it onto the reverse stop (blocked) or, for the long plate, bounces off the lip.
  Gravity returns the gate; the swing decays through the stops, the pivot friction and the switch.

4.5 Stops in detail
  Each stop: contact angle theta_s, lever arm l (distance from the axis to the touching point), Hertz stiffness of steel on steel at the touching radius (wire radius
  or plate edge t/2) and Hunt-Crossley damping from stop_e (coefficient of restitution of the stop, default 0.5, UNVERIFIED). The contact force acts on the body as
  torque l * F and nowhere else (the bracket is fixed). LimitEOS fires when the forward stop goes from free to touching; LimitBOS when the reverse stop does (one-way
  gates). A two-way gate has no rest stop and fires no BOS (stock fires one when it parks; a convention difference). The event parameter is the angular speed at
  the touch (deg/s), as stock.

4.6 Gate switch
  A crank outside the pivot pushes a leaf switch. It closes when the gate is open far enough for the ball to pass, slightly before (user).
  Pass angle from geometry: the lowest point of the gate clears a ball on the surface when pivot_height - D_low(theta) >= 2 R_ball. For a wire or plate of depth D
  below the axis: cos(theta_pass) = (pivot_height - 2 R) / D. Example: pivot 31.75 mm, D 18.25 mm -> theta_pass 75 deg. With a pivot below 2R (wire gates
  only) cos(theta_pass) < 0: the wire must swing past 90 deg (pivot 25.4 mm, D 11.9 mm -> 98 deg). A gate whose open stop is below theta_pass can never let
  a ball through: load-time notification (section 9).
  switch_make_deg = theta_pass - switch_lead_deg (default 5), switch_break_deg = switch_make_deg - switch_hyst_deg (default 3): the leaf's own travel between
  make and break (a physical contact gap, not a timer). switch_force_n default 0 (user: little resistance, the gate is almost open when it fires); when > 0 it
  is a spring over the actuation travel.
  Events: SwitchClosed / SwitchOpened (as the band switches) when the switch makes and breaks. A gate that bounces at its open stop fires again only if the
  switch really opens (below switch_break_deg) and closes again: that is what the real switch does. switch = none: no switch events.
  Hit is NOT the switch: see 4.9.

4.7 Hold open (solenoid)
  The coil moves an actuator that holds the gate open (user). No coil model. Gate.Open = True moves a hold stop to hold_angle_deg (the part's OpenAngle, or a
  setting) at hold_speed_dps (default 900 deg/s, UNVERIFIED); the stop pushes the gate through the stop contact law, so a ball resting against the gate is pushed
  with a real force and the gate can be blocked by a ball. Gate.Open = False moves the hold stop back; gravity closes the gate.
  The hold stop is a stop like 4.5 whose angle is driven by the script (a kinematic actuator). It is not a teleport: the gate's angle only changes through torques.

4.9 Hit event (user, revision 2)
  Hit fires when a ball strikes the gate: the ball-gate contact goes from not touching to touching, with the contact's own release-gap hysteresis (the distance
  a ball must move away before a new touch counts, ContactSurface::ReleaseGapM): a plate that really bounces off the ball and comes back is a second strike,
  as on the real part. No time window. The hit speed is the normal approach speed at the touch (the HitSpeed record, [Features] hit_speed). One hit event per
  strike for now; separate speeds per event type later.
  REVISION 3 (user 2026-10-15): a Hit fires only while the gate is near its start angle (rest angle for one-way, 0 for two-way): hit_window_deg, default 5.
  A ball touching a gate that is already swinging (the plate coming back onto the ball, the gate flapping after a hit from behind) fires no Hit.

4.8 Mass check (estimates, UNVERIFIED)  [wire: 6.4 mg/mm at 1.02, 8.9 at 1.2, 12.1 at 1.40]
  wire W 1.2 g, wire rectangle 1.0 g, plate 1.8 - 2.8 g, long plate 2.2 - 3.4 g + lip. Pendulum frequency of a 20 mm plate gate about 4 Hz.

5. Spinners
-----------
5.1 Blade
  A flat steel rectangle: height 31.75 mm (1.25 in, user: typical), width = the part's Length (varies), thickness 0.8 mm (user). Steel: about 7.8 g at 39 mm wide,
  plus the shaft wire. The shaft runs across the blade with Z bends: the axis lies IN THE PLANE of the blade, offset_mm = 6 above the blade's centre (user).
  So the blade reaches 9.9 mm above the axis and 21.9 mm below it.
  Inertia about the axis: I = m (H^2/12 + offset^2) + wire (thickness term negligible). 7.8 g -> about 9.4e-7 kg m^2.
  Pendulum: tau_g = -m g offset sin(theta); small-swing frequency about 3.5 Hz. This is what makes the blade come back to vertical after every spin (user).
  Pivot height: the part's Height; typical like gates (1 to 1-3/8 in); the blade meets a rolling ball from its centre height to its upper half (user).
  The ball touches both faces, both long edges and the bottom edge: slab contact as the plate gate (4.3).
5.2 Bracket: NOT collidable (user): the ball cannot reach it on the playfield. A table that wants it uses a primitive.
5.3 Angle range: full turns. The part's AngleMin/AngleMax: when they differ from the full circle, they become two stops (4.5): a spinner used as a flap.
5.4 Damping (user: a setting, spin-down varies by machine)
  Pivot Coulomb friction as 3b (mu_pivot, wire in hole) plus viscous c. Setting spin_decay: the stock Damping property keeps its meaning, the fraction of angular
  speed kept per 10 ms of free spin, and is converted exactly to c = -I ln(Damping) / 0.01 s. Default: the part's Damping value.
5.5 Switch (user: leaf, micro or opto; the force default is set by feel in testing)
  switch = opto:  a flag on the shaft interrupts a beam: the switch is closed while the angle is inside [switch_angle_deg +- switch_halfwidth_deg]. No force.
  switch = leaf or micro: ONE spring-switch model (user: they act alike through their spring forces), two presets:
  leaf:           a crank on the shaft at radius cam_r_mm (default 3, UNVERIFIED) pushes a thin copper leaf, 19 mm long (3/4 in, user). Deflection
                  delta(theta) = max(0, cam_r cos(theta - switch_angle) - gap). Spring energy 1/2 k delta^2; torque = -k delta * d(delta)/d(theta) (conservative: it
                  takes energy while the leaf closes and gives it back while it opens, the acceleration/deceleration cycles the user sees), plus a small
                  Hunt-Crossley loss on the crank/leaf contact (leaf_e). The switch closes when delta > close_travel. Setting leaf_force_n = force at full
                  deflection; DEFAULT TO BE SET BY FEEL IN TESTING (user; my first estimate of about 10 gf is probably too high). Near rest the leaf torque can
                  balance gravity and leave the blade a little off vertical, as the user sees on real machines.
  micro:          the same crank contact on the switch lever, spring with a preload F0 (micro_force_n) and its own travel to the click.
  Events: Spin and SwitchClosed when the switch closes, SwitchOpened when it opens (one closure per revolution with one crank). Stock fires Spin from the angle;
  this fires it from the switch. switch = none: Spin from the angle crossing switch_angle_deg (a spinner with no switch modelled), no switch events.
  Hit: when a ball strikes the blade, as the gate (4.9).

6. Ball contact (both parts)
----------------------------
6.1 Geometry follows the body: every substep the wire centreline / plate triangles are the body-frame geometry rotated by the current theta; the surface velocity
    at the contact is omega x r (ContactSurface::VelocityAt); the reaction goes to the body's accumulator (ApplyReaction). Position and velocity of the surface are
    taken at the SAME instant as the ball's (no lag between the geometry and its velocity).
6.2 Law: the existing Hertz + Hunt-Crossley + Mindlin friction law, steel on steel (wire: tube radius wire_d/2; plate: slab, edge radius t/2).
6.3 Step: the substep must resolve the contact between the ball and the LIGHT body. The effective mass of the body at the contact point is
    m_eff = I / r_perp^2 (r_perp = lever arm of the contact normal); the contact frequency is omega_c = sqrt(k / mu), mu = m_ball m_eff / (m_ball + m_eff) (the
    reduced mass). The substep rule takes mu instead of the ball mass when the surface is a body. This is a general rule (any moving body: bat, plate, disc), not a
    gate rule; the bat's and the disc's inertia are large, so it changes nothing for them (to be checked on the matrix).
6.4 Hit events of the ball contact: Hit on every strike (4.9) for both parts. Sounds and a second hit speed (stop clank) are a separate project (user).

7. Stock removed
----------------
In the unified engine a gate or spinner adds NO stock HitObjects (no HitGate, HitSpinner, LineSeg), and the movers do not run (no UpdateDisplacements, no
m_hingeAdvanced flag). The part still owns its render state: the body writes the angle to the part's public angle at the end of every cycle, and CurrentAngle
reads it. Collidable is read live every cycle (a script toggle acts at once). The stock objects stay for stock tables (engine chosen per table).
Files: contacthinge.{h,cpp} is replaced by contactgate.{h,cpp} and contactspinner.{h,cpp} on a small shared body (contacthingebody.h: state, torques, stops,
stick/slip, integration). The gate/spinner branches in HitRubber (SetHinge, MakeHingeHost, the hinge Probe path) move to the new surfaces.

8. Stepping and cost
--------------------
- Sleeping: a body at rest (omega = 0, held by a stop or by static pivot friction, no ball within reach, no hold change, switch not moving) costs nothing. It wakes
  when a ball's candidate list reaches its swept volume, when the script changes Open/TwoWay/Collidable, or when the cabinet acceleration changes (nudge). Sleeping is not a
  cutoff: the body is only asleep when the stick condition of 3b holds, i.e. it would not move anyway.
- Swept volume: the real one (the circle of the farthest point about the axis, clipped by the stops), not a 50 U box.
- Free swing (no ball): the body steps alone; its own step is set by the stiffest active force (stop contact when near a stop, otherwise the pendulum and the leaf
  spring, a few substeps per ms).
- A spinning spinner (up to about 30 rev/s, 190 rad/s): a few degrees per substep; the crank/leaf contact sets the step only while it is engaged.
- Budget (rule 5, worst cycle): measure with VPX_CONTACT_TIMING. Targets to agree: a ball through a gate < 30 us per cycle, a spinning spinner with a leaf < 20 us,
  a sleeping part 0.

9. Detectors (rule 8)
---------------------
- Energy audit: per ball-body contact episode (first touch to release) the energy of ball + body (kinetic, rotational, gravity potential, spring energy of the
  switch) at the start and at the end. Contact losses mean it must not grow; growth beyond 1 % + 1 uJ is unwanted: WARN in the log and a playfield notification
  naming the part (once per part per table start). This is how an energy gain like the user's is caught in play.
- Unknown keys and out-of-range geometry (a gate that cannot pass a ball, 4.6): notification at table start.

11. Script API and convention differences (to document)
-------------------------------------------------------
Gate:    Open/Close -> hold actuator (4.7); OpenAngle -> open stop and hold angle; CloseAngle -> one-way rest angle; TwoWay -> stops (live); Collidable live;
         CurrentAngle = body angle; Damping and GravityFactor have no physical meaning in the model: IGNORED on unified tables (warning once); Elasticity and
         Friction -> ball contact material; Hit = a ball strikes the gate (as stock, without its filtering); NEW events SwitchClosed / SwitchOpened (IGateEvents);
         LimitEOS/LimitBOS = stop contact begins (stock: angle limit reached); Height: the pivot comes from pivot_height_mm (convention difference, documented).
Spinner: AngleMin/AngleMax -> optional stops; Damping -> viscous decay (exact conversion 5.4); Elasticity -> ball contact; CurrentAngle = body angle;
         Spin = switch closes (stock: angle crossing); NEW Hit (ball strikes the blade) and SwitchClosed / SwitchOpened (ISpinnerEvents); LimitEOS/BOS only with stops.
Rendering (user: draw the physical geometry): the engine draws the body's own mesh, built from the same geometry as the contact: the wire as a tube mesh
         (wire_d), the plates and the blade as slabs. Material and Image from the part (the spinner's decal Image mapped onto the blade faces). The bracket
         (ShowBracket) is drawn from a simple physical bracket mesh at the axis (two side plates with the pivot holes); it is not collidable. The stock meshes
         are not drawn on unified tables.

12. What breaks and what the design does about it (rule 7)
----------------------------------------------------------
 1. Tables that tuned stock Damping/GravityFactor on gates: values ignored -> feel changes. Handled: warning + documented; physical settings instead.  REASONED.
 2. Scripts that use Gate_Hit for a ball TOUCH (not a pass): now fires only when the gate opens enough. Documented; switch_make_deg can be lowered.     REASONED.
 3. Scripts that set CurrentAngle or move the gate angle by hand: not supported (the angle is physical). Documented; Open/Close covers the solenoid case.  OPEN.
 4. Two-way gate toggled to one-way while open: the reverse stop appears inside the swing -> the stop contact law pushes it out with a large force. Handled: a stop
    only engages from its free side (it is placed when the body is on its free side, otherwise when the body next passes it).                                 REASONED.
 5. Hold actuator closing while a ball is under the gate: the gate rests on the ball, the ball is pressed by the gate's weight only (gravity close, no drive).  REASONED.
 6. Several balls on one gate/spinner at once: one island, all reactions summed. Tested in the matrix.                                                      OPEN.
 7. Ball trapped between a one-way gate and a wall (gate on its stop, ball pushing from behind at rest): contact + stop hold it; no energy source.            OPEN (test).
 8. Very fast ball (> 20 m/s) into a light gate: the reduced-mass step (6.3) keeps it resolved; cost peak measured.                                         OPEN (test).
 9. Pivot height below 2 R + wire: a plate gate can never let a ball pass; a wire gate can if its open stop is past theta_pass (4.6). Notification at load
    when the gate cannot pass a ball (9); geometry still built.                             REASONED.
10. Gate or spinner on a ramp / upper playfield (Surface property): pivot height from that surface; gravity in the playfield slope frame.                   OPEN (test).
11. Restart and teardown: the bodies and surfaces are owned by the physics engine (m_ownedHosts) and deleted with it; the leak detector covers them.          REASONED.
12. A table without the feature (stock engine): stock gate/spinner unchanged.                                                                             REASONED.
13. Nudge / tilt (user: yes): the body's gravity torque uses gravity minus the cabinet acceleration the balls feel, so gates and spinners swing on a nudge.
    A sleeping body wakes when the cabinet acceleration changes.                                                                                           REASONED.
14. Collidable false: the body still swings under gravity and the hold actuator, balls pass through. Spinner with Collidable false: same.                     REASONED.
15. Editor (UI) mode: no bodies; the editor draws the stock mesh at the stored angle.                                                                       REASONED.
17. Scripts that relied on stock Gate_Hit filtering (one Hit per pass): a real double strike now fires two Hits.                                          REASONED.
18. Spinner decal: the Image must map onto the drawn blade the same way as on the stock mesh (orientation, both faces).                                    OPEN (visual check).
16. Spinner stopped exactly upside down (unstable equilibrium): pivot friction holds it there if the static limit allows, which a real spinner can do.        REASONED.
