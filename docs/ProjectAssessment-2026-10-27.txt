2. Performance against the target (13 balls, 144 Hz)
-----------------------------------------------------
2a. The budget rule is wrong for 144 Hz.
   Physics, the table script and the frame preparation run on ONE thread (core/player.cpp, FramePacingGameLoop: "without multithreading"). At 144 Hz a frame is
   6.9 ms, which holds about 7 physics cycles plus the frame preparation.
   - Today's rule (DevelopmentRules 5): 1 ms per cycle, judged by the worst cycle.
   - A physics cost of 1 ms per 1 ms cycle uses the whole thread: every frame would drop. One 2 ms cycle among cheap ones drops nothing.
   - Proposed rule: physics over any 7 consecutive cycles <= 3 ms (about 0.4 ms per cycle on average), single cycle <= 1.5 ms.
     INFERRED: assumes the frame preparation and script take about 3-4 ms per frame on a busy table. Measure that once on the stress table and set the numbers from it.

2b. What the cost is made of (MEASURED today, bumper test launch2, one ball, focused):
   - Before today's work 85 % of the step time was the bumper's own stepping (stiff stop springs). After: >1 ms cycles 2.1 % -> about 0.1 %, p99 1.18 -> 0.52 ms.
   - The rest of the slow cycles: about 740 substeps in one cycle, set by the steel contact step (about 1.4 us).
   - Gates/spinners (gs8): face tests were 58 % of the substep time; today's short face list: 1.1 -> 0.5 us per ball substep.

2c. How the cost grows with balls (INFERRED from the code).
   - Balls that touch form one island, and an island runs at the finest step any of its members needs. A pile of 13 balls touching steel runs all 13 at the steel step.
   - The contact system runs once per stock sub-interval, not once per cycle (PhysicsEngine.cpp:1042, inside the stock hit loop). Every stock hit by any ball
     splits the cycle; each split repeats gather, islands, setup and the snapshot of every ball. More balls = more splits = more repeats.
   - Resting balls never sleep (only parts do). Balls parked in a lock or trough are stepped every cycle. (User decision 2026-10-20: not now, it does not help the worst
     case. It does help a 13-ball game where several balls are usually parked.)
   - Ball-pair search is all pairs: 78 pairs at 13 balls, small.
   - Magnet pair work grows with the number of pairs: 15 pairs at 6 balls, 78 at 13. Cost at 13 not measured.
   - Small allocations every cycle (a probe list per ball, the free-ball list, the event closures): about 1 us per cycle at 13 balls (INFERRED). Not an issue; tidy
     up when that code is next touched.


3. Opportunities, in order
--------------------------
0. ENERGY BY CONSTRUCTION (the design target the items below serve). Launches from added energy must be impossible by design, not caught afterwards.
   MEASURED: the stepping itself makes the energy (bumper replay: 100 us step launches the ball at 10.6 m/s vs about 0.5 m/s at 1 us; bumper alone: energy error
   0.8 % at 1 us, 19 % at 16 us, 83 % at 32 us). The guards we keep adding (arrival steps, rewinds, depth checks, overlap replays) all fight this one failure.
   a) Each step's contact force comes from the energy at the end of the step minus the energy at the start, divided by the compression in between (energy-conserving
      time stepping, e.g. Laursen and Chawla 1997). Same Hertz + damping law; only the stepping changes. A late-found contact, a long step or a face switch cannot
      give back more than was put in. Damping uses the mid-step speed, so it only removes energy.
   b) Models describe, the engine steps: a part supplies bodies, masses, springs, contact laws, stops and drive forces; the engine moves balls and part together in
      the same step by one rule. No part has its own stepping or step-size rule.
   c) Energy enters only through drives (coils, motors, gravity, nudge), counted as work, so every cycle the books close. The energy check (item 5) becomes a
      pass/fail test; the on-screen net stays.
   d) The step is set by accuracy only, so much of the arrival / rewind machinery can go. A sweep against tunnelling through thin walls stays.
   Open (INFERRED): cost of the small per-step solve (2-4 iterations per contact) vs longer steps; convergence in a pile of touching balls; friction; curvature
   held fixed within a step; a ball with no history (the seat stays).
   Magnets (user-approved 2026-10-27): energy-based while the current is steady (air-core: the force is the gradient of the ball's field energy, magnet.cpp:1226,
   read from the code, not tested); current changes count as drive work; the pull rises steeply near the pole, so it sets the step like a stiff contact; balls
   magnetize each other, so balls near a magnet step as one group. Iron-core and plug-at-playfield cases: not checked yet. Large change: bumper, gate, spinner lose their own
   stepping; flippers and bands move in.
   Proof before engine code, when the user schedules it: offline on the bumper launch replay (no launch at 100 and 200 us, match 1 us at 10-20 us), the 3 m/s skirt
   hit (unstable at 40 us today), a 6-ball pile (convergence and cost).
   Items 1-4 are steps toward this: build them so they fit it (one step function, one loop, parts as data), not as separate fixes.
1. ONE STEP RULE. The "how long may this substep be" rule exists in four copies with different details:
      contactsystem.cpp NextStep (line 1133), the ball-pair rule in SetupIsland (line 583), the bumper's BallStep (contactbumperpart.h:150), the gate/spinner BallStep
      (contacthingepart.h:317), plus the rest / roll / bat rules in GatherBalls (lines 389-420) with ini constants.
   Differences found: the parts use a fixed 9.81 m/s2 where the core uses gravity plus nudge; the rest test differs; hitrubber.h:57 says the rest factor defaults to 0.2
   while hitrubber.cpp sets 0.8. Each new model copied the closest one, and later fixes went into one copy only. Make it one function that every model calls
   (reduced mass, stiffness law, closing speed, acceleration bound, depth, resting or not). This is the root cause of the repeated mistakes on the physics side.
2. FLIPPERS AND BANDS ONTO THE SHARED LOOP. They are the busiest objects in a game and have none of the shared guards (no arrival steps, no rewind, no depth check). The
   2026-10-13 launch was a ball just let go by a bat (contactsystem.cpp lines 380-382). They need SaveState / RestoreState and the moving-part interface. The turntable disc likewise.
3. CONTACT SYSTEM ONCE PER CYCLE. Take the converted objects out of the stock hit-time split, so the contact system runs once per 1 ms cycle. What still splits it:
   stock objects (kickers, targets, triggers, plunger) and stock hits of free balls. How often: not measured (ContactProf::Split counts it).
4. STEEL IMPACTS. The steel step is what sets the cost. Two physical routes, both from the same Hertz + damping law:
   a) Step size at the accuracy the law needs. MEASURED offline today (two cases, bumper pinch 0.3 m/s and 3 m/s head-on on the skirt): explicit stepping matches the 1 us
      reference up to about 20 us; the engine uses about 2 us. Relaxing needs proof on the matrix (rebound, peak force, energy).
   b) Impact law (restitution from the same law, then a resting constraint), as already done for the bumper ring stops today. MEASURED: PartStep 1.95 -> 1.10 s, >1 ms
      cycles 2.1 -> 0.3 %. Apply to every internal stop first (gates, spinners, flipper end stops, plunger). For the ball's own impacts it is a design study.
      Breaks / open: a ball pinched between two moving parts, two impacts at once, friction during the impact, event timing.
5. ENERGY CHECK IN THE SHARED LOOP (design 11 point 7e, not built). A safety net and a test, not the fix (the fix is item 0): per cycle, energy end = start + drive
   work - losses; a violation is a WARN + notification naming the object, and a fail in the matrix.
6. PARALLEL ISLANDS. Islands are independent and scripts never run inside the step, so separate islands could run on separate cores. This is the biggest lever for 13
   balls, which usually split into several islands. Open: a fixed event order, parts shared between islands (they already merge islands). User decision 2026-10-20:
   not now. Revisit after 1-5 and the stress table.
7. SMALLER ITEMS. Material constants (E*, G*, damping) computed once instead of on every contact call (rubbercontact.h ContactCore, line 244); no allocation in the step;
   keep the nearest face between substeps; sleep for resting balls (see 2c).
8. STILL STOCK: kickers, targets, triggers, plunger. The user is redesigning these: ask before converting.


4. Why the same mistakes repeat (process)
-----------------------------------------
- Rules are copied, not shared: the step rule (4 copies), the rest-speed constant 0.02 (5 copies), the steel stiffness formula (8 copies in two files).
- Two stepping loops exist side by side; a new model picks one.
- Only two model types implement the shared moving-part interface (gates/spinners, bumpers). Flippers, bands, slings, the disc each have private code.
- No shared test bench: each model has its own offline test and new batch scripts (about 50 batch_*.ps1 files; runs/2026-10-09-slow-contact is 81 GB, 1,318 run
  folders). No stress table, no fixed pass/fail list per model.
- The to-do "single source" is a 429-line, 217 KB journal with lines up to 1,500 characters. A session cannot read it whole, reads part of it, misses decisions, and
  relearns them. The testing playbook memory still names the old branch.
- 31 environment-variable reads in the engine change behaviour without showing up in the ini (the playbook lists the traps they caused).
- NewPhysicsReference.txt has offline flipper-link notes with the spaces stripped (change log, about lines 440-460): noise in the document every session reads.
- The rule "use the shared guards" (CLAUDE.md) does not say which functions those guards are, so a session cannot check it.
The standards in docs/ModelStandards.txt answer these.
